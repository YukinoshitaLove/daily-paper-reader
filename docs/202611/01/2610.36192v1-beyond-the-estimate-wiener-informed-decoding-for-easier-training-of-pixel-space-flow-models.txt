## ABSTRACT 

Learning velocity fields directly in pixel space is difficult and slow. We show that coarse second-order image statistics can ease this task: frozen Wiener estimates improve velocity learning, with gains extending beyond the linear predictions they explicitly provide. Moreover, we observe similar but complementary gains from frozen bases and pixel decoding. These observations motivate Wiener-informed 

Decoding (WiD), which uses statistical knowledge both for direct prediction and to organize decoder computation without modifying native blocks. WiD combines a trainable Wiener prediction path with coefficient interfaces that inject features into the native decoder and return its predicted coefficient corrections, with these components initialized with data statistics. All introduced parameters, includ-ing the spectrum and coordinate transforms, are trained jointly under the original pixel-space velocity objective. Across model scales and decoder architectures, WiD accelerates training and improves generation quality with modest parameter overhead. It also yields smoother gradient trajectories and remains effective with representation alignment. WiD improves class-conditional and text-to-image gen-eration across different resolutions. On ImageNet 256 × 256 , PixelDiT-XL with WiD reaches FID 1.53 after 500 epochs, achieving comparable quality to Pix-elDiT with 37.5% fewer training epochs. Statistical denoisers contribute beyond the estimate: their computational structure can also ease pixel-space training. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Pixel-space flow models offer a simpler generative pipeline, but not an easier learning problem. By operating directly in pixel space, they remove the need for a separate, pretrained autoencoder. How-ever, the denoising network must operate on noisy high-dimensional pixels while learning natural image structure, which makes optimization slow and challenging (Li & He, 2026; Ma et al., 2026). Recent work addresses the challenge in two prominent directions. (i) Prediction-space approaches seek more learnable target parameterizations, such as clean-image prediction (Li & He, 2026) and rank-asymmetric velocity prediction (Chen et al., 2026a). (ii) Architectural approaches dis-tribute contextual modeling and dense pixel prediction across specialized components. For example, DeCo (Ma et al., 2026) and PixelDiT (Yu et al., 2026) pair a contextual backbone with a fine-grained pixel decoder. These advances make pixel generation more tractable by changing the parameteriza-tion or computational organization—and motivate a closer look at the pixel denoising task itself. However, high dimensionality does not imply an absence of exploitable structure. Natural images often possess correlations across space and color, whereas the added Gaussian noise is isotropic. The second-order statistics yield a closed-form linear denoiser— the Wiener estimator (Wiener, 1949; Kailath et al., 2000). Although this estimator cannot capture the full complexity of natural images, it helps previous studies (Wang & Vastola, 2024; Lukoianov et al., 2025) investigate the learned denoising behavior and locality. Motivated by these observations, we ask a training question: is it possible to ease pixel-space velocity learning by explicitly providing this statistical estimate?

We consider two typical construc-tions: global DCT (Ahmed et al., 1974) for image-wide spectral structure and patch PCA (Zhang et al., 2010) for local spatial feature. Both analytic bases rescue the otherwise struggling plain DiT (Figure 1a), bringing generation quality into the range of contemporary approaches (Figure 1b). Consequently, even coarse statistical knowledge can ease the burden of velocity learning. A frozen statistical prediction is not the whole story. For DiT, our diagnostics further show that much of the reduction in velocity prediction error lies beyond the fitted linear component (Figure 2a). Thus, the effect of Wiener bases is not confined to correcting a linear prediction. This leads us to consider the computation behind the estimate: the Wiener estimator transforms a noisy observation into statistical coordinates, adjusts its coefficients according to the noise level, and maps them back to pixels. Can we go a step further and use these operations to organize the denoiser’s computation, rather than only adding the resulting estimate to its output? 

Pixel decoding provides a natural and task-aligned interface for this integration. In PixelDiT and DeCo, the backbone provides semantic context, while the decoder maps noisy pixels to velocity— 

the same input–output task addressed by the Wiener estimator. We observe similar but complemen-tary gains from frozen Wiener bases and pixel decoding (Figure 2b). Our input-perturbation tests support the task alignment: the direct pixel pathway shows closer Wiener alignment than the seman-tic pathway (Figure 2c). Therefore, we incorporate the estimator’s analysis and synthesis operations into the pixel decoder as trainable components, while retaining the semantic backbone architecture. 

Approach: from frozen bases to adaptive decoding. We introduce Wiener-informed Decoding (WiD) that incorporates statistical knowledge in two ways. (i) A statistical starting point. Using patch PCA statistics, we initialize a direct prediction path to produce the Wiener velocity estimate. It turns noisy patches into statistical coordinates, scales their coefficients according to the noise level, and maps them back to pixels. (ii) Coordinates for decoder computation. We also allow noisy-patch coordinates to integrate with the decoder’s native input embeddings as additional features. Alongside its original pixel-space velocity output, the decoder also predicts a correction to the coefficient-space estimate. The sum of the Wiener-branch and pixel-head predictions is supervised by the original training objective, and introduced parameters are trained jointly with the network. WiD improves training stability and efficiency, and accommodates different pixel-decoder architec-tures with a modest parameter increase. Empirically, it reaches comparable generation quality in fewer training steps, with gains that persist further into training than frozen bases (Figure 1c). We validate WiD on class-conditional and text-to-image generation tasks across different resolutions.

Two structured bases. For 256 2 RGB images, the full moment matrix has (3 × 256 2)2 entries. We approximate it in two practical ways. (i) Global DCT retains a 3×3 RGB moment at each frequency of the orthonormal 2-D DCT-II, omitting cross-frequency coupling. The block eigendecompositions, combined with the DCT, specify U and Λ. (i) Frozen bases and native pixel decoding both improve the remaining prediction, yet their combi-nation yields further gains, showing similar but also complementary benefits. ## 6 RELATED WORK 

6.1 STATISTICAL DENOISING 

Statistical priors for image restoration. Wiener estimation has a long history in image denois-ing and restoration (Wiener, 1949). Methods such as BLS-GSM (Portilla et al., 2003) and NL-Bayes (Lebrun et al., 2013) build on Wiener estimation using statistics for image denoising. Similar to statistical operations in our WiD, BM3D (Dabov et al., 2007) organizes computation by con-structing representations, applying empirical Wiener filtering to coefficients, and reconstructing the 9Preprint – NUS HPC-AI Lab image through inverse transforms. Furthermore, Deep Wiener Deconvolution (Dong et al., 2020) has proposed to integrate Wiener operations into a learnable feature-processing pipeline for image deblurring. Together, these studies show how statistical priors can be constructed and utilized. 

Statistical structure in diffusion. In studies of diffusion models (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021), statistical estimates also provide a way to understand learned de-noisers. Linear fits reveal Gaussian structure associated with generalization (Li et al., 2024), while Gaussian score approximations capture moderate and high noise-level behavior and help acceler-ate sampling (Wang & Vastola, 2024). Data statistics can also explain locality patterns otherwise attributed to architecture (Lukoianov et al., 2025). These findings connect classical estimation to modern generative models, and inspire us to explore using statistical priors in generative learning. 

From estimation to generative learning. Motivated by these insights, our research takes a step further to study how this statistical knowledge can aid pixel-space velocity learning: WiD uses the statistics to initialize the prediction path as a structured starting point, and integrates the computa-tional operations and coordinates into the decoder. 6.2 PIXEL -SPACE FLOW MODELS 

Prediction parameterization. Recent approaches ease pixel-space learning by changing what the network predicts. JiT (Li & He, 2026) predicts clean images, while AsymFlow (Chen et al., 2026a) combines full-rank data prediction with low-rank noise prediction to reconstruct the velocity. Re-cently, k-Diff (Jin & Wang, 2026) learns a scalar that controls the data–noise mixture predicted by the network, while retaining velocity-space supervision. A simplified linear analysis motivates this parameterization by relating the preferred mixture to data dimensionality. Separately, WiD aims to address the similar learning difficulty within velocity prediction. 

Pixel decoding. Another direction is to separate contextual modeling from dense pixel prediction. PixNerd (Wang et al., 2026a) uses patch-wise neural fields, PixelDiT (Yu et al., 2026) uses pixel transformers, and DeCo (Ma et al., 2026) uses context-conditioned MLP decoders to alleviate the learning difficulty. WiD builds on this separation: it retains the native backbone and decoder blocks in PixelDiT and DeCo, adding statistical interfaces around their existing computation. 

Connection to preconditioning. Preconditioning addresses differences in scale and geometry that can hinder diffusion and flow training. EDM (Karras et al., 2022) normalizes network inputs and residual targets using noise-dependent scaling and a skip connection. Preconditioned Flow Match-ing (Ahamed et al., 2026) instead uses reversible data transformations to improve the conditioning of intermediate flow distributions. WiD is related in the use of statistical structure to ease learning, but focuses on decoder design: a trainable Wiener prediction path interacts with the native decoder. 

## 7 CONCLUSION 

We present Wiener-informed Decoding (WiD) to improve the velocity learning of pixel-space flow models. Specifically, we have shown that coarse second-order statistics can ease pixel-space train-ing, and the gains from frozen Wiener bases extend beyond their explicit linear predictions. Moti-vated by these insights, we developed WiD, which combines a trainable statistical prediction path with coefficient interfaces that connect it to the native pixel decoder. We demonstrate that WiD en-hances training efficiency and stability while consistently improving generation quality across model scales and decoder architectures, benefiting both class-conditional and text-to-image generation with modest parameter overhead. These findings suggest that statistical denoisers can contribute beyond the estimate: the computational organization is also helpful for learning pixel-space generation. ACKNOWLEDGMENTS

Fitted responses and remaining predictions. At each fixed t, we fit an affine map to each model’s (including the reference) complete velocity output. The PCA family is a shared 768 × 768 patch-affine map with bias; the DCT family consists of independent 3 × 3 RGB-affine maps with biases at each of 256 × 256 frequencies. Each affine family contains its corresponding frozen base, so subtracting the fitted response removes that kind of base’s explicit additive contribution. Note that the remaining prediction can still contain linear dependencies outside the chosen family, such as cross-patch or cross-frequency interactions.

## C LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK 

In this work, we mainly focus on easing velocity learning for pixel-space image generation tasks. Actually, we think the perspective of statistical denoising may also be helpful for other flow gen-erative models. Therefore, explorations of integrating statistical denoisers into other generative tasks are also attractive directions, such as latent space generation, and language, audio, as well as video generation. In the future, we are planning to improve upon our current approach, and take a step further to explore the potential of integrating Wiener-informed structures directly into diffusion transformers, and try to develop next foundation architecture for flow-matching tasks. 19 Preprint – NUS HPC-AI Lab

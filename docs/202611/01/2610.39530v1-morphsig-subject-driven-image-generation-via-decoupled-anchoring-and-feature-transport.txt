Abstract
We propose MorphSIG, a training-free framework that reformulates subject-driven
image generation (SIG) as pseudo-video feature transport. MorphSIG contains two
core modules: Progressive Decoupled Anchoring, which uses frequency-domain
decoupling and structural scrambling during early denoising to break structural
locking and enable a gradual transition from free composition to identity alignment;
and Pseudo-flow Guided Feature Transport, which constructs a latent-space pseudo-
flow field to mimic Image-to-Video temporal coherence and transport subject
semantics precisely. To address stylistic homogeneity and the lack of ground truth
in existing benchmarks, we further introduce a benchmark with diverse stylized
subjects. Extensive experiments show that MorphSIG significantly improves pose
diversity and subject fidelity over baselines without additional training. Overall,
MorphSIG bridges static generation and dynamic propagation, providing a cost-
effective paradigm for high-fidelity subject consistency. The Code and benchmark
will be available here.

Introduction
Subject-driven image generation (SIG) aims to synthesize images that follow text prompts while pre-
serving the details of reference subjects [27, 38, 16, 54, 12]. By enabling user-defined customization,
SIG has broad applications in film production [60] and advertising [20].
In practical applications, SIG faces two major challenges: ❶the "copy-paste pattern," where generated
results inherit the reference pose and lack compositional freedom; and ❷"subject inconsistency,"
where subject features degrade during diffusion, reducing identity stability.
As illustrated in Fig. 2, existing training-based SIG methods anchor identity through large-scale
paired datasets [38, 15, 57, 54, 12] or the introduction of auxiliary encoding networks [62, 32, 49],
yet these incur significant computational costs and are prone to overfitting. Training-free approaches
[64, 31, 17, 7] typically inject subject features via attention-sharing mechanisms after inverting the
reference image; however, they struggle to preserve identity due to the lack of consistency constraints.
Another research [60, 52, 30, 66, 46, 10] attempts to leverage the dynamic priors of video models for
image-level SIG, but suffers from low efficiency due to the generation of redundant transition frames.

To balance fidelity and diversity, we propose MorphSIG, a training-free SIG framework. First, to
alleviate the "copy-paste" pattern, we introduce Progressive Decoupled Anchoring. By applying
low-pass filtering and high-frequency structural permutation to reference Keys in joint self-attention,
it removes global geometric bias while preserving essential feature statistics. Combined with time-
dependent anchoring strength, this strategy unlocks compositional flexibility during early diffusion.
Second, to improve consistency, we reformulate T&I2I as a "two-frame video generation" paradigm,
treating the reference and target images as pseudo-first and pseudo-last frames. Based on this
view, we propose Pseudo-flow Guided Warped Feature Transport, which simulates temporal
coherence by warping reference features with pseudo-flow. Combining low-frequency Spherical
Linear Interpolation and high-frequency Winner-Take-All enables effective transport of warped
reference features, improving detail generation while preserving structural consistency.
We further introduce a benchmark with diverse styles and high-quality Ground Truth, enabling
rigorous evaluation of fidelity and diversity in SIG. Our contributions are summarized as follows.
• We identify the "high-frequency shortcut" as the main cause of the "copy-paste" pattern in
T&I2I, and redefine T&I2I as a "pseudo-video" task to simulate I2V temporal coherence
without redundant computation.
• We propose Progressive Decoupled Anchoring to mitigate high-frequency shortcuts, and
Pseudo-flow Guided Feature Transport to preserve subject consistency.
• We introduce a new SIG benchmark with diverse styles and high-quality Ground Truth,
addressing the lack of objective metrics and stylistic diversity in existing datasets.
• As a training-free framework, MorphSIG achieves superior fidelity-diversity balance on
both public and our proposed benchmarks.

Related Work
Consistent Text-to-Image Generation. While fine-tune approaches [38, 27, 22, 48, 43, 2] are
capable of capturing visual details, they suffer from high computational overhead. To balance
efficiency and performance, lightweight adapters [32, 65, 62, 59, 58, 61] inject external guidance into
frozen backbone; however, these often lack sufficient representation capacity. In contrast, training-
free methods [16, 45, 31, 47, 28, 35, 36] leverage in-context learning for "plug-and-play" flexibility,
but struggle with complex instructions, often failing to maintain textual consistency under large-scale
pose variations.
Subject-driven Image Generation synthesizes reference images to precisely preserve subject
identities [54, 12, 55]. For instance, DreamO [33] and USO [53] integrate reference images to
generate outputs that mirror both input identity and aesthetics. While frameworks like OmniGen
[56, 51] and others [25, 39, 13, 42] enhance identity decoupling, they often necessitate large-scale
retraining, which limits their scalability. Conversely, training-free methods [64, 24, 11, 26] utilize
optimized attention mechanisms to inject subject features across frames during inference.
Image Generation and Video. Recent video models [4, 5, 9, 23] have inspired using video priors
for image tasks [37, 60, 66, 52]. Specifically, UniReal [10] and iMontage [19] treat image generation
as video tasks to leverage world dynamics, while RealGeneral [30] unifies tasks via conditional
frame prediction. To handle complexity, PLACID [44] uses spatio-temporal trajectories for feature
constancy, and CoF-T2I [46] employs "Chain-of-Frames" for progressive refinement. Despite these
gains, video-based models suffer from high overhead and latency. Conversely, we propose achieving
high-quality generation by endowing static diffusion models with dynamic evolutionary capabilities.

3.2
Problems and Analysis
We analyze two key observations in T&I2I models, as shown in Fig.1: Observation ❶: Copy-
Paste Pattern. Generated images rigidly inherit the reference pose. Observation ❷: Subject
Inconsistency. Subject identity or features gradually degrade during generation. We first analyze and
formally explain these issues, then propose targeted optimizations to balance fidelity and diversity.
Analysis for ❶: The consistency gain in T&I2I comes at the expense of generation freedom.
As shown in Fig.1(A), T&I2I models often directly replicate reference features, reducing diversity [21,
29, 57, 15]. We attribute this to a high-frequency shortcut effect: although diffusion models generally
follow a coarse-to-fine generation process [63], T&I2I models capture high-frequency reference
features too early to preserve identity consistency. This excessive coupling prematurely locks
generation. Specifically, Fig.4(a) shows that Qgen strongly activates edge contours in Kref during
early sampling. The spectrum in Fig.4(b) further shows that these high-frequency "anchors" constrain
macro-scale pose evolution. As a result, the control ability of the text query Qt is significantly
weakened by the high-frequency shortcut, as illustrated in Fig.4(c).
Analysis for ❷: The temporal consistency via first-frame feature transport.
In I2V modeling, the attention for the latent sequence zvideo explicitly constructs cross-frame attention
by facilitating direct interaction between the query vectors Qi
t,l of subsequent frames and the key
vector K1

temporal correspondences, ensuring that visual priors from the initial frame are accurately propagated
to subsequent frames, thereby maintaining identity stability while modeling temporal motion [41, 60].
Inspired by this, recent image-level methods [30, 10] adopt a "non-contiguous" frame generation
architecture. By treating input and output images as a "pseudo-frame" sequence, they leverage the
video model’s inherent capacity for maintaining structural and feature consistency.

3.3
Progressive Decoupled Anchoring (PDA)
Motivation. While T2I offer high compositional flexibility through noise synthesis, they struggle with
subject consistency. Conversely, T&I2I anchor identity but often fall into "high-frequency shortcuts."
Current solutions typically rely on computationally expensive paradigms, such as large-scale paired
data augmentation [15, 57] or complex feedback optimization [12, 21]. We hope that an ideal T&I2I
should balance the creative freedom of T2I with the robust consistency of T&I2I.
Spectral Decoupling (SD). In joint multi-modal attention, Kref acts as a semantic anchor. Its
high-frequency components often produce sharp attention peaks that lock generation to the reference
geometry. To alleviate this pose-locking effect, we reshape the reference latent Mref ∈RH×W ×D
and apply frequency decoupling: Mlow = G(Mref, σ),
Mhigh = Mref −Mlow.
Where G denotes a Gaussian low-pass filter with a kernel scale σ. While Mlow effectively circumvents
high-frequency structural shortcuts, it also incurs a notable loss of feature energy. As illustrated in
Fig. 5(b), an approximately 20% loss in Frobenius Norm significantly degrades the feature intensity
of the pre-trained model, consequently compromising the fidelity of subject reconstruction.
Structural Permutation (SP). To compensate for information loss from filtering while eliminating
geometric bias, we apply a block-level permutation P to the high-frequency residual Mhigh:
ˆ
Mref = Mlow + P(Mhigh, p),
(2)
where p denotes the patch size. As visualized in Fig. 5(c), SP redistributes high-frequency features as
"disassembled components" rather than merely erasing details. By disrupting global pose correlations
while preserving original statistical properties and local details, SP avoids the identity blurring and
feature collapse inherent in only low-pass schemes (which retain only 0.8 of the energy).
From the perspective of attention dynamics (Fig. 5(a)), the smooth distribution of only low-pass
features (Fig. 5(c)) triggers attention diffusion, which impairs precise alignment. In contrast, while
SP perturbs spatial coordinates, its block structures retain local gradient transitions. his high-contrast
distribution (Fig. 5(a)) allows the model to bypass compositional constraints while accurately
extracting critical details from the "feature pool," ensuring high-fidelity reconstruction.
Progressive Anchoring (PA). Leveraging the coarse-to-fine nature of diffusion denoising, we
introduce a time-dependent coefficient t ∈(1 →0) to dynamically modulate anchoring intensity:
Kref = Flatten (G(Mref, σ(t)) + P(Mref −G(Mref, σ(t)), p))
(3)
Macro-Evolution (t ≈1): Low-pass filter suppresses high-frequency spatial anchors, forcing the
model to synthesize global motion structures from the prompt Qt within the "spectral blind zone."
Micro-Alignment (t →0): As the global manifold stabilizes, structural signals are reintroduced as
local guides for identity reconstruction without disrupting the established pose.
3.4
Pseudo-Flow Guided Warping Feature Transport (WFT)
Motivation. The superior consistency of I2V stems from feature transport under temporal coherence
[60, 6]. Existing methods [37, 66, 60] reformulate T2I as “video frame prediction” to inherit this
property, but require generating redundant intermediate frames. We instead ask: “Can T&I2I
directly simulate the ‘I2V generation logic’ within T2I to enhance subject consistency?”

4
Experiments
Implementation Details. We implement our method on FLUX.2-Klein-4B, a mainstream T&I2I
framework with a DiT-based joint self-attention architecture. Progressive spectral decoupling is
applied in the first 20% of denoising steps, while pseudo-flow-guided feature transport is injected
during the intermediate phase, i.e., 50%–70% of steps, when semantic structures are stabilized
but fine-grained identity details are still refined. All experiments are conducted at 1024 × 1024
resolution on NVIDIA A100 40GB GPUs. Unless otherwise specified, hyperparameters are fixed
across benchmarks and prompts. Detailed hyperparameters are provided in the Appendix.
Baselines. We compare our method with recent SOTA SIG approaches covering both training-based
and training-free paradigms: Scone [50], UMO [12], UNO [54], MOSAIC [39], OmniGen2 [51],
DreamO [33], DiptychP [40], and XVerse [8]. These methods improve subject consistency through
large-scale paired training, preference optimization, explicit identity supervision, or inference-time
attention/latent feature manipulation without finetuning. For fair comparison, all baselines use official
checkpoints and default hyperparameters.
Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate our framework on the public VistoryBench [67] and our proposed

Local Consensus Analysis. As shown in Fig. 11(a), selecting K = 4 in Local Consensus achieves
the best balance between identity fidelity (CIDS) and diversity (INC). A smaller K admits spurious
matches from texture ambiguity, whereas a larger K over-suppresses valid correspondences by
incorporating noisy boundary neighbors.
Scheduling Sensitivity Analysis. As shown in Fig. 11(b, c), our default schedule achieves the optimal
balance between identity fidelity and generation diversity. Terminating PDA prematurely (10%)
locks macro-composition via high-frequency shortcuts, whereas prolonging it (>20%) impairs fine-
grained detail alignment. Similarly, WFT requires sufficient propagation without over-intervention:
truncating it early (<70%) hinders detail consistency, while extending it into late denoising (>80%)
introduces distribution mismatch and artifacts. Thus, our setting (PDA: 20%, WFT: 50%–70%)
reliably optimizes performance.

Conclusion
We presented MorphSIG, a training-free framework for subject-driven image generation. By iden-
tifying high-frequency reference shortcuts as a major cause of copy-paste artifacts, we proposed
Progressive Decoupled Anchoring to relax structural locking while preserving identity-related statis-
tics. We further reformulated T&I2I as pseudo-video generation and introduced Pseudo-flow Guided
Feature Transport to improve subject consistency through latent-space feature transport without
generating intermediate frames. In addition, we built a new SIG benchmark with diverse styles and
high-quality ground truth. Experiments on public and proposed benchmarks show that MorphSIG
achieves a strong balance between identity fidelity and generation diversity. These results demon-
strate the effectiveness of frequency-decoupled conditioning and pseudo-flow-guided transport for
controllable and consistent subject-driven generation.

## 1 Introduction 

The demand for expansive, high-fidelity 3D urban environments has surged in recent years, driven by rapidly expanding applications in virtual reality, immersive gaming, autonomous driving simulation, and urban digital twins [15, 20, 22, 32]. Traditionally, constructing these large-scale scenes relies on exhaustive manual modeling by technical artists [7]. While recent advancements in 3D generative AI have shown remarkable success in synthesizing single objects or constrained indoor scenes [26, 28, 34, 38], subsequent feed-forward and texturing-oriented systems have further improved consistency and efficiency [8, 19, 30]. Scaling these capabilities to the magnitude of an entire city nevertheless remains a formidable challenge. The primary bottleneck in city-scale 3D generation lies in the extreme scarcity of high-quality, real-world urban 3D data. Con-strained by this limitation, existing generative approaches are forced into a fundamental trade-off, typically falling into one of two sub-optimal paradigms. On one hand, bottom-up instance composi-tion methods synthesize individual buildings and subsequently stitch them into a scene [10, 11, 18, 46, 53]. While scalable, such approaches inevitably break global contextual consistency and com-promise stylistic coherence. On the other hand, holistic scene-level models rely on synthetic datasets or low-quality satellite imagery for training [25, 27, 43–45], which introduce significant domain gaps and inherit physical artifacts, such as distorted (“melted”) geometries and baked-in shadows. As a result, the generated envi-ronments often lack the richness and realism of real-world cities. In stark contrast, while 3D urban data remains scarce, 2D image data is abundantly available. Modern diffusion models [4, 9, 12, 36, 42, 52] have demonstrated an extraordinary ability to capture globally consistent visual priors, encoding rich semantic structures of urban layouts and architectural styles. This raises a fundamental question: can we leverage these pre-trained 2D priors as a “free lunch” to enable photorealistic 3D city texturing without requiring city-scale 3D supervision? To bridge this gap, we propose CityPaint, a 3D-data-free frame-work that reformulates city-scale 3D generation as a geometry-grounded generative reconstruction problem. Instead of synthesiz-ing unconstrained 3D volumes from scratch, CityPaint leverages readily available geographic data, such as OpenStreetMap, to initial-ize a semantically structured city mesh. As illustrated in Fig. 1, this lightweight geometric scaffold serves as a structural canvas, onto which the generative power of pre-trained 2D diffusion models is projected, enabling diverse textures and stylistic variations directly on 3D surfaces.

However, directly projecting 2D generations onto large-scale 3D geometry introduces significant challenges. Fixed-height camera trajectories either collide with tall structures or drift excessively above low-rise regions, while oblique viewpoints lead to severe texture distortion and cross-view inconsistencies. To address these issues, CityPaint introduces a heightfield-aware camera placement strategy that adapts to local urban geometry, ensuring stable and consistent observations. Furthermore, rather than naively averaging multi-view projections, we propose a robust face-wise top-𝐾 camera baking scheme. By explicitly modeling visibility and discarding grazing-angle observations, this strategy enables seamless texture aggregation into a globally coherent UV atlas, effectively avoiding the blurring artifacts common in conventional blending approaches. To account for the distinct visual characteristics of different ur-ban elements, we introduce a decoupled refinement mechanism. Large-scale top-down renderings often lack sufficient resolution to capture fine-grained facade details, while stitched terrain patches tend to introduce noticeable seams. These observations suggest that a uniform treatment of the entire city is inherently suboptimal. To address this, CityPaint decomposes the refinement process, optimiz-ing each structural component in its most suitable representation. In summary, our main contributions are as follows: 

• We propose CityPaint , a novel 3D-data-free framework that leverages the rich visual priors of pre-trained 2D diffusion models to generate high-fidelity and globally consistent city-scale 3D textures. 

• We introduce a heightfield-aware observation policy, coupled with a robust top-𝐾 camera baking strategy, which effectively resolves visibility conflicts and mitigates texture distortion in large-scale 2D-to-3D projection. 

• We design a decoupled refinement mechanism that opti-mizes terrain in UV space and building facades in instance space, significantly improving both global continuity and fine-grained structural details. 

• Extensive experiments show that CityPaint achieves stronger overall performance than the evaluated baselines in visual fidelity, realism, and cross-view consistency, offering a scal-able solution for high-quality urban 3D content generation. 

## 2 Related Work 2.1 2D-to-3D Generation via Diffusion Priors 

The emergence of large-scale 2D diffusion models has significantly advanced 3D content generation. A dominant line of work leverages CityPaint: A Free Lunch from 2D Diffusion for City-Scale 3D Texturing MM ’26, November 10–14, 2026, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil 

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) [26, 34, 40, 41] to lift 2D diffusion priors into 3D representations, including Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [31], explicit meshes, and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [21]. In parallel, feed-forward and multi-view diffusion models [14, 16, 28, 38] have been proposed to improve generation efficiency and enforce cross-view consistency. Despite these advances, exist-ing approaches are primarily designed for object-centric scenarios and struggle to scale to unbounded and structurally complex envi-ronments. In particular, unconstrained optimization in SDS-based methods often suffers from over-smoothing, mode collapse, and multi-face artifacts, while feed-forward approaches remain limited by their reliance on implicit geometric priors. These limitations become more pronounced in urban scenes, where large spatial ex-tents, intricate structural layouts, and ambiguous text descriptions jointly exacerbate geometric inconsistencies and texture misalign-ment. Rather than relying on unstable gradient-based distillation, CityPaint grounds the generative process on deterministic geom-etry derived from semantic maps. This design effectively reduces spatial ambiguity and enables precise, globally consistent texture synthesis at the city scale. 

## 2.2 Urban Scene Generation 

Recent deep learning approaches for urban scene generation gener-ally follow two paradigms. The first is instance-based composition, which employs object-level asset pipelines [18, 46, 53] to generate individual buildings that are subsequently populated onto layouts sourced from OpenStreetMap [33], satellite or generative models [10, 11]. However, this bottom-up assembly naturally neglects the global urban context, resulting in disjointed environments with conflicting architectural styles. The second paradigm attempts holistic scene generation by train-ing directly on large-scale 3D urban datasets. These approaches typ-ically rely on either synthetic city corpora [17, 24, 45] or real-world captures such as satellite and Google Earth imagery [25, 27, 43, 44]. Unfortunately, both data sources present significant drawbacks. Models trained on synthetic data often exhibit a severe domain gap, lacking real-world intricacies. Conversely, pipelines driven by aerial photogrammetry inevitably inherit the physical flaws of their source data, suffering from “melted” geometries, blurry textures, and baked-in shadows. Our CityPaint framework bypasses these 3D data bottlenecks. By leveraging lightweight geographic data purely for geometric scaffolding, our method extracts photorealistic textures directly from 2D priors without requiring city-scale 3D urban training data. 

## 2.3 Texture Synthesis for 3D Meshes 

Recent methods for texturing existing 3D geometries primarily fall into two paradigms: direct UV-space generation and multi-view projection. Direct UV generation approaches [2, 13, 39, 49] use generative models to synthesize unwrapped texture atlases. While subsequent coarse-to-fine and city-oriented extensions [46, 50] have advanced this paradigm, their performance remains limited by the scarcity of high-quality, paired 3D-UV training data. Consequently, scaling these approaches to city-scale meshes is fundamentally impractical. To overcome these data limitations, multi-view projection pipelines [6, 23, 35, 51] have gained prominence. These methods bypass ex-plicit UV generation by projecting synthesized 2D views directly onto the 3D mesh. Although synchronized and scene-level exten-sions [8, 19, 29, 47] have improved multi-view consistency, scaling these techniques to expansive urban environments introduces crit-ical geometric challenges. Specifically, standard fixed-trajectory sampling struggles with extreme topography, while grazing-angle projections and naive view blending cause severe stretching, ghost-ing, and blurring. Alternatively, decomposing building facades into UVs [37] introduces noticeable stitching artifacts and compromises realism. To overcome these challenges, we introduce a heightfield-aware top-𝐾 camera baking strategy and a decoupled refinement mechanism tailored for complex urban geometries. 

## 3 Method 

CityPaint generates city-scale textured 3D scenes from geographic coordinates and a text prompt. Instead of synthesizing urban en-vironments from scratch, we exploit appearance priors from pre-trained 2D diffusion models and cast city texturing as a geometry-grounded generative reconstruction problem. As shown in Fig. 2 and Fig. 3, the pipeline contains four stages: scene initialization, global iterative generation, terrain refinement, and building refine-ment.

## 4 Experiments 4.1 Experimental Details 

Across all three stages (global generation, terrain refinement, and building refinement), we adopt the open-source generative model Z-Image [4] as the shared diffusion prior. In the global stage, we iteratively texture the scene onto an 8192 × 8192 UV atlas from 320 views. The cameras follow our heightfield-aware trajectory with 8 scan strips and 40 views per strip, using a 48 ◦ downward elevation and a 40 ◦ field of view. For each selected view, we render a depth map, a semantic control map, and the current partial appearance from the UV atlas, and run 25 denoising steps in the conditional diffusion model. For the subsequent refinement stages, terrain refinement oper-ates on overlapping 1024 × 1024 UV tiles with a denoising strength of 0.2 and the shared global-noise initialization described in Eq. 15. ## 4.2 Evaluation Metrics 

A key challenge in evaluating city-scale 3D generation is the lack of reliable reference image distributions. Existing works commonly use Google Earth imagery [43, 44] as an evaluation benchmark. However, such imagery is not ideal for assessing photorealism be-cause it often contains reconstruction artifacts, compression noise, and noticeable blurring, as illustrated in Fig. 4. Consequently, met-rics computed solely on this distribution may not accurately reflect the visual quality of generated results. To address this limitation, we introduce a complementary high-quality benchmark, termed CityWeb . We collect a large-scale set of real-world urban photographs from publicly accessible web sources and apply a multi-stage filtering pipeline to remove non-photographic, low-resolution, and watermarked images. A GPT-based scoring mechanism [1] then ranks the candidates according to urban relevance, visual clarity, and overall fidelity. The top 20,000 images are retained to form the CityWeb benchmark, yielding a cleaner and more realistic reference distribution than Google Earth alone. We evaluate CityPaint using both benchmarks. For distribution-level alignment, we report Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Kernel Inception Distance (KID) against Google Earth for compara-bility with prior work and against CityWeb for high-fidelity realism. To further evaluate perceptual quality without relying on a refer-ence distribution, we also report the no-reference metrics Natural Image Quality Evaluator (NIQE) and BRISQUE. Finally, to assess the geometric consistency of the generated 3D scenes, we follow prior works [43, 44, 46] and report two met-rics. Camera Error (CE) measures the scale-invariant L2 distance between the inferred camera trajectory and poses estimated by COLMAP. Depth Error (DE) evaluates geometric accuracy using the normalized L2 distance between predicted and pseudo ground-truth depth maps.

## 4.4 Ablation Study 

We ablate each core component to assess its impact on geometric consistency and image quality. Quantitative results are reported in Table 3. 

Effect of Heightfield-aware Camera Placement: Replacing our adaptive trajectory with fixed-height grid observations ( w/o Height-aware Cam. ) increases both CE and DE. Fixed cameras en-counter grazing views or geometry collisions in uneven urban regions, causing texture stretching and projection mismatches. 

Effect of Top-𝐾 Camera Baking: Replacing face-wise view selection with multi-view averaging ( w/o Top-𝐾 Baking ) causes the largest drop in visual fidelity. Averaging conflicting observations introduces blurring, ghosting, and incoherent facades, whereas top-𝐾 baking preserves sharp, consistent textures (Fig. 6). 

Effect of Terrain Refinement: Removing tile-wise UV denois-ing ( w/o Terrain Refinement ) degrades perceptual quality because global projections leave large terrain regions at low effective resolu-tion with visible tile-boundary seams. UV-space refinement restores coherent ground details. 

Effect of Building Refinement: Removing instance-centric optimization ( w/o Building Refinement ) increases FID and perceptual errors. Object-centric orbit views provide the pixel density needed to recover high-frequency facade details that are missing from global top-down observations. 

## 5 Conclusion 

We presented CityPaint , a 3D-data-free framework that reformu-lates city-scale 3D generation as a geometry-grounded generative reconstruction problem. By exploiting 2D diffusion priors, CityPaint reduces the reliance on scarce city-scale 3D urban datasets. Through face-wise top-𝐾 camera baking and a decoupled refinement pipeline, our method synthesizes text-guided urban environments that bal-ance global consistency with high-fidelity local details. Extensive evaluations show that CityPaint achieves stronger overall results than the compared baselines across geometric consistency and visual-quality metrics. Furthermore, we introduce CityWeb , a high-fidelity real-image benchmark designed to provide a more reliable reference for evaluating photorealism in urban scene synthesis.

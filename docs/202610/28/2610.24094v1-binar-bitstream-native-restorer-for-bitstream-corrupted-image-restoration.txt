ABSTRACT 

Bitstream-corrupted image restoration (BCIR) aims to reconstruct faithful images from corrupted compressed bitstreams. However, corruption occurs in bitstream-domain may lead to unpredictable error propagation and distortion amplifica-tion in pixel-domain, and even result in complete undecodability by standard de-coders, posing significant challenge to existing image restoration paradigms. To address this challenge, we propose Bi tstream Na tive Restorer ( BiNar ), a genera-tive restoration framework that leverages fine-grained bitstream-native semantics to guide bitstream-corrupted image restoration. In BiNar, Bitstream Semantic Modeling ( BSeM ) extracts robust informative semantics and models sequential bytes directly from corrupted bitstream, enabling to generate fine-grained bit-stream descriptions without relying on pixel decoding. Based on the descriptions, the Corruption-adaptive Multimodal Semantic Aggregation ( CMSA ) further ex-tracts adaptive visual modal cues in decoded corrupted images and aggregates to bitstream descriptions. The multimodal aggregated fine-grained descriptions can serve as coherent restoration guidance. Finally, Blind Corruption Localization (BCoL ) exploits the segmentation prior of visual foundation models to identify and locate error regions that requires recovery in corrupted images. The located error regions and the fine-grained descriptions jointly serve as visual prompt and language prompt of diffusion models image restoration, respectively. Extensive experiments under diverse bitstream corruption conditions show that BiNar out-performs all State-of-the-arts image restoration models regardless of perceptual quality or objective quality. Ablation studies demonstrate the effectiveness of fine-grained bitstream-native semantics in BCIR task. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Image restoration aims to recover clean images from degraded inputs by exploiting the texture, struc-ture, and semantic cues that remain visible. Faithful restoration therefore depends on whether these cues remain informative about the original image. However, bitstream corruption can severely dis-tort or even eliminate these cues. Compressed image bitstreams are highly vulnerable to corruption during storage and transmission. Since bitstream corruption occurs before image decoding, localized errors may propagate through the decoding process and disrupt subsequent coded content Lam & Reibman (1995); Bourdon et al. (2005). As a result, even limited corruption can produce widespread and unpredictable pixel-domain distortions, including color shifts, structural displacement, missing content, and severe visual artifacts Liu et al. (2023b). Under such degradation, semantic modeling from the decoded image becomes increasingly challenging, as the observed pixels may no longer faithfully represent the original content. In more severe cases, bitstream corruption can even cause decoding failure, leaving no usable visual information from which semantic cues can be extracted. Such degraded or missing semantics make faithful image restoration particularly challenging. As illustrated in Fig. 1, existing methods for bitstream-corrupted image restoration (BCIR) mainly attempt to recover visual information after decoding or exploit thumbnail guidance for restoration Liu et al. (2023b); Hu et al. (2025). When decoding produces severe color distortion, structural dis-placement, or missing regions, the remaining pixels provide limited information for recovering fine structures and semantic details. Moreover, thumbnail guidance is not universally available. Some compressed image files do not contain an embedded thumbnail, since common image formats such as Exif and JFIF do not require one Hamilton (2004). A bitstream error can disrupt the decoding process and propagate to subsequent content, causing a much larger region to become distorted in the pixel Kuo et al. (2019). However, this propagation does not imply that the corresponding information has been equally lost from the compressed rep-resentation. Large portions of the coded data may remain unchanged even when the decoded image exhibits severe color distortion, structural displacement, or missing regions. These surviving bytes can therefore preserve information about the original image that is no longer faithfully expressed in the decoded observation. This makes the corrupted bitstream itself a promising source of semantic information for restoration. To this end, we propose Bitstream Native Restorer (BiNar) , a generative restoration framework that exploits fine-grained bitstream-native semantics for bitstream-corrupted image restoration. The key contribution of BiNar is to establish a semantic pathway directly from corrupted bitstreams, pro-viding informative restoration cues when pixel-derived semantics become unreliable or unavailable. Specifically, Bitstream Semantic Modeling (BSeM) directly models sequential bytes from cor-rupted bitstreams to extract fine-grained semantics without relying on pixel decoding. Corruption-adaptive Multimodal Semantic Aggregation (CMSA) further extracts useful visual cues from decoded corrupted images and aggregates them with the bitstream-derived semantics to obtain en-hanced fine-grained descriptions. In parallel, Blind Corruption Localization (BCoL) exploits the segmentation prior of a visual foundation model to identify regions requiring recovery. The result-ing fine-grained descriptions and localized corruption regions serve as language and visual prompts to jointly guide a pretrained diffusion model for image restoration. Extensive experiments across diverse bitstream corruption conditions show that BiNar achieves the best performance on seven of eight restoration metrics, while ablation studies further demonstrate the effectiveness of fine-grained bitstream-native semantics and the proposed components. 

2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 BITSTREAM -C ORRUPTED VISUAL RESTORATION 

Early restoration of corrupted compressed images mainly relied on error concealment, using surviv-ing spatial or transform-domain information to reconstruct damaged content (Gormish, 2003; Lee & Chen, 2003; Bourdon et al., 2005). More recent BCIR methods improve decoding robustness and exploit auxiliary information such as embedded thumbnails or neighboring coefficients for restora-tion (Liu et al., 2023b; Hu et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025). Generative approaches further introduce corruption localization, coding information, and visual foundation models to guide image or video recovery (Wang et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2023a; 2025; 2026). However, these methods still rely heav-ily on decoded visual information or auxiliary cues from the corrupted file. Generative priors have substantially improved image restoration by enabling plausible reconstruc-tion beyond the information directly available in degraded pixels. Early inpainting and diffusion-based methods recover missing content from known image regions (Yu et al., 2018; Suvorov et al., 2022; Lugmayr et al., 2022; Rombach et al., 2022). Recent restoration methods further incorporate large generative models and semantic conditions to improve perceptual quality and content con-sistency (Lin et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024). More explicit semantic guidance has been introduced through vision-language models, textual prompts, and global or local visual representations (Luo et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024a; Kong et al., 2025). However, such guidance is typically derived from degraded images and may become unreliable under severe bitstream corruption. This motivates extracting semantic information directly from the corrupted bitstream. 2.3 BITSTREAM -D OMAIN UNDERSTANDING 

Bitstream-domain understanding aims to extract visual information directly from encoded data with-out relying on full pixel reconstruction. Early studies mainly exploited structured representations available in valid compressed images for recognition and segmentation (Duan et al., 2023; Ji & Karam, 2024). More recent work moves beyond codec-specific features and models raw byte se-quences directly. ByteFormer (Horton et al., 2023), MEGABYTE (Yu et al., 2023), MambaByte (Wang et al.), and bGPT (Wu et al., 2024b) demonstrate that meaningful representations can be learned directly from byte-level inputs, reducing dependence on conventional decoding. Recent studies further extend this direction to corrupted bitstreams, where semantic information must be re-covered from incomplete or damaged encoded data. Cibic (Liang et al., 2026) explores semantic ex-traction and caption generation from corrupted image bitstreams, while BLMSP (Huang et al., 2026) investigates semantic priors from corrupted video bytes for multiple downstream tasks. CBSU-ALLM (Wu et al., 2026) further treats corrupted image bitstream fragments as a byte-language modality for semantic understanding without successful pixel decoding. Together, these studies show that corrupted bitstreams can retain useful semantic information beyond what is recoverable from decoded pixels. 33 METHODOLOGY 

3.1 FRAMEWORK OVERVIEW 

We propose Bitstream Native Restorer (BiNar) for bitstream-corrupted image restoration (BCIR). BiNar exploits fine-grained bitstream-native semantics together with available visual information and corruption localization to guide generative restoration. As shown in Fig. 2, Bitstream Semantic Modeling (BSeM) directly models the corrupted byte sequence to extract Plot and Scene semantics, where Scene is characterized by Locale and Vibe. Corruption-adaptive Multimodal Semantic Aggregation (CMSA) aggregates these bitstream-native semantics with useful visual cues from the decoded corrupted image to produce a fine-grained description. In parallel, Blind Corruption Localization (BCoL) identifies regions requiring recovery. The resulting semantic description and corruption regions jointly guide a pretrained diffusion model for image restoration. 3.2 BITSTREAM SEMANTIC MODELING 

BSeM models the corrupted byte sequence from two complementary semantic perspectives, Plot and 

Scene . Plot captures the main subjects, their appearance, actions, and interactions, while Scene de-scribes the broader context through Locale and Vibe . Together, they provide fine-grained bitstream-native semantics for restoration. 

Plot Semantic Modeling (PSM). Given a corrupted bitstream ˜b, PSM employs a ByteFormer encoder Ep to obtain its bitstream representation. Since the resulting representation is variable in length, a Query Adapter A with a fixed set of learnable queries aggregates it into compact semantic tokens,

Vibe . Locale describes the type of place or scene, while Vibe characterizes its overall environ-mental attributes. Two independently trained ByteFormer-based classifiers estimate their category distributions,

This retains multiple plausible scene hypotheses under corruption for subsequent aggregation. 

Training Objectives. PSM is optimized with an autoregressive generation loss Lcap , a token-level alignment loss Lalign , and a batch-wise contrastive loss Lcon ,

SSM is trained separately for Locale and Vibe prediction using classification supervision. The Plot description cp, Locale candidates Cl, and Vibe candidates Cv are subsequently passed to CMSA. 3.3 CORRUPTION -ADAPTIVE MULTIMODAL SEMANTIC AGGREGATION 

CMSA aggregates the bitstream-native semantics produced by BSeM with useful visual cues from the decoded corrupted image. Given the Plot description cp, Locale candidates Cl, Vibe candidates 4Cv , and the decoded image ˜x, the resulting fine-grained description is formulated as 

cm = FCMSA (cp, Cl, Cv , V(˜ x)) , (4) where V(˜ x) denotes the available visual cues and cm denotes the aggregated multimodal description. When recognizable content remains in the decoded image, CMSA uses visual cues to complement and refine the bitstream semantics. As decoding artifacts become more severe, CMSA relies in-creasingly on the information extracted directly from the bitstream. When decoding fails, cm is constructed solely from the BSeM outputs. The resulting description serves as the language prompt for restoration. 3.4 BLIND CORRUPTION LOCALIZATION AND SEMANTICALLY GUIDED RESTORATION

4 EXPERIMENT 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Dataset and Corruption. We construct the training and evaluation sets based on ADE20K, which contains diverse visual scenes with annotations corresponding to the Locale and Vibe semantics modeled in BSM. Each image is resized to 256 × 256 , center-cropped to 224 × 224 , and encoded into an image bitstream. We consider two corruption types, Bit-Flip and Byte-Loss, which randomly invert bits and remove byte information, respectively. For each corruption type, corruption is applied within four stream ranges, covering 25% , 50% , 75% , and 100% of the bitstream. Each of the re-sulting eight corruption configurations is independently applied to the complete test split, producing eight corrupted test subsets. For evaluation, each of the eight corruption configurations is applied to the complete test split, producing a separate corrupted test subset for each setting. All methods are evaluated on the same corrupted inputs. 

Implementation Details. For semantic extraction, BiNar directly takes the corrupted bitstream as input, while vision-language baselines operate on the corresponding decoded image. For restoration comparison, the restoration backbone and sampling settings are kept consistent whenever applicable. Clean images are used only as references for training or evaluation and are never available during inference. Additional training details are provided in the supplementary material. 

Evaluation Metrics. For semantic extraction, we report BLEU-1, BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, CIDEr, Token-F1, and BERTScore-F1. For image restoration, we report eight complementary metrics, di-vided into objective and perceptual categories. The objective metrics include PSNR, SSIM, MAE, and VFID, which evaluate pixel-level fidelity, structural consistency, reconstruction error, and dis-tributional similarity, respectively. The perceptual metrics include DreamSim, LPIPS, CLIP-I, and DINO-I, which assess high-level visual similarity, perceptual feature consistency, and semantic or representation-level alignment between restored and reference images. 54.2 EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS 

Since fine-grained semantic descriptions serve as the language guidance for restoration in BiNar, their quality directly affects the fidelity of the reconstructed content. We therefore first evaluate whether BiNar can obtain more accurate and robust semantics under bitstream corruption, and fur-ther examine whether this semantic advantage translates into better restoration quality. We then compare the complete BiNar framework with existing image restoration methods. 4.2.1 EVALUATIONS ON BITSTREAM -NATIVE SEMANTICS 

Fine-grained Semantic Description Quality. We compare the fine-grained semantic descriptions produced by BiNar with those generated by six representative vision-language models, including GLM-4.6V, InternVL3, Qwen3-VL, GPT-5.6, Gemini-3.8-Flash, and Claude Opus 4.6. The com-peting models infer semantics from decoded corrupted images, whereas BiNar combines bitstream-native semantics with useful visual cues to construct fine-grained descriptions. As shown in Table 1, BiNar achieves the best performance across all reported metrics. The examples in Fig. 3 further show that BiNar preserves image content, Locale, and Vibe more faithfully under bitstream corrup-tion, demonstrating the quality and robustness of the resulting fine-grained descriptions. 

Robustness of Bitstream-native Semantics. We further evaluate the robustness of bitstream-native semantics against semantics extracted from decoded corrupted images. The two variants use identical corrupted inputs, localization masks, diffusion models, and sampling settings, differ-ing only in the semantic source. As shown in Fig. 4, both variants degrade as corruption increases, but restoration guided by bitstream-native semantics degrades more slowly. Consequently, the per-formance gap consistently widens from 25% to 100% corruption across DreamSim, LPIPS, CLIP-I, and DINO-I. In particular, the gap increases from 0.009 to 0.160 on DreamSim and from 0.027 to 

0.265 on DINO-I. These results demonstrate that bitstream-native semantics remain more robust to increasing corruption and provide more stable restoration guidance when semantics inferred from decoded images become unreliable. 4.2.2 COMPARISON WITH SOTA I MAGE RESTORATION MODELS 

We compare BiNar with eight representative restoration methods, covering recent generative restora-tion approaches and methods designed for bitstream corruption. All methods are evaluated on the same corrupted test sets. Since our test images contain no embedded thumbnails, methods requiring thumbnail guidance use a downsampled version of the decoded corrupted image as auxiliary input, following their original preprocessing without introducing clean-image information. For video-based baselines, each corrupted image is treated as a single-frame sequence. When a fixed temporal length is required, the same corrupted frame is replicated along the temporal dimension, providing no additional temporal information.

We conduct controlled experiments to validate three key designs of BiNar, including the Plot–Scene decomposition in BSeM, corruption-adaptive multimodal semantic aggregation, and blind corrup-tion localization. 

Ablation on Plot–Scene Decomposition in BSeM. We examine the structured semantic modeling design in BSeM by comparing it with unified semantic generation. The unified variant directly generates a complete semantic description, while BSeM decomposes the bitstream semantics into Plot and Scene, with Scene further characterized by Locale and Vibe. As shown in Table 3, the proposed decomposition consistently improves all semantic description metrics, demonstrating the effectiveness of structured semantic modeling in BSeM. 

Ablation on Corruption-adaptive Multimodal Semantic Aggregation. We compare visual-only, bitstream-only, and the full CMSA. As shown in Table 4, the bitstream-only variant outper-forms the visual-only variant on most metrics, while CMSA further improves semantic quality by incorporating complementary visual cues when they remain informative. 

Ablation on Blind Corruption Localization. We compare BCoL with a generic SAM-based lo-calization baseline. BCoL keeps the pretrained visual encoder frozen and trains only a lightweight corruption localization head. It improves mIoU from 0.7957 to 0.9925 and Dice-F1 from 0.8765 to 

0.9962 , demonstrating substantially more accurate localization of bitstream-induced corruption. 

5 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we presented Bitstream Native Restorer (BiNar), a generative framework for bitstream-corrupted image restoration that exploits fine-grained bitstream-native semantics for image recon-struction. BiNar uses Bitstream Semantic Modeling (BSeM) to extract semantic information directly from corrupted bitstreams without relying on pixel decoding. Corruption-adaptive Multimodal Se-mantic Aggregation (CMSA) further aggregates useful visual cues with the bitstream-derived se-mantics, while Blind Corruption Localization (BCoL) identifies regions requiring recovery. The resulting semantic descriptions and spatial prompts jointly guide diffusion-based restoration. Exten-sive experiments demonstrate that bitstream-native semantics remain more informative than pixel-derived semantics under bitstream corruption and provide effective guidance for restoring both vi-sual quality and semantic content. BiNar consistently achieves strong restoration performance across diverse corruption conditions, while ablation studies validate the effectiveness of its key components. These results highlight corrupted bitstreams as a valuable native source of semantic information for robust image restoration.

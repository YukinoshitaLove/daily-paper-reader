# Taming Noise Initialization for Zero-shot Video Restoration with Image Diffusion Priors 

Chong Wang, Zixuan Fu, Hao Cheng Member, IEEE , Siyuan Yang Member, IEEE ,Alex C. Kot, Life Fellow, IEEE , Bihan Wen, Senior Member, IEEE 

Abstract —Recent advances in diffusion models have achieved remarkable success in restoring high-quality images in a zero-shot manner. Unfortunately, extending this success to video restoration is challenging due to the substantial computational resources required to pre-train video diffusion models. A rising trend seeks to address video restoration by utilizing a pre-trained image diffusion model, initiating restoration from either frame-wise independent or identical Gaussian noise. Yet, these approaches often fall short, as such simple noise initiations fail to capture and utilize the inherent temporal correlations within degraded video frames. In this work, we propose taming noise initialization for zero-shot video restoration, which preserves the intrinsic temporal correlations by generating initial noises directly using DDIM inversion on the corrupted video frames themselves. Building on this, we introduce an adaptive noise blending strategy that suppresses degradation patterns encoded during DDIM inversion while gradually steering the noise toward a Gaussian distribution. To further emphasize the temporal consistency, we propose measurement-guided trajectory attention to better align features across frames. Extensive experiments on multiple video restoration tasks demonstrate that our method outperforms existing zero-shot approaches, consistently achieving superior results on the DAVIS, REDS4, and Set8 datasets. 

Index Terms —Diffusion models, video restoration, zero-shot restoration.

ever, existing approaches directly start restoration with asimple noise initiation, completely neglecting the temporal correlation across frames. Specifically, [1] initializes restora-tion with identical noise for all frames, imposes an overly strong restriction between frames, potentially constraining the natural dynamics of the restored frames. Conversely, [23] adopts frame-wise completely independent Gaussian noises that may allow excessive freedom for each frame, thereby violating the temporal correlation across frames. Therefore, determining how to appropriately impose temporal correlations during noise initialization is crucial for achieving promising video restoration results. To be more specific, the initialization determines the starting point of the sampling trajectory and strongly affects (i) how well the reverse process stays within the diffusion model learned data manifold, and (ii) how effec-tively the subsequent steps can correct the trajectory to satisfy the physical degradation model. To this end, we argue that the degraded videos inherently contain sufficient temporally correlated information, which can be effectively utilized to initialize the “right” noise for the diffusion restoration process. In this work, we propose a novel zero-shot framework for video restoration, called TINDER , which only necessitates a pre-trained image diffusion prior. Motivated by the success of DDIM inversion in video editing [24]–[27], which establish a deterministic mapping between clean image and the cor-responding noise sample. Our method starts by performing DDIM inversion on the degraded video frames to obtain tem-porally correlated initial noise, effectively preserving temporal correlations and providing essential temporal guidance for the restoration process. Unfortunately, directly applying DDIM inversion in video restoration will produce sub-optimal results since the degradation pattern in the input frames can also be encoded into the inverted noise. To this end, the main con-tribution of this paper is introducing an adaptive filtering strategy to tame noise initialization for video restoration. 

Since video sequences inherently exhibit dynamic content, with significant variations in frequency distribution even across consecutive frames. Thus, our filtering strategy dynamically tailors filters based on the energy distribution of each frame, which is further utilized to blend the DDIM inversion results with Gaussian noise. This strategy not only enables varying yet adaptive filtering on each DDIM inverted noise, but also compensates for the missing high-frequency energy caused by degradation in the inverted noise. Consequently, it steers the DDIM inversion noise towards a Gaussian distribution that better aligns with the diffusion process. To further emphasize temporal consistency, we propose a measurement-guided trajectory attention during the diffusion sampling pro-cess. More in detail, motion trajectories are estimated from adjacent input frames and incorporated into the attention layer of the diffusion network, ensuring temporal consistency between consecutive frames. Finally, the proposed method offers a flexible framework for video restoration, where data consistency can be guaranteed by plugging any existing image-based diffusion sampling strategy. Extensive experiments show that the proposed method consistently achieves superior per-formances in various video restoration tasks on the DAVIS, REDS4, and Set8 datasets. A. Conditional Diffusion for Video Restoration 

Denoising diffusion probabilistic models [2]–[4], [28]–[31] have been proven to be a powerful class of unconditional gen-erative models for high-quality image synthesis. This iterative denoising mechanism not only results in high-fidelity and di-verse image synthesis but also establishes a flexible framework that can be extended to various conditional tasks. Building on their remarkable success in unconditional image genera-tion, researchers have adapted these models for conditional settings by incorporating additional inputs such as degraded images. This adaptation allows the model to generate out-puts that are not only photorealistic but also context-specific, making them particularly well-suited for video restoration tasks where preserving spatial detail and temporal coher-ence is critical [15], [16], [32]. Traditional video restoration methods [33]–[41] typically employ deterministic mappings that learn a fixed transformation from degraded to restored frames. Recent representative approaches employ Transformer architectures to capture long-range spatio-temporal depen-dencies while explicitly addressing motion and alignment across frames. For instance, VRT [40] presents a Transformer-based framework with spatio-temporal attention and alignment mechanisms for high-quality video restoration, and RVRT [41] further improves temporal modeling and efficiency via arecurrent formulation with guided deformable attention for correspondence-aware feature aggregation under complex mo-tion. Benefiting from task-specific supervision, these end-to-end methods typically achieve strong performance and fast feed-forward inference when the target degradation and data distribution match the training setting. In contrast, diffusion-based approaches embrace a probabilistic framework that models the full underlying distribution of clean video frames. This probabilistic nature enables the model to capture intrinsic variability and uncertainty, leading to more robust restora-tion outcomes even in challenging conditions. Several studies have explored the application of diffusion models in video restoration. For instance, Zhou et al. [15] introduced Upscale-A-Video, a text-guided latent diffusion framework for video upscaling that ensures temporal coherence through integrated temporal layers and a flow-guided recurrent latent propagation module. Similarly, SATeCo [32] leverages spatial-temporal guidance extracted from low-resolution videos to calibrate the high-resolution video denoising and restoration. B. Diffusion Models of Zero-shot Restoration 

Although these task-specific diffusion models excel in nar-rowly defined problems, they frequently struggle with general-izability across different restoration tasks. Instead, another line of research tends to leverage pre-trained diffusion networks as generative priors to solve restoration tasks in a zero-shot manner [18]–[22], [42]. 

Zero-shot Image Restoration. Existing zero-shot approaches typically apply constraints on the intermediate estimation, guiding the generative process to align with the degradation process of each specific image restoration task. For example, DDNM [21] uses a range-null space decomposition on the clean image prediction, restricting diffusion to null-space components. A relaxed constraint is introduced in [18] by incorporating a least-squares penalty on the data term to ensure data consistency. In [22], a latent diffusion model enforces data consistency by optimizing the intermediate clean image prediction to minimize the data term during reverse sampling. DPS [19] further extends diffusion models to gen-eral nonlinear inverse problems by introducing a manifold-constrained gradient approximation, enabling wide applica-bility to noisy cases. Additionally, DDPG [42] proposes an iterative-preconditioned guidance approach that smoothly tran-sitions from back-projection to least-squares gradient steps, enhancing noise robustness in image restoration tasks. 

Zero-shot Video Restoration. Building on the successes in image restoration, recent studies have explored extending these techniques to video data. Zero-shot video restoration presents unique challenges due to the need for maintaining tempo-ral consistency across frames where simple frame-by-frame restoration methods often fail to address, leading to noticeable temporal flickering. To overcome this, Cao et al. [23] modify a pre-trained image diffusion model by incorporating cross-previous-frame attention layers. This adaptation explicitly models inter-frame dependencies, thereby mitigating flickering and ensuring that temporal features are coherently maintained across the sequence. Yeh et al. [43] introduce hierarchical latent warping to leverage pre-trained image restoration dif-fusion models for video restoration. Kwon et al. [1] proposed a video inverse solver that utilizes image diffusion models, introducing a batch-consistent diffusion sampling strategy to ensure consistency across batches. However, existing works only adopt a simple noise initiation, struggling to achieve temporal consistent results. In this paper, we propose to initiate obtaining a temporal-correlated noise by exploiting the temporal correlation inherent in the degraded video, which provides crucial guidance for the restoration process. A. Overall Framework 

The proposed framework aims to solve the video restoration problem while only necessitating a pre-trained image diffusion model without re-tuning for each specific task. As shown in Figure 2, our framework can be divided into three main components: 1) DDIM inversion for initial noise generation, 2) adaptive noise blending for degradation pattern mitigation, and 3) sampling with trajectory attention for temporal consistency enhancement. In contrast to the existing works [1], [23] where the Gaussian noise is adopted as the starting point of diffusion sampling, we initiate the restoration process by obtaining temporal-correlated noises via performing DDIM inversion on the input video frames, denoted as XT = {xiT }Ni=1 =

{DDIM inv (yi)}Ni=1 . With the merit of DDIM inversion, the temporal correlation across each frame in the input video 

Y can be encoded into the initial inversion noise XT , thus offering effective guidance for the subsequent restoration process. However, as the initial noise XT is derived from the input degraded video, the undesired degradation pattern can also be preserved and encoded through the DDIM inversion. Thus, directly performing diffusion sampling starting from 

XT could cause significant artifacts and hinder the restored results, as shown in Figure 3. To address this issue, we propose an adaptive noise blending strategy to mitigate the encoded degradation in initial inversion noise (Section IV-B). Lastly, we further propose a measurement-guided trajectory attention mechanism (Section IV-C) to achieve temporal consistent restoration during diffusion sampling. 

B. Adaptive Noise Blending 

In the context of video restoration, the noise is initialized via performing DDIM inversion on the degraded video frames. The inverted noise contains significant degradation patterns and directly adopting it for sampling will lead to sub-optimal restoration results. Figure 4 (a) and (c) present the image and frequency domain of the inverted noises using a degraded frame and corresponding clean frame, respectively. The initial inversion noise contains noticeable jagged patterns with details being smeared out compared to the corresponding ground truth (GT) inversion noise. This discrepancy is particularly pro-nounced in the frequency domain, where high frequencies are either completely lost or distorted, compelling the restoration process to infer or amplify the missing information. We can notice that the low frequency components of initial inversion noise are relatively accurate compared to the high frequency. Based on this observation, one may trivially adopt a pre-defined low-pass filter (LPF) to perform filtering on the initial inversion noise, which is common sense. However, video is known for its dynamic content, with significant variations in frequency distribution even between frames of the same video. Datasets and tasks. To evaluate the efficacy of the proposed method, we conducted comprehensive experiments across three video datasets: DAVIS [46], REDS4 [47], and Set8 [48]. For each dataset, we sampled 100 16-frame video clips as the ground truth for evaluation. To demonstrate the gener-alization capability of our method, we conduct three repre-sentative video restoration tasks including Gaussian deblur, super-resolution (SR), and inpainting. Following [1], [18], the degradation is performed using 2 × and 4 × bicubic down-sampling for SR. For Gaussian deblurring, a blur kernel of sized 13 ×13 with a standard deviation of 2.0 is employed. For video inpainting, we randomly drop 70 % pixels in each video frame.

G. Limitations 

Our method improves zero-shot restoration quality and stability through enhanced noise initialization and trajectory-guided temporal attention. However, it also introduces addi-tional computational overhead. Compared with the previous method [45], our current implementation is slower in wall-clock time under the same experimental setting. The previous method [45] takes 9.67 s per video, whereas our full pipeline takes 20.18 s per video. This increase is mainly due to the proposed components required by our framework, including DDIM inversion for obtaining the initial noise, as well as extra operations such as adaptive noise blending and trajec-tory attention. Specifically, the measurement-guided trajectory attention introduces a modest overhead in both runtime and memory, with around +1.94 s runtime and +500 MB memory overhead. Future work includes accelerating the pipeline by reducing the number of sampling steps, adopting faster initial-ization strategies, caching or reusing trajectories, and exploring acceleration techniques to obtain a faster feed-forward variant while preserving the proposed stability benefits. VI. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we present a novel zero-shot video restoration framework that only leverages a pre-trained image diffusion model. We initiate by obtaining correlated noise via DDIM inversion, which can effectively capture and utilize the inherent temporal correlation in the degraded video frames. Based on that, we propose an adaptive noise blending strategy to remove the degradation pattern encoded during the inver-sion process while steering the noise toward the Gaussian distribution to enhance the generation ability. We further propose a measurement-guided trajectory attention to empha-size the temporal consistency during the restoration process.

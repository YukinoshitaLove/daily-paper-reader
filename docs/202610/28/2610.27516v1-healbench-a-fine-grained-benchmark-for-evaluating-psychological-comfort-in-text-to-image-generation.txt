Comfort in Text-to-Image Generation

Wenxin Hu1, Xingjiao Wu1, Liang He1
1East China Normal University

Abstract
Current text-to-image and image-editing models can generate
photorealistic images with strong semantic fidelity and high
visual quality. Yet an important human-centered capability
remains underexplored: whether these systems can create im-
ages that users perceive as psychologically comforting. Draw-
ing on cognitive evaluation theory, we conceptualize psycho-
logical comfort not as a low-level visual property, but as an
affective response shaped by a viewer’s appraisal of restorative
and emotionally supportive cues. To study this problem, we
introduce HealBench, a large-scale, fine-grained benchmark
for evaluating perceived psychological comfort in restorative
visual stimuli. HealBench contains 30K high-quality image-
text pairs spanning 19 high-level categories and 92 subcate-
gories, constructed from 100K candidates through a human-
and-expert-in-the-loop pipeline. To preserve the restorative
nature of the data, the pipeline integrates VLM-based se-
mantic matching, multiple human preference models, expert
filtering, and augmentation guided by highly restorative ex-
emplars. We further construct HealBench-Eval, a rigorously
selected evaluation set of 1,095 instances using feature cluster-
ing. Using HealBench-Eval, we conduct extensive objective
and subjective evaluations of 14 representative image genera-
tion models, together with prompt-rewriting generation strate-
gies. Our results reveal a clear gap between conventional vi-
sual generation quality and perceived psychological comfort:
models that perform well on semantic alignment and aesthet-
ics still struggle to produce images experienced as emotionally
restorative. Further reinforcement learning experiments show
that HealScorer can serve as a practical reward signal for as-
sessing and improving the emotional and restorative quality
of generated images. These findings suggest that psycholog-
ical comfort is a distinct and underexplored dimension of
human-centered image generation, and establish HealBench
as a foundation for future work on affect-aware generative
models.

evaluable and optimization-relevant objective for image gen-
eration and image editing? Rather than treating comfort as
a vague holistic preference, we formulate healing-oriented
generation through three necessary dimensions: text-image
alignment, heal accuracy, and image quality. A generated
image should be considered healing only when it satisfies all
three dimensions; failure in any one dimension is sufficient
to disqualify the output. Accordingly, our goal is not merely
to rank images from better to worse, but to identify whether,
where, and why a generated image fails to provide perceived
psychological comfort.
Based on this formulation, we propose HealBench (shown
in Figure 2), a fine-grained benchmark for evaluating the
perceived psychological comfort of generated images. Heal-
Bench is constructed through a human-and-expert-in-the-
loop pipeline that curates high-quality data from a large-scale
candidate image-text pool, performs imitative data augmen-
tation, and builds a rigorously filtered test set for unified
comparison across models and prompting strategies. Unlike
preference-style benchmarks that primarily produce aggre-
gate scores, HealBench is designed around failure-based di-
agnosis: it identifies whether an output violates text-image
alignment, heal accuracy, or image quality, thereby revealing
different ways in which a generated image may fail to be heal-
ing. Evaluation alone, however, is insufficient for improving
generation systems. To make diagnosis actionable, we intro-


duce a score-plus-rationale feedback paradigm, where each
result is paired with both a comfort score and an explicit ex-
planation of which dimension fails and why. This structured
feedback supports prompt rewriting for both T2I genera-
tion and instruction-guided image editing. Since large-scale
model iteration cannot rely entirely on costly human evalua-
tion, we further train an automatic evaluator, Tri-dimensional
Healing Scorer (THS), on top of HealBench to approx-
imate these structured judgments and study its use as an
optimization signal for downstream improvement, including
RL-based fine-tuning.
Within this work, we treat psychological comfort as a dis-
tinct human-centered objective for visual generation, rather
than as a by-product of semantic alignment, aesthetics, or
generic preference. Our contributions are as follows: (1)We
formulate perceived psychological comfort as a failure-based
evaluation problem for healing-oriented visual generation,
defined by three necessary dimensions: text-image align-
ment, heal accuracy, and image quality. (2)We build Heal-
Bench, a fine-grained benchmark that diagnoses whether a
generated image fails on any of the three dimensions, in-
stead of relying solely on aggregate preference-style scoring.
(3)We introduce a score-plus-rationale feedback paradigm
and THS, turning structured diagnosis into an actionable
signal for prompt refinement and downstream model opti-
mization.
Related Work
Image Generation and Editing Models. Recent visual gen-
eration models have made substantial progress in fidelity,
semantic alignment, controllability, and human preference
alignment. Diffusion models such as DDPM, latent diffusion,
Imagen, and DiT enable high-resolution synthesis and strong
prompt following (Ho, Jain, and Abbeel 2020; Rombach et al.
2022; Saharia et al. 2022; Peebles and Xie 2023); autore-
gressive and token-based models such as DALL·E, Make-
A-Scene, and Muse improve complex content modeling and
composition (Ramesh et al. 2021; Gafni et al. 2022; Chang
et al. 2023); and flow-matching or rectified-flow methods
further enhance training and sampling efficiency (Lipman
et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2025; Esser et al. 2024). Preference op-
timization and reinforcement learning methods further align
T2I models with human judgments (Black et al. 2024; Fan
et al. 2023; Wallace et al. 2024), while text-guided editing
methods provide instruction-based control over image con-
tent and style (Hertz et al. 2023; Mokady et al. 2023; Brooks,
Holynski, and Efros 2023). However, these methods mainly
optimize whether images are faithful, realistic, editable, or
generally preferred. Existing affective methods, such as Af-
fective Image Filter and EmoEdit, focus on explicit emotion
cues or targeted emotional editing (Weng et al. 2023; Yang
et al. 2025), leaving subtler outcomes such as psychological
comfort, safety, companionship, and healing underexplored.
Text-to-Image Evaluation Benchmarks and Metrics. Ex-
isting T2I evaluation primarily targets semantic correctness,
visual quality, compositional reasoning, and generic pref-
erence. CLIP-based metrics estimate text-image alignment
(Radford et al. 2021); reward and preference models such as
PickScore, ImageReward, HPS, and HPSv3 capture overall
human preference (Kirstain et al. 2023; Xu et al. 2023; Wu
et al. 2023; Ma et al. 2025); and aesthetic metrics such as AVA
and NIMA assess visual appeal (Murray, Marchesotti, and
Perronnin 2012; Talebi and Milanfar 2018). Broader bench-
marks, including DrawBench, GenEval, T2I-CompBench,
VQAScore / GenAI-Bench, and HEIM, evaluate composi-
tionality, spatial relations, reasoning, robustness, safety, and
overall quality (Saharia et al. 2022; Ghosh, Hajishirzi, and
Schmidt 2023; Huang et al. 2023; Lee et al. 2023; Lin et al.
2024; Li et al. 2024). Yet these metrics mainly ask whether
an image is correct, high-quality, or preferred, rather than
whether it is psychologically comforting. Although HRS-
Bench introduces emotion-grounded evaluation (Bakr et al.
2023), and HEIM and GenAI-Bench cover broad evaluation
dimensions (Lee et al. 2023; Li et al. 2024), deeper affec-
tive outcomes such as soothingness and emotional comfort
remain insufficiently evaluated. Recent studies further show
that metric rankings can be unstable across prompts and
protocols (Wiles et al. 2025), and that faithfulness metrics
remain unreliable on challenging subsets (Saxon et al. 2024).
HealBench addresses this gap by treating perceived psy-
chological comfort as a dedicated, fine-grained, and failure-
diagnostic evaluation target.
Method
Overview
HealBench is constructed in four stages: (1) taxonomy de-
sign and web-video-based image collection; (2) caption-
ing, hierarchical labeling, and reward-model-based filter-
ing; (3) category-aware evaluation protocol construction and
HealScore annotation; (4) tri-dimensional scorer training and
data augmentation. Experts participate in taxonomy design,
question revision, and iterative quality checks, while VLM-
s/LLMs are used as scalable assistants.

THS as an Automatic Healing Scorer
We next examine whether THS can provide a more suitable
automatic evaluation signal for healing-oriented generation
than generic scoring models. To this end, we compare THS
with several widely used baselines, including PickScore,
LAION Aesthetic, CLIPScore, and HPSv3, using the same
pairwise ranking protocol as in the preference validation ex-
periment. As shown in Table 6, THS substantially outper-
forms all generic baselines across all three ranking metrics,
achieving 0.8527 in Accuracy, 0.7741 in Kendall’s τ, and
0.9223 in Spearman’s ρ. By contrast, the generic scorers
remain far behind, with Accuracy values clustered around
0.50–0.56 and much weaker rank correlation. These results
indicate that existing scoring models for semantics, aesthet-
ics, or broad human preference do not adequately capture
the specific notion of perceived psychological comfort tar-
geted in this work. Results justify the need for a task-specific
automatic scorer. Rather than serving only as a descriptive
metric, THS provides a scalable evaluation signal that is bet-
ter aligned with healing-oriented preference and can there-
fore support both large-scale benchmarking and downstream
optimization.

Exploratory RL Fine-Tuning with THS Reward
Finally, we conduct a preliminary study to examine whether
THS can serve not only as an evaluator, but also as a re-
ward signal for direct model optimization. To this end, we
fine-tune SD 3.5 Medium with Flow-GRPO using a reward
defined by a weighted combination of THS and CLIPScore.
As shown in Table 7, the Flow-GRPO fine-tuned model im-
proves over the base SD 3.5 Medium across all four metrics,
including Overall (23.47 →25.67), Semantic (6.12 →6.48),
Heal (5.73 →6.45), and Quality (5.88 →6.28). Notably, the
largest gain appears on the Heal dimension, which is consis-
tent with the role of THS as a healing-oriented reward sig-
nal. This suggests that the proposed scorer is not only useful
for benchmark evaluation, but can also provide an effective
optimization signal for improving healing-oriented genera-
tion. We emphasize that this experiment is exploratory rather
than a fully optimized RL recipe. Nevertheless, the consistent
gains across all dimensions provide initial evidence that THS
can support downstream alignment toward psychologically
comforting image generation.
Conclusion
We present HealBench, the first fine-grained benchmark for
evaluating perceived psychological comfort in text-to-image
generation and image editing. By explicitly defining healing-
oriented generation along three complementary dimensions
(text-image alignment, heal accuracy, and image quality), we
move psychological comfort from an implicit notion of pref-
erence to a structured and measurable objective. Building on
this formulation, we develop a large-scale human-and-expert-
in-the-loop benchmark, introduce the rule-based HealScore,
and further propose THS, a learnable automatic evaluator.
Our experiments reveal a clear gap between strong perfor-
mance on conventional generation metrics and true effec-
tiveness under healing-oriented evaluation. Beyond bench-
marking, we show that structured score-plus-rationale feed-
back can directly support generation improvement, and that
the HEAL taxonomy provides a useful basis for personalized
healing-content delivery. In conclusion, our results establish
psychological comfort as a distinct evaluation and optimiza-
tion target in generative vision, and position HealBench as
a strong foundation for future work on affect-aware, human-
centered multimodal generation.

Abstract 

Preference alignment has become a standard practice for text-to-image di ff usion models. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) simplifies this process by eliminating explicit reward modeling. Its di ff usion variant, Di ff usion-DPO, has become a widely adopted baseline. Di ff usion-DPO essentially encourages the likelihood of preferred samples while suppressing dispreferred ones. In this paper, we revisit DPO-style alignment methods for di ff usion models from the perspective of the manifold hypothesis. Under this view, natural images concentrate near a low-dimensional manifold embedded in the high-dimensional ambient space, whereas DPO directly optimizes preference distributions in the full space without accounting for this geometric structure. This creates a mismatch in the optimization dynamics: it suppresses geometry-preserving tangential updates, while insu ffi ciently restricting hazardous normal-direction updates. This mismatch gradually degrades image quality and diversity. To address this issue, we propose Anisotropic Geometry-Aware Preference Optimization ( APO ), which replaces the uniform Euclidean treatment of prediction errors with a geometry-aware anisotropic metric derived from the reference model. Concretely, APO adaptively strengthens regularization in directions where the reference denoising function is highly sensitive, while relaxing constraints in directions that permit safe semantic adjustment. This recalibrates preference optimization according to the local manifold geometry, and maintains the original manifold structure. Experiments show that APO achieves strong

1. Introduction 

Text-to-image (T2I) di ff usion models have achieved remarkable progress in re-cent years, enabling high-fidelity and semantically controllable image synthesis across diverse domains [1, 2, 3, 4]. Inspired by the success of reinforcement learning for alignment in large language models, a growing number of post-training strategies have recently been introduced for di ff usion models to improve human preference alignment, aesthetic quality, and prompt consistency [5, 6, 7, 8, 9]. Most existing approaches rely on training a reward model to learn preference signals and guide policy opti-mization. Di ff usion-DPO [10], which adapts Direct Preference Optimization [11] to di ff usion models, has emerged as a practical alignment approach. It directly optimizes pairwise preferences through implicit likelihood comparisons while avoiding explicit reward model training, substantially simplifying the alignment pipeline. Following Di ff usion-DPO, numerous variants have been proposed to improve aspects such as reward modeling, sampling e ffi ciency, optimization stability, and model adaptability in di ff usion alignment[12, 13, 14, 15]. Despite these advances, we find that DPO-based alignment of di ff usion models still su ff ers from a previously overlooked geometric failure mode: o ff -manifold drift. The manifold hypothesis posits that natural image distributions concentrate near a low-dimensional manifold embedded in the ambient latent denoising space [16, 17]. In di ff usion models, preference optimization is performed in the noisy latent denoising space conditioned on the text prompt, where the local geometry evolves across di ff usion timesteps. However, although preference signals are ultimately defined over natural images, optimization is still performed in the full high-dimensional latent denoising space without considering the intrinsic geometry of the underlying data manifold. In 2particular, existing DPO-style objectives [10] optimize denoising prediction errors under an isotropic Euclidean metric, implicitly treating all perturbation directions in the latent space equally. This leads to two consequences: 

• Updates along manifold-normal directions can easily push samples away from the natural image manifold, causing semantic distortion and quality degradation. Without explicit suppression of these geometry-sensitive directions, o ff -manifold drift accumulates during alignment. 

• Updates along manifold-tangent directions are generally safer and often capture semantically meaningful variations. However, they are constrained by the same uniform regularization strength, unnecessarily limiting flexible semantic adjustments in preference optimization. While some prior works have incorporated manifold-aware geometric constraints into generative dynamics, such considerations remain largely unexplored in post-training preference alignment for pretrained di ff usion models. To address this issue, we propose Anisotropic Geometry-Aware Preference Opti-mization (APO) . APO constructs a Jacobian-induced anisotropic metric from the local score-field geometry of the frozen reference model, adaptively suppressing updates along geometry-sensitive normal directions while allowing more flexible exploration along manifold-aligned tangent directions. To make the method computationally prac-tical, we further derive an e ffi cient forward-only approximation based on Hutchinson estimation and finite di ff erences, eliminating the expensive reference-model backward pass while preserving the geometric regularization e ff ect. Our key observations and technical contributions are summarized as follows: 

• We identify o ff -manifold drift as a fundamental geometric failure mode in DPO-based di ff usion alignment, and provide theoretical and empirical analyses connecting this phenomenon to isotropic optimization in high-dimensional denoising space. 

• We propose APO, a Jacobian-induced anisotropic preference optimization frame-work together with an e ffi cient forward-only Hutchinson finite-di ff erence approxima-tion, enabling scalable geometry-aware di ff usion alignment without reference-model backpropagation. 3• Extensive experiments on SD1.5 and SDXL across multiple benchmarks demon-strate that APO consistently improves alignment quality, image realism, semantic preservation, and generation diversity while maintaining strong training e ffi ciency. 

2. Related Works 

Di ff usion Models for Visual Generation. Di ff usion models have emerged as the domi-nant paradigm for image synthesis due to their training stability and high sample quality [18, 19, 20, 21]. The introduction of latent di ff usion substantially reduced computational costs by operating the denoising process in a compressed representation space, enabling high-resolution text-to-image generation at scale [1]. This architecture catalyzed a series of large-scale open-weight models, including SDXL [2], SD3 [3], and the FLUX series [4], which form the backbone of most contemporary visual generation research. However, di ff usion models are often trained on large-scale general-purpose datasets with varying quality, which tends to introduce biases that deviate from human preferences. Therefore, aligning di ff usion models with human preferences is a highly necessary practice. 

Reward-Based Reinforcement Learning for Di ff usion. Early post-training e ff orts adopt supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on curated high-quality image-text pairs [22]. While eff ective, SFT inherently treats all samples equally and does not capture the comparative nature of human preference. Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) [23, 24] addresses this by first training a proxy reward model on human annotations and then optimizing the di ff usion policy with policy gradient methods such as DDPO [5] and DPOK [6], or by directly backpropagating di ff erentiable rewards as in DRaFT [8] and AlignProp [7]. These approaches, however, are prone to reward overoptimiza-tion. To bypass explicit reward learning, Di ff usion-DPO [10] adapts Direct Preference Optimization to di ff usion models, enabling o ffl ine fine-tuning directly on pairwise preference data without a separate reward model. Subsequently, a series of methods have been proposed to address the weaknesses of Di ff usion-DPO in various aspects, including sampling e ffi ciency, reward estimation, model adaptability, and reward di-versity [25, 12, 26, 13, 27, 15, 28, 29]. In addition, online reinforcement learning 4methods such as Flow-GRPO [9] and DanceGRPO [30] have become an increasingly popular paradigm. Some of these approaches seek to unify tasks spanning text-to-image, text-to-video, and image-to-video generation within a single framework, while oth-ers focus on improving computational e ffi ciency [31, 32, 33, 34, 35]. While existing alignment methods primarily modify the reward formulation, optimization strategy, or sampling e ffi ciency, our work instead revisits the geometric assumptions underlying the optimization metric itself. 

Manifold Learning for Di ff usion. The manifold hypothesis posits that high-dimensional natural data concentrate near a low-dimensional submanifold of the ambient space [16, 17]. In the context of di ff usion models, this connection is theoretically well-grounded: the score function is intimately related to the geometry of the data distribution and approximates the normal bundle of the data manifold [20, 36, 37, 38], a structural prior that has motivated a rich line of research connecting manifold learning with generative modeling [39, 40, 41, 38, 42]. Furthermore, manifold awareness has proven essential for improving inference-time quality, as demonstrated by CFG ++ [43], TCFG [44], and Be Tangential to Manifold [45]. However, in the domain of preference alignment for di ff usion models based on standard Euclidean di ff usion parameterizations, manifold learning theory has yet to be considered.

3.3. O ff -Manifold Drift in Di ff usion-DPO 

As discussed in Section 3.2, the training objective in Di ff usion-DPO compares the prediction errors of the winner and the loser samples using a uniform Euclidean metric, treating all reverse-trajectory update directions in RD equally. Under the man-ifold hypothesis [16, 17], however, natural data concentrate near a low-dimensional latent submanifold M ⊂ RD, and the ambient space locally decomposes into tangent directions TxM and normal directions NxM. These directions play fundamentally dif-ferent geometric roles. Reverse-trajectory displacements along tangent directions tend to preserve semantic consistency and remain close to the data manifold, whereas dis-placements along normal directions are more likely to move trajectories away from the 8manifold and degrade image quality. DPO-style di ff usion alignment methods measure all trajectory-update discrepancies isotropically under the same Euclidean geometry, without distinguishing geometry-preserving directions from geometry-sensitive ones. As a result, updates along geometry-sensitive manifold-normal directions are insu ffi ciently constrained, while manifold-aligned tangent directions become unnecessarily restricted for semantic adjustment. We refer to this geometric failure mode as off -manifold drift .We illustrate this phenomenon with a minimal example (Figure 1), where a DDPM pretrained on a unit circle embedded in R32 is fine-tuned. In this setup, the left semicircle corresponds to low-reward (loser) regions, while the right semicircle corresponds to high-reward (winner) regions. For each preference pair, we randomly sample one point from each semicircle and designate the point from the right semicircle as the winner. We compare DPO and its Euclidean-metric variants, all of which progressively shift probability mass toward the preferred region, yet exhibit noticeable o ff -manifold drift during training, producing large numbers of o ff -manifold samples ( red ). This observation motivates the central question of our work: can we design a geometry-aware anisotropic regularizer that adapts to the local structure of the di ff usion representation space? We provide a positive answer in the next section.

Single-probe stability. Although the Hutchinson estimator in Equation (19) is a stochas-tic approximation, a single probe direction ( nv = 1) is already su ffi cient in practice. The resulting optimization remains highly stable, and this behavior arises from the temporal coherence of di ff usion trajectories themselves. APO does not require recovering the full Jacobian structure at each timestep. The objective only depends on the dominant anisotropic direction induced by J⊤ 

> ref

δt. Along the di ff usion trajectory, nearby timesteps share highly correlated geometric structure and similar local manifold orientation. As a result, although a single probe may introduce instantaneous stochasticity, its projected direction repeatedly aligns with the same underlying anisotropic component across neighboring noise scales. Consequently, the stochastic errors introduced by individual probes tend to fluctuate inconsistently across timesteps, while the persistent geometric component accumulates coherently throughout optimization. This creates a natural temporal averaging e ff ect 16 along the di ff usion trajectory: unstable probe noise is progressively canceled, whereas stable manifold-aware directions are repeatedly reinforced. In practice, this reduces the variance to a low level, making nv = 1 already su ffi cient to produce stable optimization dynamics. Naturally, increasing the number of probe directions can further improve the accuracy and stability of the anisotropic estimate. However, the empirical gains quickly exhibit diminishing returns relative to the additional computational cost. Therefore, we retain the multi-probe formulation in Algorithm 1 for completeness, and provide a detailed ablation study on nv in Section 5.3.3.

Our experiments seek to answer three questions: 

• Does APO improve preference alignment quality and generation behavior across architectures and benchmarks? 

• Are the gains truly attributable to the proposed Jacobian-induced anisotropic geome-try? 

• Under what regimes is the geometric regularization most beneficial? subsection 5.2 presents the main quantitative and qualitative results. subsection 5.3 provides ablations and detailed analyses of the underlying geometric mechanism. 

5.1. Setup Models, Datasets, and Baselines. We conduct experiments on two widely used text-to-image backbones, Stable Di ff usion 1.5 (SD1.5) and Stable Di ff usion XL (SDXL) [2]. All models are fine-tuned on the Pick-a-Pic v2 dataset [46], which provides 851,293 training preference pairs over 58,960 distinct prompts after removing ties. We compare our method against the unmodified base models (SD1.5 and SDXL), supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on winner images [22], Di ff usion-DPO [10], InPO [13], and Di ff usion-SDPO [14]. Additionally, on SD1.5, we also compare against Di ff usion-KTO [12], and on SDXL, we additionally compare against LPO [15]. All the above baselines employ the uniform Euclidean treatment of prediction errors. Our experiments are evaluated on the HPSv2 [47], Parti-Prompts [48] and Pick-a-Picv2 [46] test sets, following standard protocols. 17 Evaluation. We assess alignment quality using three reward models that capture human preference: PickScore [46], HPS [47], and the LAION Aesthetic Predictor [49]. We further report CLIP score [50] for text-image alignment. Furthermore, we conducted a human evaluation on SDXL across four dimensions. We recruited 40 professional participants, each evaluating 50 di ff erent sets of images. Each set included the base model, DPO, and APO, with the order randomly shu ffl ed. For each set, participants rated images across four dimensions: text-content alignment, image realism, aesthetic quality, and overall preference. We also evaluated the training e ffi ciency on HPSv2 for SD1.5 and SDXL: we report the single-GPU (NVIDIA A100) training hours and peak GPU memory (GB) on SD1.5 and SDXL, along with a comparison against the standard DPO method. 

Implementation Details. We train APO on all models for 900 optimization steps with a global batch size of 2048. For SD1.5, we perform full fine-tuning. For SDXL, we adopt LoRA-based fine-tuning to reduce memory and computational cost, using rank 

r = 32, scaling factor α = 32, and dropout rate 0 .05. The text encoder remains frozen throughout training. Optimization is performed using AdamW with a cosine learning rate schedule. The learning rate follows the scaling rule 2000

6. Conclusion and Limitation 

Conclusion. In this paper, we presented APO, a geometry-aware preference optimiza-tion framework for di ff usion models. We revisit di ff usion preference alignment from the perspective of manifold geometry, and identify o ff -manifold drift as a fundamental failure mode induced by isotropic optimization in high-dimensional denoising space. To address this issue, APO constructs a Jacobian-induced anisotropic metric from the reference score field, selectively suppressing geometry-sensitive directions while pre-serving flexible exploration along manifold-aligned directions. Our results suggest that incorporating intrinsic geometric structure into preference optimization provides an eff ective and principled direction for robust di ff usion alignment. 

Limitation. APO assumes that the reference score field provides a meaningful local approximation of the underlying data manifold. When the preference data distribution itself contains substantial o ff -manifold corruption or systematically noisy annotations, the induced geometry may become less reliable and potentially bias the alignment process. This may limit the e ff ectiveness of APO in heavily distribution-shifted or weakly curated preference-learning scenarios. Extending APO toward more robust geometric estimation under noisy preference supervision remains an important direction for future work. 27 Table 4: Win Rate comparison on SDXL.

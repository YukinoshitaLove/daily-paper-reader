ABSTRACT 

Diffusion models learn semantic representations while generating images. In the Decoupled Diffusion Transformer (DDT), a condition encoder provides features that guide a velocity decoder in denoising. To enable effective denoising at all noise levels, these features must capture both high-level abstract structures and low-level details. However, skip/residual connections in the encoder allow shal-low features to bypass successive transformations, which may limit progressive abstraction, or at least make it difficult to disentangle different levels of abstrac-tion. We propose DDT-RFE, which removes the residual connections around the Self-Attention and MLP operations in each encoder block while maintaining sta-ble training. To retain the information that abstraction discards but that the decoder still needs, we fuse the input patch embedding with intermediate and final encoder features to form the encoder output. The decoder thus has access to information from multiple encoder depths, while each encoder block is able to learn more ab-stract representations. DDT-RFE achieves overall improvements over DDT across visual understanding tasks, including image classification, semantic segmentation, object discovery, and semantic correspondence, while using fewer encoder blocks. It also achieves a lower FID for image generation on ImageNet. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Diffusion transformers (Peebles & Xie, 2023) have emerged as a dominant backbone for image gen-eration, achieving remarkable fidelity and diversity across a wide range of visual domains. Beyond synthesis, their intermediate activations encode semantic information that supports downstream vi-sual recognition and dense prediction tasks (Stracke et al., 2025; Gan et al., 2025; Helbling et al., 2025; Xiang et al., 2023). Crucially, representation learning and image synthesis are not inde-pendent processes: in standard diffusion transformers, semantic abstraction and fine-grained signal reconstruction are implicitly coupled within the same denoising network. Recent work has begun to explicitly decouple these two roles (Yu et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2024). Decoupled Diffusion Transformer (DDT) (Wang et al., 2026), for example, introduces a “condition encoder” for learning representations and a “velocity decoder” for signal reconstruction. The condition encoder retains the standard diffusion transformer block design and transforms noisy inputs into representations that provide the decoder with information useful for reconstruction, including abstract structures and low-level image details. However, how the encoder develops abstract features and combines features at different levels affect the quality of the representations that are provided to the decoder. In DDT, each encoder block trans-forms its input and integrates the resulting features with the input through residual connections (He et al., 2016). Stacking blocks allows features to undergo successive transformations, which sup-ports abstract representations. At the same time, residual connections carry information from earlier blocks into the inputs of later transformations. Although this can retain information useful for recon-struction, we hypothesize that the repeated accumulation of shallow features may hinder abstraction from the noisy input (Ji et al., 2026a; Zhang et al., 2026). As shown in Fig. 1, object structure re-mains difficult to distinguish in the visualized DDT encoder features, even in deeper blocks. This raises a central question: can the encoder develop more abstract features through successive trans-formations, while combining them with earlier features at its output, to provide the decoder with information at multiple levels of abstraction?

Our main contributions are as follows: • We propose DDT-RFE, which combines a residual-free condition encoder, a new initializa-tion scheme, and feature fusion to improve representation learning and image generation. • Qualitatively, our feature visualizations show clearer object structure and cleaner back-grounds in the representations learned by DDT-RFE than in those learned by DDT. • Quantitatively, DDT-RFE achieves overall improvements over DDT on image classifica-tion, semantic segmentation, object discovery, and semantic correspondence with fewer encoder blocks. It also achieves a lower FID for image generation on ImageNet. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Diffusion transformer architectures. DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) establishes a scalable backbone for latent diffusion built from a stack of transformer blocks. U-DiT (Tian et al., 2024) adds long skip connections that carry shallow features to deeper layers. Other designs separate the learning of semantic representations from reconstruction. In pixel-space diffusion, PixelDiT (Yu et al., 2026) models global semantics at the patch level in an encoder and refines details at the pixel level in a 2decoder. DDT (Wang et al., 2026) uses a condition encoder to learn representations that condition a velocity decoder for signal reconstruction. We build on the condition encoder of DDT so that the representations learned in the encoder can be studied in isolation from the decoder. 

Representation learning with diffusion models. Diffusion denoising has been studied as a self-supervised objective for learning visual representations that transfer effectively to downstream tasks. Through pretraining on image generation, Xiang et al. (2023) demonstrate that diffusion models learn linearly separable intermediate representations and achieve image classification performance comparable to that of contemporary contrastive methods. For diffusion transformers, DiTF (Gan et al., 2025) investigates the capabilities of DiTs for accurate dense correspondence and proposes a training-free method to extract semantically discriminative features from DiTs, achieving superior performance across multiple benchmarks. Furthermore, CleanDIFT (Stracke et al., 2025) introduces a lightweight, unsupervised fine-tuning method that enables diffusion backbones to provide high-quality, noise-free semantic features for downstream visual tasks. Our work examines how the con-dition encoder shapes representations, with a focus on progressive abstraction, and how information that may be discarded during this process can remain accessible to the decoder for reconstruction. 

Residual connections in transformers. Residual connections affect both optimization and the learned representations. Ji et al. (2025) link the training difficulties caused by removing attention shortcuts to poor network conditioning in self-attention and analyze how residual pathways improve network conditioning. To address this optimization challenge, subsequent work develops initializa-tion and optimization strategies that enable effective training of residual-free transformers (Ji et al., 2026a;b). Beyond optimization, residual pathways allow information from earlier blocks to persist in deeper representations (Raghu et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2026). Ji et al. (2026a) further report that residual-free vision transformers learn more abstract representations and achieve improved perfor-mance on dense prediction tasks. An additional consideration in our setting is how to include more abstract features in the representations provided to the decoder while retaining other information useful for reconstruction. Our work examines this question in DDT by removing residual connec-tions within the condition encoder and aggregating features from different depths at its encoder output.

5 EXPERIMENTS 

We evaluate our methods in training diffusion transformers from scratch on the ImageNet-1k dataset at a resolution of 256 × 256 and patch size of 2 for 80 epochs, using a total training batch size of 1024. Following Zhou et al. (2026), we use the Muon optimizer with a constant learning rate of 0.0002 and a momentum of 0.95 throughout training. We set the gradient clipping threshold to 1, weight decay to 0, and the exponential moving average (EMA) decay rate to 0.9999. We use the off-the-shelf VAE-ft-EMA from Hugging Face 2, with a downsampling factor of 8. For sampling, we use the Euler solver with 250 steps. We train both DDT and DDT-RFE in both Large (L) and Extra Large (XL) configurations, with 458M and 675M parameters, respectively. For DDT-RFE, we set ρ = 0 .1 for the L configuration and ρ = 0 for the XL configuration. The original DDT

To assess how our architectural design affects the representations learned by diffusion models, we evaluate representations on four visual tasks: image classification, object discovery, semantic corre-spondence and semantic segmentation. For all evaluations, we extract features using conditioning on the null class. 5.1.1 IMAGE CLASSIFICATION 

Setting. We assess the representations of the frozen models by classification on ImageNet-1k. Given a noised latent xt, we extract token features from an encoder block and apply global av-erage pooling to obtain an image-level representation. The same representation is used for both evaluations below. First, following the DINO protocol (Caron et al., 2021), we evaluate k-nearest-neighbor (kNN) top-1 accuracy for every encoder block at noise levels t ∈ { 0.1, 0.3, 0.5}, using the same timestep t to construct the noisy input and to condition the network. Second, following DDAE (Xiang et al., 2023), we evaluate linear separability by training a classification head consist-ing of BatchNorm and a linear layer for 30 epochs. For each model we select the encoder block and noise level with the best validation accuracy and report top-1 and top-5 accuracy, and we additionally probe the encoder output that conditions the decoder with the same procedure. We compare DiT-L, DDT-L in its best configuration (20en4de), and DDT-RFE-L (12en12de). Because DDT-RFE uses fewer encoder blocks than the best DDT configuration, we also train DDT-L with the same encoder depth as ours (12en12de) for a fair comparison.

We evaluate both single representation and multiscale representations from different blocks. Results and analysis. Table 4 reports mIoU on the three datasets. On VOC2012, DDT-RFE out-performs DDT by about 6 mIoU at both L and XL scales, with single-block and with multi-scale features. We attribute this to the greater scene complexity of these datasets, where multi-scale information is critical. The residual connections of DDT mix representations implicitly across depth, so a single block already carries multi-level information. In the residual-free encoder, ab-straction arises within the hierarchy and multi-scale context is recovered by aggregation. 6 LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK 

Due to limited computational resources, our experiments focus on DDT models trained on ImageNet at 256 × 256 resolution for 80 epochs. We leave experiments with longer training schedules, higher resolutions (e.g., 512 × 512 ), and other diffusion architectures to future work to assess the broader applicability of our approach. 

7 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we studied how residual connections in the condition encoder shape the representations learned by diffusion transformers, with a focus on how the encoder learns abstract features. We introduced DDT-RFE, which combines a residual-free encoder with feature aggregation at its output. This design allows successive encoder blocks to transform features without identity shortcuts, while keeping information from different encoder depths accessible to the decoder. With the proposed initialization, DDT-RFE achieves overall improvements over DDT across four visual understanding tasks using fewer encoder blocks. It also achieves a lower FID for image generation on ImageNet under the same training budget. Our ablations further show the importance of both initialization and output feature aggregation. Together, these results suggest that separating feature transformation within the encoder from aggregation at its output can benefit both representation learning and image generation.

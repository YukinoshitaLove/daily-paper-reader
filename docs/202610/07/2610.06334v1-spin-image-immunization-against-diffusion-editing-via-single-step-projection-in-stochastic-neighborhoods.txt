## ABSTRACT 

Diffusion models have greatly advanced instruction-guided image editing, while also raising concerns about unauthorized image manipulation. Image immuniza-tion addresses this risk by adding imperceptible perturbations to an input image to disrupt subsequent edits. Since editing requests are unknown at image release, protection should remain effective beyond the instruction used to construct the perturbation. Existing immunization methods either require costly full-trajectory backpropagation or use intermediate objectives whose effects may be weakened by subsequent denoising. Meanwhile, a single inference path provides limited feedback about alternative denoising continuations. To address these challenges, we propose SPIN, a framework for image immunization via one-step projection over local stochastic trajectory neighborhoods. Starting from an early denoising state, SPIN generates stochastic neighboring states under the same instruction and predicts their clean latents through one-step projection without full unrolling. We then optimize a bounded input perturbation to maximize the average devia-tion of these predictions from a clean-edit reference, encouraging the perturbation to disrupt multiple possible editing outcomes. Experiments on two image editors demonstrate substantial gains in protection performance, with SPIN outperform-ing compared methods across all six metrics under seen instructions and in the more challenging unseen instruction setting. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Instruction-guided diffusion models have made it possible to modify a source image using natural-language instructions while preserving content unrelated to the requested changes (Brooks et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024). Publicly available image editing models have substantially lowered the barriers to personalized image manipulation. However, the same accessibility also makes pub-licly released images vulnerable to unauthorized editing, which may compromise personal privacy and reputation or facilitate misleading content. Deepfake detection (Rossler et al., 2019) and AI-generated image detection (Ojha et al., 2023) can identify manipulated content, but operate only after such content has been produced. This motivates proactive protection that is embedded imperceptibly in images before release. Image immunization protects source images by adding imperceptible perturbations that disrupt subsequent unauthorized edits. A direct approach optimizes perturbations against the final edit-ing output, but backpropagation through the denoising trajectory incurs substantial computational cost (Salman et al., 2023). More efficient alternatives construct surrogate objectives from denoising predictions or intermediate representations (Liang et al., 2023; Liang & Wu, 2023). Such objectives can target encoder representations (Xue et al., 2024), instruction-related cross-attention (Lo et al., 2024), or early denoising states (Choi et al., 2025), providing protection signals through shorter gradient paths. 1arXiv:2610.06334v1 [cs.CV] 5 Oct 2026 Nevertheless, deviations induced by intermediate representations may be attenuated by subsequent denoising, weakening their effect on the final edit. Protection under a fixed editing instruction may also depend on the specific sampling trajectory. This dependence becomes particularly relevant when future editing requests are unknown, as a single trajectory offers limited feedback about other possible continuations. The challenge is therefore to construct an effective optimization signal that reflects the perturbation’s impact on editing outcomes across multiple possible continuations, en-abling protection beyond the original editing instruction. We address this challenge by proposing SPIN (Single-step Projection in Stochastic Neighborhoods), an image-immunization framework that uses predicted editing outcomes to guide protective pertur-bations. Single-step projection predicts a clean latent from an early denoising state. The discrepancy between this prediction and a fixed clean-edit reference provides an effective surrogate for optimiz-ing perturbations that disrupt the final editing output. To extend this signal beyond a single inference path, SPIN generates neighboring candidate states through stochastic transitions from a shared early state. We then optimize a bounded input perturbation to maximize the average discrepancy between their projected outcomes and the reference, reducing dependence on a single construction path and promoting generalization to unseen editing instructions. Experiments on SD3 and InstructPix2Pix show that SPIN outperforms all baselines across six met-rics under both seen and unseen editing instructions. Immunization success rates reach 90% and 94% under seen instructions (85% and 91% under unseen instructions), consistently exceeding those of competing methods. Ablations demonstrate that single-step projection provides an effective pro-tection signal, while stochastic neighborhood sampling further improves generalization over single-candidate projection. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose SPIN, an image-immunization framework that uses single-step projection to guide perturbation optimization with predicted editing outcomes, providing an effective signal for disrupting the final edit. • We introduce stochastic neighborhood optimization to construct a single perturbation that targets multiple possible continuations of the editing process. By driving their projected outcomes away from the clean-edit reference, this strategy promotes disruption across dif-ferent inference paths and improves generalization to unseen editing instructions. • Experiments under standard editing pipelines show that SPIN consistently outperforms the evaluated baselines in immunization success rate under both seen and unseen instructions, across flow-matching and noise-prediction diffusion editors. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 INSTRUCTION -G UIDED DIFFUSION IMAGE EDITING 

Diffusion models support image editing by combining a generative prior with a source image and user-specified control. SDEdit (Meng et al., 2022) perturbs and denoises an input through a stochas-tic differential equation to balance faithfulness and realism. Later methods introduced more ex-plicit semantic control. Prompt-to-Prompt (Hertz et al., 2023) manipulates cross-attention maps to preserve spatial layout while changing textual content, whereas Imagic (Kawar et al., 2023) optimizes a text embedding and fine-tunes the model for semantic edits on a single image. In-structPix2Pix (Brooks et al., 2023) establishes a supervised learning paradigm for directly following natural-language editing instructions without per-image inversion or fine-tuning. UltraEdit (Zhao et al., 2024) advances this paradigm through large-scale editing supervision with diverse instruc-tions, real-image anchors, and region annotations, supporting the adaptation of pretrained generative models, including Stable Diffusion 3 (Esser et al., 2024), to instruction-guided image editing. These advances improve the capability and accessibility of image editing, while also exposing publicly released images to increasingly diverse unauthorized transformations. 2.2 ADVERSARIAL EXAMPLES 

Norm-bounded adversarial perturbations can alter model predictions (Goodfellow et al., 2015), and projected-gradient methods (Madry et al., 2018) formulate their construction as constrained opti-2mization. To improve cross-model transferability, MI-FGSM (Dong et al., 2018) stabilizes updates through momentum, while VMI-FGSM (Wang & He, 2021) further refines them using neighborhood gradient information. EOT (Athalye et al., 2018) averages attack objectives over input transforma-tions to improve transformation robustness, whereas universal adversarial perturbations generalize across images (Moosavi-Dezfooli et al., 2017). SPIN targets the generation trajectories of diffu-sion editors, optimizing a bounded input perturbation over local stochastic continuations to disrupt generation and drive final editing outputs away from their clean counterparts. 2.3 IMAGE IMMUNIZATION AGAINST DIFFUSION EDITING 

Image immunization methods mainly differ in the optimization signal used to construct protective perturbations. PhotoGuard exposes the central trade-off through an efficient encoder-targeting objec-tive (PhotoGuard-E) and a more output-related but expensive full-diffusion objective (PhotoGuard-D) (Salman et al., 2023). AdvDM optimizes the diffusion denoising loss, while MIST combines semantic and textural objectives to disrupt the generation process at multiple representation lev-els (Liang et al., 2023; Liang & Wu, 2023). Subsequent methods shorten the gradient path by target-ing specific internal components: SDS (Xue et al., 2024) applies score distillation at the vulnerable VAE bottleneck, EditShield (Chen et al., 2024) shifts instruction-conditioned latent representations, and SA (Lo et al., 2024) disrupts prompt-relevant cross-attention features. DiffusionGuard (Choi et al., 2025) places its objective at early denoising stages. Other approaches improve complemen-tary aspects of protection: DCT-Shield (Bala et al., 2025) optimizes frequency-domain perturbations for visual fidelity and JPEG resistance, whereas SIFM (Dong et al., 2025) combines intermediate-feature manipulation with semantic and perceptual criteria for evaluating edit failure. Despite their different targets, these methods either require costly differentiation through the diffusion trajectory or rely on internal surrogates whose deviations may be attenuated during subsequent denoising. 

## 3 METHOD 

We present an image-immunization framework, termed Single-step Projection in Stochastic Neigh-borhoods (SPIN), for protecting images against unauthorized editing. To efficiently obtain a protec-tion signal that reflects the perturbation’s effect on the predicted edit, we use single-step projection to estimate the clean latent from an intermediate denoising state. We further optimize the pertur-bation over a local stochastic neighborhood to reduce dependence on a single inference path and promote generalization across editing instructions. 3.1 PRELIMINARIES AND PROBLEM FORMULATION

## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 SETUP AND EVALUATION PROTOCOL 

Benchmark Datasets. We construct a benchmark of 500 samples from Pico-Banana, covering landscapes, artworks, multi-object scenes, real-world photographs, news imagery, public figures, and indoor scenes. For text-conditioned protection methods, perturbations are optimized using the original prompt associated with each image. Each sample is evaluated with its original prompt and five semantically disjoint editing instructions, resulting in 3,000 image-instruction pairs. Additional dataset and instruction details are provided in Appendix A.1. 

Editing Models and Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate InstructPix2Pix and Stable Diffusion 3 (SD3) as denoising-diffusion and flow-matching editors, respectively. We compare edits of clean and protected inputs using PSNR (Wang & Bovik, 2009), SSIM (Wang et al., 2004), FSIM (Zhang et al., 2011), VIFP (Sheikh & Bovik, 2006), and LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018). Lower values of the first four and higher LPIPS indicate stronger protection; the directions reverse for perturbation imperceptibility. Following SIFM (Dong et al., 2025), we report Immunization Success Rate (ISR): the fraction of protected edits with instruction failure or substantial perceptual degradation, judged by consensus of Kimi K2.5 (Team et al., 2026) and Qwen3-VL (Bai et al., 2025). Higher ISR indicates stronger protection. See Appendices A.2 and D for evaluation details. 

Implementation Details. For each image, we optimize an additive perturbation δ under ∥δ∥∞ ≤

8/255 while keeping the model parameters frozen. Starting from δ = 0 , we perform 20 PGD steps with a step size of 1/255 , projecting the perturbation and clipping the protected image after each step. We use branch index j = 1 for both editors and set τ = 2 for InstructPix2Pix. Sampling noise is fixed during optimization. Evaluation uses unseen seeds, with clean and protected inputs sharing the same editing instruction and sampling noise. Further implementation details are provided in Appendix A.3. 4.2 MAIN RESULTS

## 5 CONCLUSION 

We introduced SPIN to protect images against unauthorized editing by jointly considering the pre-dicted outcomes of multiple local editing trajectories. This formulation makes output-oriented pro-tection tractable without differentiating through the full denoising process. It also allows a protective perturbation to account for alternative continuations of an edit, rather than relying on a single con-struction path. ## A ADDITIONAL EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS 

A.1 BENCHMARK CONSTRUCTION 

Our benchmark contains 500 samples drawn from Pico-Banana. The selected images cover land-scapes, artworks, multi-object scenes, real-world photographs, news imagery, portraits of public figures, and indoor scenes. For protection methods that require textual conditioning, the original prompt associated with each image is used during perturbation optimization. At evaluation time, each image is paired with its original prompt and five additional editing in-structions that are semantically disjoint from one another. The additional instructions are used only for evaluation and are not exposed during perturbation optimization. This separation tests whether the protection transfers to editing requests that differ from the optimization prompt. The resulting benchmark contains 3000 image–instruction pairs. A.2 EDITING MODELS AND METRIC COMPUTATION 

We evaluate protection efficacy using InstructPix2Pix and Stable Diffusion 3 (SD3), representing denoising-diffusion and flow-matching image editors, respectively. All protection methods are eval-uated using the same editor configurations.

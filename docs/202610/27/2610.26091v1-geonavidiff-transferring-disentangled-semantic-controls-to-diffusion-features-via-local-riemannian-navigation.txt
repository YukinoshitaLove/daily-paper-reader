Abstract 

Disentangled real-image manipulation requires factor-specific semantic control while preserving image fidelity and non-target content. StyleSpace S provides interpretable controls but is limited by GAN inversion, whereas diffusion models offer stronger image realization without an explicit factorized control basis. We introduce GeoNaviDiff, an unsupervised cross-space framework that transfers disentangled StyleSpace interventions to local Riemannian trajectories in diffusion h-space. GeoNaviDiff constructs reconstruction-decoupled su-pervision by extracting a spherical Log-map tangent from a matched StyleGAN reconstruction–edit pair and parallel-transporting it to the real-image diffusion state. A shared, factor-conditioned Navigator predicts this tangent at infer-ence, while norm-preserving geodesic realization and source-anchored rollout stabilize the reverse process without edited references or factor-specific models. Experiments across mul-tiple datasets show that GeoNaviDiff outperforms the com-pared methods in fidelity, factor selectivity, edit locality, and trajectory smoothness. 

1 Introduction 

Disentangled Representation Learning (DRL) (Bengio, Courville, and Vincent 2013) seeks to factorize complex ob-servations into semantically meaningful variables, providing a foundation for interpretable and controllable generation. For representation manipulation, its value lies in selective in-tervention: a target factor, such as shape, color, or expression, should change while identity, structure, and other non-target factors remain stable. This factor-specific controllability is essential for fine-grained editing, yet it depends on both an interpretable representation and a generative model capable of faithfully realizing the prescribed change. Early DRL-based manipulation methods primarily re-lied on the structured latent spaces of Variational Au-toencoders (VAEs) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) (Härkönen et al. 2020; Karras, Laine, and Aila 2019). In particular, StyleGAN exposes increasingly fine-grained semantic controls through its latent hierarchy, with channel-level coordinates in StyleSpace S supporting lo-calized and interpretable interventions (Karras et al. 2020; Wu, Lischinski, and Shechtman 2021). However, GAN-based real-image manipulation remains constrained by inversion fi-delity and the fixed generator prior (Tov et al. 2021; Bobkov DDIM Inv + Bottleneck DDIM Inv + Bottleneck

Diffusion models of-fer a stronger generative prior and high-quality image recon-struction, but their internal representations do not explicitly expose a factor-indexed semantic basis. This creates a fun-damental tension: structured GAN representations provide factor-specific semantic controls, whereas diffusion priors favor high-fidelity reconstruction and synthesis. To exploit the diffusion prior despite this missing factor-ized control space, existing conditional diffusion manipula-tion methods introduce semantic controls externally through text, attribute labels, or auxiliary guidance. Their control-lable granularity, however, remains tied to predefined con-ditions and may cause unintended changes when those con-ditions are coarse or misaligned (Parihar et al. 2024; Hu et al. 2023; Sun et al. 2026). An intrinsic alternative is to manipulate the U-Net bottleneck feature space, known as 

h-space, which encodes rich high-level semantics without such external controls (Kwon, Jeong, and Uh 2022; Jeong, Kwon, and Uh 2024). Yet h-space does not expose these semantics as explicit, reusable control coordinates. There-fore, prior work trains a separate shift predictor for each prescribed semantic attribute and injects its output as an arXiv:2610.26091v1 [cs.CV] 19 Oct 2026 additive Euclidean residual (Kwon, Jeong, and Uh 2022). This direction-specific design prevents reuse across factors and ignores the geometry and feature statistics of h-space. As illustrated in Figure 1, its straight ambient path departs from the spherical shell of normalized bottleneck states and changes the bottleneck norm, disrupting its correlation with skip-feature statistics and producing visible artifacts (Jeong, Kwon, and Uh 2024). Remaining on the hyperspherical shell alone is insufficient: an arbitrary geodesic may be geometri-cally valid while reaching a semantically incorrect endpoint. Therefore, stable disentangled manipulation requires a local tangent direction conditioned on an explicit semantic factor and adapted to the source image and diffusion timestep. Motivated by this insight, we propose GeoNaviDiff , an un-supervised DRL framework for cross-space semantic trans-fer via Geo desic Navi gation in Diff usion h-space. Rather than disentangling diffusion h-space from scratch, GeoNa-viDiff transfers unsupervised S-space controls into it, en-abling high-fidelity manipulation of individual semantic fac-tors while preserving non-target content. A shared cross-space Navigator conditions on the S-space displacement, source bottleneck state, and timestep to predict the corre-sponding local tangent direction in h-space. Geometry-aware learning and source-anchored inference then realize this pre-diction as a stable manifold-preserving diffusion trajectory without requiring an edited reference or a separate Navigator for each factor at inference. Our main contributions are: 1. We introduce a cross-space paradigm that maps inter-pretable S-space directions to stable semantic trajectories along the local hyperspherical geometry of diffusion h-space. This paradigm decouples semantic representation from image realization, with S-space defining the seman-tics and the diffusion prior realizing them beyond GAN reconstruction fidelity. 2. We develop a local Riemannian navigation mecha-nism with reconstruction-decoupled tangent supervi-sion, norm-preserving geodesic realization, and source-anchored inference. This design aligns the transferred se-mantic direction with the real-image diffusion state while suppressing radial drift and cumulative rollout error. 3. We conduct extensive evaluations across multiple datasets and diverse object categories. The results demonstrate that GeoNaviDiff generalizes robustly across object cat-egories and achieves state-of-the-art performance across all reported metrics of image fidelity, representation dis-entanglement, edit locality, and trajectory smoothness. 

2 Related Work 

2.1 Disentanglement in VAEs and GANs 

Early disentangled representation learning primarily relied on variational autoencoders (VAEs), which separate genera-tive factors by regularizing the latent distribution or encour-aging factorized representations (Burgess et al. 2018; Kim and Mnih 2018; Rolinek, Zietlow, and Martius 2019). GAN-based approaches subsequently showed that interpretable se-mantic directions can be identified in pretrained generators through principal components, closed-form factorization, or unsupervised direction discovery (Härkönen et al. 2020; Shen and Zhou 2021; Voynov and Babenko 2020). Mov-ing beyond the dense directions of W and W+, StyleSpace Analysis exposed the channel-wise modulation coefficients of StyleGAN and showed that selected coordinates provide localized controls with stronger disentanglement and com-pleteness under its evaluation protocols (Karras et al. 2020; Wu, Lischinski, and Shechtman 2021). Fantastic Style Chan-nels further selects representative and diverse channel di-rections from their generated image changes without using attribute labels (Simsar et al. 2023). These advances provide fine-grained semantic controls, but realizing them on real images remains constrained by GAN inversion and the fixed generator prior (Xia et al. 2023; Tov et al. 2021). 

2.2 Disentanglement–Fidelity Trade-off 

The tension between semantic editability and image fidelity is particularly evident in real-image manipulation. In Style-GAN, latent codes that remain close to the native distribution are generally more editable, whereas representations with greater freedom can reconstruct finer details at the cost of less stable manipulation; e4e, CLCAE, and SFE address different aspects of this trade-off through editability-aware inversion, cross-space guidance, or feature editing (Tov et al. 2021; Liu, Song, and Chen 2023; Bobkov et al. 2024). Diffusion-based DRL methods approach the complementary side of the prob-lem by learning factorized representations while retaining the generative capacity of diffusion models: InfoDiffusion max-imizes information in an auxiliary semantic latent variable, DisDiff learns factor-conditioned representations without su-pervision, and EncDiff uses cross-attention as an inductive bias for factor separation (Wang et al. 2023; Yang et al. 2023, 2024). These methods require training or modifying the dif-fusion model to learn a disentangled representation, rather than reusing an established semantic basis with a frozen de-noiser. Inspired by these complementary advances, GeoNa-viDiff uses S-space to provide factor-specific semantic con-trols and the high-dimensional h-space of a pretrained U-Net denoiser as the realization space for their semantic shifts. This cross-space formulation combines the disentanglement of S with diffusion reconstruction fidelity without explicit conditioning or predefined h-space directions. 

2.3 Semantic Manipulation in Diffusion Models 

Diffusion models can be controlled through text, attribute labels, or auxiliary guidance, but the resulting editing gran-ularity remains tied to the conditioning interface and may cause unintended changes when the condition is coarse or misaligned (Parihar et al. 2024; Hu et al. 2023; Sun et al. 2026). Intrinsic feature manipulation provides an alterna-tive: Asyrp identifies the U-Net bottleneck h-space as se-mantically editable and learns a direction-specific predic-tor whose output is injected as an additive Euclidean resid-ual (Kwon, Jeong, and Uh 2022), while subsequent work dis-covers semantic directions across diffusion timesteps without attribute supervision (Park et al. 2023). InjectFusion further demonstrates that direct replacement or addition can dis-rupt bottleneck feature statistics, and improves content in-jection through norm-matched spherical interpolation and latent calibration (Jeong, Kwon, and Uh 2024). These ap-proaches establish the editability of diffusion features and the importance of preserving their geometry, but do not provide a shared factor-conditioned mechanism that maps an exter-nal disentangled control to a source- and timestep-adaptive tangent trajectory. GeoNaviDiff addresses this gap by learn-ing a shared Navigator from S-space displacements to local Riemannian navigation in diffusion h-space.

4.1 Overview 

As shown in Figure 2, GeoNaviDiff transfers unsupervised, factor-specific S-space controls into diffusion h-space to en-able high-fidelity, disentangled manipulation of real images. Given a source image and an intervention ∆s, the StyleGAN branch constructs a matched reconstruction–edited reference pair, and a frozen U-Net extracts bottleneck states for the source and both references under shared noising. The Rie-mannian displacement between the reference states defines a semantic tangent, which is parallel-transported to the real-image source state to form the Navigator target. 4.2 Disentangled Semantic Basis in S-space 

GeoNaviDiff uses StyleGAN2’s StyleSpace S as its fac-torized semantic basis. Given an inverted code w+ =

{wℓ}Lℓ=1 , the layer-wise affine transforms yield s =(A1(w1), . . . , A L(wL)) ∈ S = QLℓ=1 RCℓ , where each co-ordinate modulates a synthesis channel (Karras et al. 2020; Wu, Lischinski, and Shechtman 2021). Selected coordinates provide localized controls; the resulting displacement ∆s

both conditions the Navigator and generates the matched reconstruction–edit pair used for tangent supervision. Since S-space contains thousands of channels with redun-dant effects, we adopt the unsupervised Fantastic Style Chan-nels method (Simsar et al. 2023) to discover representative and diverse candidate directions. It evaluates axis-aligned channel perturbations through their generated image changes and groups channels with similar effects into candidate clus-ters. As shown in Figure 2(a), a pretrained vision-language model (VLM) filters these clusters by inspecting their traver-sal sequences to retain semantically meaningful directions; the same filtering can instead be performed manually. Let 

I = {ik}Kk=1 denote the retained Fantastic Style Channels indices and dk = eik the corresponding coordinate basis vector. During Navigator training, a sampled direction and strength specify ∆s = αd k and s′ = s + ∆ s, where the sign and magnitude of α determine the traversal direction and extent, respectively. For a real image x, a pretrained and frozen e4e inver-sion encoder (Tov et al. 2021) produces w+, and the fixed affine transforms above yield s. The frozen StyleGAN gen-erator then produces the matched pair X(R) = G(s) and 

X(V ) = G(s + αd k). This pair shares the same inverted code and generator pathway and differs only in the pre-scribed S-space intervention. It therefore externalizes the factor-specific change encoded by ∆s as a matched visual reference for constructing the diffusion-space navigation tar-get in Sec. 4.3. In GeoNaviDiff, S thus supplies both the factor-indexed control descriptor and its visual supervision.

5 Experiments 

5.1 Experimental Setup 

Datasets. We evaluate GeoNaviDiff on FFHQ (Karras, Laine, and Aila 2019), CelebA-HQ (Karras et al. 2017), AFHQ-v2 (Choi et al. 2020), and Stanford Cars (Krause et al. 2013), spanning human faces, animal faces, and vehicles. Details on preprocessing, data splits, hardware, and compu-tational cost are provided in Supplementary Appendix G. 

Metrics. For all four datasets, we report FID (Heusel et al. 2017) for distributional fidelity, Perceptual Path Length (PPL) (Karras, Laine, and Aila 2019) for perceptual smooth-ness along semantic traversals, OFlow (Jin et al. 2024) for spatial editing leakage, and the disentanglement component of DCI (Eastwood and Williams 2018) to measure how selec-tively individual representation dimensions encode semantic factors. The PPL computation protocol and dataset-specific DCI factor definitions are provided in Supplementary Ap-pendix A. Lower FID, PPL, and OFlow are better, whereas higher DCI is better. 

5.2 Comparison with Existing Methods 

Table 1 compares GeoNaviDiff with representative VAE-, GAN-, and diffusion-based methods, and Figure 3 provides a visual comparison on smile manipulation. GeoNaviDiff achieves the best results on all reported metrics across the evaluated datasets: lower FID and PPL indicate better im-age fidelity and smoother semantic traversals, while lower OFlow and higher DCI show reduced non-target leakage and stronger factor-wise disentanglement. The visual comparison is consistent with these trends, where GeoNaviDiff produces more monotonic semantic changes while better preserving identity and unrelated facial attributes. These improvements support our central design: S supplies factor-selective se-mantic controls, while diffusion h-space provides the high-fidelity realization space; Riemannian navigation and source-anchored inference further reduce feature-geometry mis-match and accumulated non-target drift. More qualitative comparisons are provided in Supplementary Appendix E.

5.4 Ablation Study 

Figure 5 presents a stepwise ablation using matched source images and semantic strengths. All GeoNaviDiff variants share the same Navigator architecture and inference settings. Starting from Asyrp’s direction-specific Euclidean residual, the variants successively introduce the S-conditioned Nav-igator, Riemannian realization, and source-anchored roll-out. The progression shows that the Navigator improves target-attribute consistency, Riemannian realization better preserves identity and non-target appearance, and source an-choring yields the most stable trajectories at stronger edits by suppressing cumulative drift.The corresponding quantitative ablation results are reported in Supplementary Appendix D. 

6 Conclusion 

We presented GeoNaviDiff, an unsupervised cross-space framework that maps interpretable S-space directions to lo-cal Riemannian trajectories in diffusion h-space, decoupling semantic representation from image realization. Across mul-tiple datasets, GeoNaviDiff improves representation disen-tanglement, image fidelity, and trajectory smoothness while preserving non-target content, supporting semantic-basis transfer as an alternative to disentangling diffusion features from scratch.

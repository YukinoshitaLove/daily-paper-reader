ABSTRACT
Robust demoiréing of screen-recaptured images remains challenging: moiré varies
in frequency, color, and spatial support across display-camera pairs and capture con-
ditions. Dedicated restoration networks trained on finite paired data struggle with
this diversity, especially when low-frequency color banding and high-frequency
stripes co-occur. We present DemoiréEdit, a content-preserving editing frame-
work that adapts the natural-image prior of a pretrained flow-matching editor to
demoiréing. First, an endpoint-supervised training objective supplements velocity
matching with pixel, perceptual, and frequency-domain restoration losses. Second,
a dual-scale condition-shift strategy performs global correction at low resolution,
then refines the image at full resolution by shifting the denoising condition from
the first-stage output to the original input for detail recovery. We also introduce
DailyMoiré130, a benchmark of challenging moiré in everyday screen photography,
comprising 130 smartphone captures across diverse displays with low-frequency
color banding, fine high-frequency stripes, and mixed-frequency interference. On
the challenging DailyMoiré130 benchmark, our method achieves the lowest resid-
ual moiré area and receives 71.3% overall-quality preference in a blind Best-of-7
study with 31 participants. We also evaluate on paired FHDMi/UHDM, reducing
residual moiré confidence by 47.1%/9.3% and increasing MUSIQ by 4.3%/2.0%
over the best baseline for each metric.

INTRODUCTION
Moiré artifacts arise from the spatial-frequency interference between a display’s pixel array and a
camera’s sensor grid during screen recapture (Sidorov & Kokaram, 2002; Sun et al., 2018; Wang
et al., 2023a). Unlike noise or blur with a statistically stable spectrum, moiré has no fixed frequency
1
arXiv:2610.26644v1  [cs.CV]  19 Oct 2026


signature: low-frequency color banding and high-frequency stripes can co-occur within the same
image (Figs. 1 and 2). Their mixture varies with viewing angle, device pairing, and local scene
content (Sun et al., 2018; Yuan et al., 2019), making the degradation difficult to characterize with a
single spectral model.
Existing approaches train dedicated convolutional or transformer networks from scratch on dataset-
specific moiré pairs (Sun et al., 2018; He et al., 2019; Zheng et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2020; He et al.,
2020; Yu et al., 2022; Xiao et al., 2024; Zhong et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025). These models lack a
broader natural-image prior, and their restoration quality can degrade on scenes, devices, or capture
conditions outside the training distribution. Moreover, existing benchmarks cover relatively limited
device pairings and capture conditions (Sun et al., 2018; Yuan et al., 2019; 2020; He et al., 2020;
Yu et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2024). This limited diversity underrepresents complex real-world moiré,
limiting how accurately these benchmarks assess demoiréing performance in the wild.
Our key observation is that dedicated demoiréing models trained on limited paired data often struggle
to restore complex real-world textures under challenging out-of-distribution moiré corruption. Their
ability to recover the underlying content is constrained by the visual patterns represented in their
training data. In contrast, a pretrained image editor provides a broader natural-image prior acquired
through large-scale pretraining, offering the potential to restore content under severe moiré corruption
beyond the coverage of task-specific paired data (Rombach et al., 2022; Brooks et al., 2023; Saharia
et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024). This motivates
treating demoiréing as a content-preserving editing task and adapting a pretrained flow-matching
image editor (Wu et al., 2025) to exploit this prior.
However, applying this generative prior to demoiréing requires adapting it to the fidelity demands of
image restoration. We therefore supplement standard velocity matching with endpoint restoration
losses that directly supervise the restored image. The remaining challenge is to handle the co-
occurrence of low-frequency color banding and high-frequency stripes while preserving fine details.
To address this, DemoiréEdit uses a dual-scale condition-shift strategy that first performs global
correction at low resolution and then refines the image at full resolution. Since the corrected estimate
provides global guidance but lacks fine detail, early denoising steps use the Stage 1 output, whereas
later steps return to the original input for detail recovery.
To evaluate challenging moiré encountered in daily life, we introduce DailyMoiré130: 130 smart-
phone captures of signage, monitors, and laptops exhibiting low-frequency color banding, fine
high-frequency stripes, and mixed-frequency interference (Fig. 4). These captures lack aligned clean
targets; evaluation combines residual moiré metrics with separate human judgments of suppression,
detail preservation, and overall quality.
On DailyMoiré130, our method achieves the lowest residual moiré area and receives 71.3% overall-
quality preference in a blind Best-of-7 study with 31 participants. We also evaluate on paired
FHDMi/UHDM, reducing residual moiré confidence by 47.1%/9.3% and increasing MUSIQ by
4.3%/2.0% over the best baseline for each metric (Tab. 2). Our contributions are:
• We reformulate demoiréing as content-preserving editing and introduce endpoint-supervised flow
training to adapt a pretrained editor for faithful restoration.
• We propose dual-scale condition-shift inference to separate global moiré correction from full-
resolution detail recovery.
• We introduce DailyMoiré130, a benchmark targeting challenging moiré in everyday screen photog-
raphy, with a reference-free evaluation protocol. The dataset is publicly available to support future
comparisons (Appendix D).

RELATED WORK
Image demoiréing.
Early demoiréing methods relied on hand-crafted spectral priors or signal-
processing assumptions to suppress periodic moiré patterns (Sidorov & Kokaram, 2002; Siddiqui et al.,
2009; Liu et al., 2015). With the rise of deep learning, Sun et al. (Sun et al., 2018) introduced the first
CNN-based solution, followed by frequency-aware designs using learnable bandpass filters (Zheng
et al., 2020), wavelet decomposition (Liu et al., 2020; Luo et al., 2020; Yeh et al., 2024), and multiscale
fusion (Peng et al., 2024). Subsequent work extends demoiréing to ultra-high-definition inputs (Yu
2


et al., 2022; Xiao et al., 2024), unpaired data (Zhong et al., 2024), RAW-domain processing (Xu et al.,
2024; Yang et al., 2025a), and universal data generation (Yang et al., 2025b; Choi, 2018). In particular,
Freqformer (Liu et al., 2025) explicitly separates moiré artifacts into high-frequency localized textures
and low-frequency color distortions. While these approaches emphasize task-specific frequency
modeling, we adapt a pretrained editor’s natural-image prior to challenging real-world moiré.

Flow matching and trajectory supervision.
Flow matching trains continuous normalizing flows
by regressing vector fields along prescribed probability paths (Lipman et al., 2023), and rectified
flow learns straight transport paths that enable efficient ODE sampling (Liu et al., 2022). These
objectives are effective for generative modeling, but do not by themselves guarantee pixel-faithful
restoration endpoints. Related acceleration and consistency objectives supervise shortened or distilled
trajectories (Salimans & Ho, 2022; Song et al., 2023). We therefore complement velocity supervision
with pixel, perceptual, and frequency losses (Zhang et al., 2018; Jiang et al., 2021).

source image, edit references, and a text prompt. We first define the restoration objective and training
procedure, then describe dual-scale inference (Fig. 3) and the training data.

3.4
MIXED DEMOIRÉ DATA
We train on a mixed corpus D = Dpublic ∪Ddoc ∪Dself−rec. Dpublic combines paired bench-
marks (Sun et al., 2018; Yuan et al., 2019; 2020; He et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2024),
the UniDemoire augmentation pipeline (Yang et al., 2025b), and the screen-photo simulator of Choi
(2018). Ddoc contains high-contrast documents and forms from DocVQA (Mathew et al., 2021) and
XFUND (Xu et al., 2022) to strengthen text preservation. Dself−rec uses identical clean images as
input and target to preserve artifact-free content and discourage over-editing.

EXPERIMENTS
4.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
Implementation details.
We fine-tune the pretrained Qwen-Edit (Wu et al., 2025) flow-matching
editor via LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) adapters on DiT using our endpoint-supervised objective (Sec. 3.2)
on the mixed datasets described in Sec. 3.4, which aggregates six public demoiréing datasets. We
use a batch size of 96 and a patch size of 256 × 256 with a cosine learning-rate schedule decaying
from 1 × 10−4 to 1 × 10−7. We fine-tune for 3k optimizer steps. All experiments are conducted on 4
NVIDIA H100 GPUs. During inference, Stage 1 resizes the input to a short side of 1024 pixels and
uses 10 denoising steps. Stage 2 starts from latent noise at full resolution and uses N=10 steps with
switching ratio r=0.3. The fixed prompt and full training recipe are given in Appendix C.
Benchmarks and metrics.
To assess restoration in challenging everyday screen-capture scenarios,
we collect DailyMoiré130, a benchmark of 130 screen photographs from shopping-mall signage,
desktop monitors, and laptops. These captures exhibit low-frequency color banding, fine high-
frequency stripes, and mixed-frequency interference, and have no aligned clean targets. They were
captured with iPhone 17, vivo X200s, Honor 90 Pro, Huawei Enjoy 90 Pro Max, and iPhone 16
Pro Max (Fig. 4). We also use FHDMi (He et al., 2020) with 2019 1080p images and UHDM (Yu
et al., 2022) with 500 4K images for paired evaluation. Due to the spatially non-uniform nature of
moiré, full-image fidelity metrics such as PSNR and SSIM (Wang et al., 2004) can be dominated by
artifact-free regions and fail to reflect actual moiré removal quality.
We therefore additionally report two auxiliary metrics based on a pretrained moiré detector D (Yang
et al., 2023) called MoiréDet. Given a restored image ˆI, we divide it into P overlapping patches
and obtain a moiré probability ci ∈[0, 1] for each patch, where a higher ci indicates stronger moiré
presence. Let M denote the number of patches whose moiré probability exceeds 0.05. We define:

QUANTITATIVE EVALUATION
Tab. 1 evaluates all methods on our proposed DailyMoiré130, which targets challenging moiré
in everyday screen photography. In a blind Best-of-7 study with 31 participants, our method is
preferred in 74.7% of trials for moiré suppression, 68.5% for detail preservation, and 71.3% for
overall quality. Participants assess detail preservation separately against the visible input using
full-image views and magnified crops (Appendix E). The strongest baseline, ESDNet-L, receives
8.8% overall preference. The no-reference perceptual metric further supports this finding, with our
method achieving the highest CLIP-IQA+ (0.5928). It also achieves the lowest residual moiré area
(0.0551), while UniDemoiré-control has slightly lower RMC. These results demonstrate effective
moiré suppression and strong human preference on challenging real-world captures.
Each participant completes 18 Best-of-7 trials, collectively covering all 130 images, with method
identities hidden and candidate positions randomized and counterbalanced. Uncertain responses
remain in the denominator. The overall-quality preference rate has a participant-clustered 95%
confidence interval of 65.1–76.9%, and the result remains consistent under stricter attention checks
(Appendix E).

DISCUSSION
Our method improves visible moiré suppression through a pretrained natural-image prior, but intro-
duces trade-offs. Restored images may not align exactly with clean targets, lowering PSNR/SSIM,
and severe color bands can retain color shifts. Two-stage inference is costly and not optimized for
real-time use. We therefore assess fidelity, perceptual quality, and residual moiré jointly.
6
CONCLUSION
We present a content-preserving editing framework for real-world demoiréing. Endpoint supervision
adapts a pretrained natural-image prior to restoration, while dual-scale condition-shift inference
combines global correction with detail recovery. Moreover, we introduce DailyMoiré130 to evaluate
challenging everyday screen photographs with color bands, fine stripes, and mixed-frequency artifacts.
Our method achieves the lowest residual moiré area and strong human preference on this benchmark,
with complementary gains on paired datasets. These results support pretrained editing priors for
restoring moiré-corrupted content beyond the coverage of task-specific paired data.

C.2
INFERENCE DETAILS
We use the same prompt for all test images. Stage 1 resizes the short side to 1024 pixels, pairs the
image with a black reference, and uses 10 denoising steps. Stage 2 performs one full-resolution loop
from latent noise with N = 10 steps and r = 0.3. The first ⌈rN⌉= 3 steps use the upsampled
Stage-1 edit latent, and the remaining seven use the original input latent.
Public implementation.
Inference, training, and an interactive demo are available in the
DemoiréEdit repository; the restoration adapter is available on Hugging Face. The maintained
implementation additionally includes a latent-MSE extension, separate from the four-term objective
reported in this manuscript. The released adapter is a historical checkpoint; its identity, provenance,
and the limits of its correspondence to the maintained training recipe are documented in the repository
model card.
C.3
RUNTIME
The full two-stage recipe takes approximately 260 seconds for a 4K image on a single NVIDIA H100
GPU in FP32.

DAILYMOIRÉ130 DATASET AND EVALUATION PROTOCOL
DailyMoiré130 contains 130 real-world screen photographs targeting challenging moiré in everyday
life. The images cover signage, monitors, and laptops, with low-frequency color banding, fine
high-frequency stripes, and mixed-frequency interference. Since these captures have no aligned
clean targets, evaluation combines no-reference perceptual scores, residual moiré detector scores, and
human preference (Tab. 1).
Public release.
DailyMoiré130 is available on Hugging Face (revision 573da28), with per-image
checksums and an evaluation protocol. The public release retains the original resolutions and color
profiles. It includes local blur in 18 images, while the remaining 112 retain their scene pixels;
pixels outside the edited regions are unchanged. The localized edits are partial occlusion rather than
comprehensive anonymization. Such edits can affect restoration outputs and no-reference metrics.
The results reported in this manuscript use the historical inputs and have not been rerun on this public
derivative.
For detector-based analysis, a restored image is divided into P overlapping patches with moiré
confidence ci ∈[0, 1]. RMC averages these confidence scores, while RMA measures the fraction of
patches exceeding 0.05, as defined in Eq. 11.

HUMAN PREFERENCE STUDY
Participants and inclusion.
The blind study includes 31 participants who complete the randomized
questionnaire and pass at least one of two embedded attention checks. Each questionnaire contains
18 Best-of-7 trials, two black-reference A/B trials, and two attention checks. Each attention check
presents one intact restoration alongside six strongly corrupted variants. Among the 31 completed
questionnaires, 29 participants pass both checks and two pass one. We also report the stricter 29-
participant subset below. Incomplete questionnaires and responses from the earlier, non-randomized
questionnaire version are excluded.
The study is conducted remotely on 13 laptops, 10 mobile devices, and eight desktops. A break
is offered after Question 11. Summed active response time has a median of 10.5 minutes and an
interquartile range of 6.9–13.8 minutes. Displays are not calibrated, and no formal visual-acuity or
color-vision examination is imposed.
Image sampling and presentation.
The schedule contains 40 counterbalanced templates of 18
distinct main-trial images, assigning each image five or six times. Public entry cycles through these
templates. The completed cohort uses 24 distinct templates, with seven repeated assignments. Its 558
main trials cover all 130 DailyMoiré130 images, with a median of four evaluations per image and a
range of one to nine.
Each trial compares the seven methods in Tab. 1, with identities hidden and random-
ized/counterbalanced across fixed A–G positions. Each method appears at each position 63–95
times. Participants inspect the input and candidates through full-image views and manually selected
magnified crops. The same crop coordinates are used for every method and marked on the input
overview.
Questionnaire criteria.
For each image, participants answer three questions, translated here from
the Chinese questionnaire.
• Moiré suppression. Which result has fewer residual moiré patterns, color bands, and screen-
photography artifacts?
• Detail preservation. Which result better preserves the text, edges, colors, and content details in
the input, without obvious rewriting, missing content, or structural deformation?
• Overall quality. Overall, which result has better and more natural visual quality?
Each question offers A–G, difficult to distinguish / no clear best, and unable to answer / image display
problem. The detail question explicitly assesses preservation against the visible input.

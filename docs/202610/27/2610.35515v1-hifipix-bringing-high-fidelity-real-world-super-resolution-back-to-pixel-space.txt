ABSTRACT 

Diffusion-based real-world image super-resolution suffers from limited reconstruc-tion fidelity due to information loss in VAE-based latent representations. Recently, pixel-space diffusion models offer a promising high-faithfulness alternative by avoiding latent compression, but adapting them to Real-ISR remains challenging due to inherent patch boundary artifacts and degraded generative capability. To address the issues and maximize the potential of pretrained pixel-space diffusion models, we propose HiFiPix , a pixel-space Real-ISR framework capable of ultra high-fidelity reconstruction and fine-grained visual generation effects. To sup-press patch boundary artifacts, we penalize unrealistic high-frequency periodic components with a Cross-Scale Wavelet Discriminator (CWSD) for image-level supervision, and Mixup Contrastive Self-Distillation (MixCSD) for intermediate representation regularization. Furthermore, we uncover a heterogeneous generation-fidelity trade-off across low-rank adapters (LoRA) and propose Selective LoRA Pruning (LoRA-SP) to adaptively prune LoRA layers, enhancing generative ca-pability with minimal deterioration of reconstruction fidelity. Extensive experi-ments demonstrate that HiFiPix consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art latent-space methods in terms of both restoration accuracy and perceptual quality by a large margin. Code and models will be released.

However, lacking powerful generative priors, GAN-based models exhibit limited capability for recovering fine-grained details. Over the past few years, diffusion-based generative models Rombach et al. (2022); Labs (2025) have advanced Real-ISR by producing highly photo-realistic details. However, existing Real-ISR meth-ods Dong et al. (2025); Fang et al. This irreversible compression discards the subtle textures from LQ inputs, often leading to structural inconsistencies, ghosting artifacts, and ultimately compromised restoration fidelity. To mitigate this issue, prior works try to improve input quality Lin et al. (2024), reduce the VAE compression ratio Yi et al. (2025), or inject LQ features into denoising networks Yang et al. (2024). However, these strategies fail to fundamentally address the information loss introduced by latent space compression, yielding reduced generative capability ( e.g., blurry leaves in the top row of Fig. 1) or limited fidelity gains ( e.g., distorted characters in the bottom row of Fig. 1). As a result, restoring highly faithful textures while preserving excellent visual quality still remains a significant challenge in Real-ISR. More recently, pixel-level text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models Yu et al. (2026); Cai et al. (2026) have demonstrated effectiveness in mitigating reconstruction accuracy issues by removing the VAE component. However, roughly fine-tuning pixel-space models to Real-ISR via LoRA adapters still faces two key challenges. First, pixel-space models typically adopt patch-based tokenization, while different tokens only interact within pure MLP-based attention blocks. Unlike VAE-based ones Saharia et al. (2022); Esser et al. (2024), whose convolutional decoders naturally preserve spatial continuity, pixel-space decoding does not explicitly enforce information interaction across patch boundaries. This leads to discontinuities between patches and periodic grid artifacts, particularly in texture-rich regions. As shown in Fig. 2 (a), under the same training strategy, pixel-space models suffer from more severe periodic artifacts than latent models Esser et al. (2024); Labs (2025), degrading the structural consistency across neighboring patches. Second, as depicted in Fig. 2 (b), although removing the VAE eliminates latent compression, it discards the compact semantic representations, making high-level semantic modeling more challenging in pure pixel space. Therefore, this weakens the generative prior and biases models toward deterministic reconstruction rather than plausible detail synthesis. Consequently, models tend to overfit reasonable but over-smoothed structures, while losing their ability to generate missing high-frequency details, especially under severe degradations. To address these challenges, we propose HiFiPix , a pixel-space framework for high-fidelity Real-ISR. We first focus on patch-boundary artifacts arising from pixel-space decoding and observe that they mainly manifest as discontinuities in high-frequency structures. This motivates us to exploit coherent high-frequency information for cross-patch continuity. Specifically, we introduce a Cross-Scale Wavelet Discriminator (CSWD) that propagates high-frequency features across scales for boundary-aware image-level supervision, and further propose a Mixup Contrastive Self-Distillation (MixCSD) 

that leverages an HQ-mixed branch to regularize intermediate representations at feature levels. To-gether, they provide complementary external and internal supervision to suppress patch-boundary artifacts. Furthermore, we address degradation of generative capability during training. We observe that LoRA updates across layers are not equally necessary: some deep layers merely echo trans-formations already made by shallower layers, contributing to overfitting pixel-level characteristics rather than preserving high-level semantics. We thus propose Selective LoRA Pruning (LoRA-SP) ,which detects and prunes such redundant layers via Centered Kernel Alignment-based representa-tional Kornblith et al. (2019), protects fidelity-critical layers from pruning, and progressively freezes rest, thereby constraining fine-tuning capacity precisely where overfitting risks occur. 2Under Review Furthermore, existing Real-ISR benchmarks are hindered by low-quality ground truth, suffering from missing fine textures Cai et al. (2019); Wei et al. (2020) or background de-focus Wu et al. (2026). As noted in A-FINE Chen et al. (2025a), generative restoration models may yield images visually superior to these flawed GT references, rendering nearly all reference-based fidelity metrics unreliable. To address this issue, we construct Dual-Q-Bench , an ultra-high-quality paired real-world ISR benchmark captured via screen photography, whose HQ reference images feature rich textures and minimal background de-focus. Dual-Q-Bench enables reliable evaluation of both fidelity and perceptual quality. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose HiFiPix , a Real-ISR method built on a pretrained pixel-level model, shifting Real-ISR from latent-space to pixel-space modeling, achieving high-fidelity restoration without sacrificing generative capability or inference efficiency. • We propose CSWD that propagates high-frequency features across scales for boundary-aware image-level supervision, and MixCSD , which distills clean representations to enhance patch-level discriminability, jointly mitigating patch-boundary artifacts. • We propose LoRA-SP , which identifies functionally redundant LoRA layers via representa-tional drift and progressively prunes them while protecting fidelity-critical layers, preserving the pretrained generative prior during supervised fine-tuning. • We introduce Dual-Q-Bench , a high-quality paired Real-ISR benchmark that addresses the low-quality GT and background de-focus in existing benchmarks, enabling more reliable evaluation of both generative capability and reconstruction fidelity. 

2 RELATED WORKS 

2.1 REAL -WORLD IMAGE SUPER -R ESOLUTION 

With the advent of large-scale T2I models, diffusion-based Real-ISR methods have surpassed GAN-based approaches. Existing methods utilize LQ input to control diffusion models for faithful restora-tion by leveraging pluggable adapters Chen et al. (2025b), dual-stream architectures Yang et al. (2024), ControlNet Yu et al. (2024), initialized restoration modules Lin et al. (2024), semantic condi-tioning Wu et al. (2026), and large-scale high-quality training datasets Yu et al. (2024). Furthermore, to fully use input information, methods like OSEDiff Wu et al. (2024b) and TSD-SR Dong et al. (2025) replace random noise with LQ images, while S3Diff Zhang et al. (2024) and ODTSR Fang et al. (2026) incorporate GAN loss to enhance perceptual quality. TVT Yi et al. (2025) increases the VAE latent resolution to improve fidelity, whereas LucidFlux Fei et al. (2026) and StrSR Wang et al. (2026a) improve fidelity by fine-tuning FLUX Labs (2025) or Z-image Cai et al. (2025) with in-creased VAE channel capacity. Nevertheless, these methods still operate in latent spaces, where VAE compression inevitably limits reconstruction fidelity. In contrast, we perform Real-ISR directly in pre-trained pixel spaces Cai et al. (2026), enabling high-fidelity and perceptually pleasing Real-ISR. 2.2 PIXEL -SPACE DIFFUSION MODELS 

Early pixel-space diffusion models Dhariwal & Nichol (2021); Saharia et al. (2022) suffered from prohibitive computational costs at high resolutions. Recent works Chen et al. (2025c); Wang et al. (2026b); Li & He (2026); Ma et al. (2026b); Lu et al. (2026b); Ma et al. (2026a); Yu et al. (2026) have revisited pixel-space diffusion by improving its training efficiency, sampling strategy, and architectural design. Among them, PixelFlow Chen et al. (2025c) enables efficient end-to-end pixel-space generation, while JiT Li & He (2026) and PixelDiT Yu et al. (2026) further improve the scalability and efficiency of pixel-space modeling. HiDream-O1-Image Cai et al. (2026) extends this paradigm to a large-scale pixel-space T2I diffusion transformer with a unified generative architecture. 3 METHOD 

3.1 EMPIRICAL OBSERVATIONS 

Following classical one-step fine-tuning paradigms Yi et al. (2025), we identify two critical insights when applying HiDream-O1-Image for Real-ISR. First , patch-boundary artifacts may not arise solely from the patch-wise architecture of pixel-space models. As shown in Fig. 3 (a), grid-like artifacts become more pronounced under severe degradation than under mild degradation, suggesting that imperfect high-frequency reconstruction is an important cause of boundary artifacts, as severe degradation makes high-frequency reconstruction more challenging. This observation motivates a low-cost and architecture-agnostic strategy to explicitly regularize high-frequency components. Specifically, we design the Cross-Scale Wavelet Discriminator, which penalizes spurious boundary artifacts via multi-scale information constraints. Besides, we propose Mixup Contrastive Self-Distillation to ensure faithful generation of genuine high-frequency details. 

Second , simply applying LoRA layers yields SR outputs lacking fine-grained details, indicating that such a general strategy drastically reduces the generative capability of pixel-space models. As shown in Fig. 3 (b), we perform ablation studies to investigate individual LoRA layers, then reveals that generative capacity and fidelity are non-uniformly entangled across depth: removing certain layers boosts generative expressiveness at negligible fidelity cost, whereas removing others yields a similar generative gain but at the expense of severe fidelity degradation. This heterogeneity rules out uniform layer pruning or fine-tuning as an effective strategy; therefore, we propose Selective LoRA Pruning that identifies and removes layers that mainly reduce the generation ability. 3.2 CROSS -S CALE WAVELET DISCRIMINATOR

CSWD & MixCSD. As shown in Fig. 6, both CSWD and MixCSD effectively suppress periodic boundary artifacts, and their combination yields the best visual results. From Tab. 3, both terms also substantially improve reconstruction fidelity (PSNR/LPIPS), yet using either alone degrades generation quality (MUSIQ/MANIQA). In contrast, combining them achieves the best fidelity without excessive loss of generation capability. CSWD and MixCSD penalize artifacts from image and intermediate feature domains, respectively, making them complementary. 

GT-LQ Mixing Strategy. We conduct comparison experiments by replacing the GT-LQ mixture branch with GT-only inputs. Tab. 4 validates the benefits of mixed LQ-HQ teacher inputs. An HQ-only teacher creates a huge domain gap relative to LQ student inputs, degrading performance. 

Contrastive Loss. We substitute contrastive loss with knowledge distillation loss Dong et al. (2025). As shown in Tab. 5, contrastive learning is superior to distillation by leveraging negative samples to avoid representation collapse and maintain feature diversity, thereby improving performance. 

LoRA-SP. LoRA-SP identifies redundant LoRA layers for pruning and incorporates a fidelity-aware veto mechanism, which retains critical layers by postponing their pruning to later candidates. As shown in Tab. 7, redundancy detection alone boosts generation quality yet causes notable fidelity drops. Equipped with the fidelity-aware veto, LoRA-SP yields an improved generation-fidelity trade-off and consistently surpasses uniform and random LoRA pruning. These results validate our design and suggest an interpretable path to enhance pixel-space model generation via selective LoRA pruning. Moreover, Tab. 7 indicates that this strategy yields marginal gains for latent-space models (e.g., Z-Image), probably because VAE-driven semantic compression produces little layer redundancy. Hence, LoRA-SP is tailored for boosting the generation performance of pixel-space models. Due to page limitations, more ablation studies can be found in Appendix. A.4. 4.4 LIMITATIONS 

HiFiPix suffers from two main limitations. First, while patch-boundary artifacts are mostly mitigated, several extremely blurred inputs still contain faint residual seams within texture-rich areas. Adding a generative decoder ( e.g., PiD Lu et al. (2026a)) could suppress these artifacts further, yet we abandon it for the additional consumption. Second, as our pipeline does not rely on text prompts, we exclude text-conditioned metrics such as CLIPIQA. These metrics focus mainly on text-image alignment, thereby our prompt-free setting yields inherently low scores that offer limited evaluation results. 

5 CONCLUSION 

We present HiFiPix, a pixel-space generative framework which enables faithful restoration by re-moving VAE compression. To mitigate patch-boundary artifacts and generative degradation, we 9Under Review propose CSWD, MixCSD for enhanced high-frequency reconstruction and cross-patch consistency, and LoRA-SP to generate fine-grained details with minimal fidelity sacrifice. We further construct Dual-Q-Bench, a high-quality benchmark for reliable evaluation. Experiments show HiFiPix outper-forms VAE-based methods, demonstrating the promise of pixel-space modeling for Real-ISR. AI USE STATEMENT

Existing paired Real-ISR benchmarks often suffer from low-quality or unrealistic GT, which can compromise the reliability of fidelity and generation evaluation. As shown in Fig. 7, existing benchmarks ( e.g., RealSR Cai et al. (2019) and DrealSR Wei et al. (2020)) contain GT with blur details or background de-focus. Such imperfections introduce an undesirable evaluation bias: outputs with richer and more realistic details may deviate from deficient GT and receive lower fidelity scores, while outputs that closely mimic the imperfect GT may be favored instead, finally leading to unreasonable measurements, which is also indicated in Chen et al. (2025a). Besides, background de-focus in DIV2K Agustsson & Timofte (2017) and ScreenSR Wu et al. (2026) complicates fidelity assessment, as recovering sharp structures in the background can be incorrectly penalized as deviation from the GT. Furthermore, large-model-generated GT ( e.g., GGT-GeneralMix Kong et al. (2026)) do not necessarily guarantee faithful details due to potential hallucinations. To address these limitations, we construct Dual-Q-Bench , a high-quality real-world paired benchmark with realistic and sharp GT images at 1024 2 resolution, enabling more reliable evaluation of both fidelity and generation quality. 

GT Construction: We crawl high-resolution photographs from Unsplash and filter them using a combination of NR metrics ( e.g., MANIQA Yang et al. (2022), MUSIQ Ke et al. (2021), Q-Insight Li et al. (2026)), retaining only images whose aggregated score exceeds GT images in LSDIR Li et al. (2023) by an average of 30% to ensure superior intrinsic quality. Laplacian-gradient response is then used to crop texture-rich regions, followed by manual inspection to discard images with background de-focus or painterly appearance, yielding 100 GT images with sharp, rich details at 1024 2 resolution. 

LQ Acquisition: We obtain real-world LQ images following the same screen photographing strategy in Wu et al. (2026). Specifically, the original GT images are shown on an ultra-high-definition screen in the Imaging Laboratory. We implement a timed routine to capture LQ from GT images displayed on-screen by using a mobile phone for real-world degradation simulation. The specific mobile phone model used for image capture will be disclosed upon acceptance of our paper. 

GT-LQ Alignment Strategy: Each LQ is registered to its GT via SIFT keypoint matching with RANSAC homography estimation, falling back to multi-scale template matching when correspon-dences are insufficient, and further refined by ECC for sub-pixel accuracy. Number of Selected Layers in LoRA-SP. With 35 LoRA layers total for fine-tuning, Tab. 11 examines how many layers should be pruned to boost generative capability while preserving fidelity. We find that both insufficient and excessive pruning yield suboptimal results. Specifically, pruning too few layers has only a limited effect on improving generative performance. While pruning more layers can improve generative performance, it severely compromises the model’s fidelity. Pruning 4

layers of all LoRA layers achieves the optimal trade-off between fidelity and generative capability. 

Number of Distillation Layers in MixCSD. Tab. 12 analyzes the effect of intermediate layer quantity for MixCSD-based contrastive distillation. We find that insufficient distillation layers provide limited supervision, hindering the generation of high-frequency representations and thus impeding performance improvements. Conversely, distilling excessive layers imposes redundant constraints on intermediate features and reduces generative flexibility. Accordingly, we uniformly adopt 5 layers (roughly 1/7 of total layers) for contrastive distillation in final settings.

We initially expected that layer-adaptive attenuation of LoRA across all layers would improve generative quality, as weakening the adapters should release the generative prior of the pretrained backbone.

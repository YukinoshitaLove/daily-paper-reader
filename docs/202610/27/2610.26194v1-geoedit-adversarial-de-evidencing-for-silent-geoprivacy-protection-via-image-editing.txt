Abstract
Recent efforts have demonstrated the strong capabilities of Multi-
modal Large Reasoning Models (MLRMs) in geographic reasoning.
By jointly analyzing subtle visual cues, these models can infer
the locations of photos, thus enabling doxing even by non-expert
adversaries. Existing defense methods struggle to balance the share-
ability of photos with effective protection, and often fail to suffi-
ciently disrupt the implicit geo-evidence chains embedded in scene
semantics. To this end, we propose GeoEdit, an adversarial de-
evidencing framework for silent geoprivacy protection via image
editing. Specifically, we apply minimal edits to high-risk evidence
anchors in the image, thereby disrupting the underlying spatial
reasoning chains while preserving overall scene semantics. We
identify these anchors by combining structured knowledge with
adversarially discovered geographic cues. To further navigate the
large editing action space, we adopt a learnable policy to iteratively
refine editing decisions, distilling successful trajectories into trans-
ferable natural-language insights for consistent and efficient editing.
Evaluated on LifePhotoBench, GeoEdit achieves strong protection
against 6 mainstream MLRMs, outperforming visual perturbation
baselines by over 0.59 in image fidelity.

conceal visible cues [19, 20, 22], but they often compromise semantic
completeness and visual naturalness, while also leaving clear signs

Moreover, such perturbations are often model-specific and tend to
overfit, making them fragile under model evolution or common
image transformations in real-world settings.
To this end, we aim to achieve more generalizable geoprivacy
protection by introducing targeted, localized modifications that
weaken relations among geo-evidence cues, thereby disrupting
the spatial reasoning chain. We propose Geo-Evidence Editing
(GeoEdit), a de-evidencing image editing framework for silent geo-
privacy protection. GeoEdit combines structured knowledge with
adversarially discovered geo cues to identify high-risk evidence an-
chors and applies minimal edits to break their mutual corroboration
while preserving scene semantics and visual naturalness. This de-
evidencing process is inherently challenging, as the editing space is
large, subtle, and structured around geo-evidence cues. Navigating
this space requires identifying the visual elements that support
geolocation, understanding how they jointly contribute to geo-
graphic reasoning, and modifying them in a restrained manner that
avoids visible artifacts. To address this, GeoEdit introduces a test-
time insight distillation mechanism built on an agentic process that
includes evidence discovery, edit synthesis, and adversarial verifica-
tion. From this process, the framework extracts successful editing
trajectories and summarizes them as transferable natural-language
protection insights to guide future edits and improve cross-sample
robustness. Extensive experiments on LifePhotoBench show that
GeoEdit achieves state-of-the-art protection against 6 mainstream
MLRMs. It neutralizes precise geolocation accuracy (Acc@1km) to
near 0% across all MLRMs, and can achieve a remarkable Median
Distance Error (MDE) of over 5000 km on existing mainstream ML-
RMs. Meanwhile, it maintains a high fidelity of 0.944, far surpassing
existing visual perturbation methods (typically below 0.35). Our
major contributions are as follows:
• We propose GeoEdit, an image-editing framework for Silent
Geoprivacy Protection that disrupts geo-evidence corroboration
instead of relying on obfuscation or perturbations, thereby re-
ducing geolocation risk while preserving visual quality.
• To navigate the large and subtle editing space structured around
geo-evidence cues, we introduce a self-evolution mechanism
to distill successful rollouts into transferable and interpretable
natural-language protection insights.
• Extensive experiments show that GeoEdit achieves strong pro-
tection against 6 mainstream MLRMs. It can reduce geolocation
Acc@1km to near 0% and achieves a Median Distance Error ex-
ceeding 5,000km, while maintaining high visual fidelity (0.944),
surpassing perturbation-based methods by over 0.59.

Related Work
Geoprivacy Threats from Multimodal Models. Geoprivacy
risks have broadened with the rapid advancement of multimodal
models, evolving from early encoders (e.g., GeoCLIP) to current
MLRMs (e.g., Gemini-2.5) with strong geolocation capabilities.
Early methods such as DeepGeo [24], efficient feature extractors
[1], and multimodal alignment models like GeoCLIP [26] estimate
coarse geographic locations via image retrieval or joint embedding
spaces, extending privacy risks from isolated landmarks to regional
identification. Recently, MLRMs have greatly amplified doxing
threats by enabling fine-grained geographic inference from previ-
ously unexploitable daily-life photos, fundamentally transforming
geo-localization into a reasoning-driven task [13, 39]. MLRM-driven
methods such as PIGEON [8], LLMGeo [27], GeoLocator [34], and
Doxing via the Lens [17] leverage vast world knowledge, spatial
graphs, and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to synthesize subtle
visual clues (e.g., architecture, vegetation, language context) [12].

Methodology
We design GeoEdit as an evidence-level editing framework for
geoprivacy protection, which focuses on identifying and modifying
key location-sensitive cues rather than introducing fragile and
visually unnatural perturbations. We highlight our trap-like Silent
Geoprivacy Protection strategy, in which the defense preserves
visual naturalness under normal viewing while covertly interfering
with geolocation inference when it is attempted.
3.1
Architecture Overview
As shown in Figure 3, GeoEdit consists of De-Evidencing Image
Editing and Self-Evolution via Geoprivacy Protection Insight
Distillation. Specifically, ❶The image editing process follows a
ReAct loop [35], enabling the policy 𝜋to explicitly reason about
location-sensitive evidence, propose edits, and verify whether an
edit improves geoprivacy while preserving visual quality for the
current sample. ❷A self-evolution mechanism is further introduced
to address the short-sightedness of per-sample decisions, thereby
avoiding overly aggressive edits, unstable preferences across roll-
outs, and limited interpretability of the edits.

Experiments
4.1
Experimental Setup
Datasets. Existing studies [15, 38] often treat geoprivacy protec-
tion as an adversarial perturbation problem evaluated on standard
geolocation benchmarks such as Im2GPS3k [10] and YFCC4k [25].
These benchmarks mainly contain public and benign scenes, such
as tourist attractions or landmark check-ins, where location dis-
closure is intentional. This leads to a mismatch between current
evaluation settings and the real-world geoprivacy risk scenarios.
To better reflect real-world privacy risks, we establish LifePho-
toBench, a curated subset comprising 1,553 challenging images
(383 from Im2GPS and 1,170 from YFCC). The selection criteria
strictly focus on unintentional privacy leakage in everyday scenar-
ios: ❶Event-centered daily-life photos, where the primary narra-
tive focuses on activities (e.g., dining, walking, resting). ❷Subject-
centered environmental portraits, where a person is the visual focus.
In both cases, explicit landmarks are intentionally excluded. The
scene context is not the main focus of the image but often plays a
key role in enabling geographic inference through implicit cues.
Evaluation Metrics. Geolocation accuracy is measured by the
Haversine distance between predicted and ground-truth coordi-
nates, evaluated at five granularities: street (1 km), city (25 km),
region (200 km), country (750 km), and continent (2500 km). We
report the Median Distance Error (MDE) to represent the overall
performance. To assess the Fidelity (Fid.) of the protected images,
we measure the cosine similarity between the CLIP (ViT-B/32) im-
age embeddings of the original and edited images. This metric
evaluates the extent to which the core visual narrative and overall
semantic content are preserved after protection.
Baselines & Target Models. We compare GeoEdit against: Un-
protected original images, M-Attack [14] (we use its untargeted
variant), GeoShield [15], and a simple Vanilla Edit baseline that
directly invokes an MLRM to generate and execute editing prompts

Impact of the Editing Insights. To evaluate the value of these
insights, we observe the performance changes of GeoEdit during
its self-evolution process from the perspective of insight buffer (K)
size growth. We introduce the following metrics for analysis:
• Δall is the average distance gain (in km), reflecting the total
geographical displacement of all edit attempts.
• Δeff is the average effective distance gain (in km), considering
only successful edits that displace geolocation without noticeable
artifacts, highlighting the quality of modifications.
• Eff% is the effective edit rate, representing the percentage of
successful and meaningful edits (those that cause measurable
displacement while maintaining the image’s naturalness).
As shown in Figure 6, we illustrate the trade-off between seman-
tic fidelity and defense efficiency (Eff%). As |K| increases to 12, the
effective rate peaks at 73.6% while fidelity remains stable around
0.91. Figure 6(b) shows the absolute privacy protection strength
(Δall and Δeff) and ReAct Steps. The average distance gain (Δall)
drops from 3,019 km at |K| = 0 and stabilizes around 1,954 km
at |K| = 20. At |K| = 0, the agent executes edits producing large
geographical displacements but lower fidelity. As the insight buffer
grows, the agent learns to reject edits that degrade visual natu-
ralness, stabilizing around 2,000 km to confuse geolocation at the
continent level. More importantly, this transition directly optimizes
inference efficiency. The accumulation of protection insights sig-
nificantly accelerates the ReAct convergence, dropping the average
iterations from an initial exploratory peak to merely 7.45 turns per
image. This highlights the contribution of our self-evolution mech-
anism in optimizing inference efficiency and mitigating concerns
regarding the high computational cost of agentic frameworks.

Conclusion
In this paper, we propose Geo-Evidence Editing (GeoEdit), an ad-
versarial de-evidencing framework designed for silent geoprivacy
protection via image editing. GeoEdit aims to disrupt the underly-
ing spatial reasoning chains by introducing semantic de-evidencing,
thereby achieving an optimal balance between robust privacy pro-
tection and visual utility. This de-evidencing process is guided by
an interpretable agentic loop that integrates risk assessment, edit
planning, and verification. Furthermore, the proposed insight buffer
offers interpretable guidance for the editing process, ensuring its
transferability across diverse scenarios. Evaluated on the LifePho-
toBench dataset, GeoEdit achieves a better trade-off in protection,
effectively reducing precise geolocation accuracy (Acc@1km) to
near 0% and achieving a Median Distance Error exceeding 5000 km
on some models, while maintaining exceptional fidelity of 0.944.

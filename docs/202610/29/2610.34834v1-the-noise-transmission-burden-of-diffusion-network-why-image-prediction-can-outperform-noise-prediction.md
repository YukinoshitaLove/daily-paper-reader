## 摘要
干净图像预测（x 预测）与噪声预测产生等价的总体最优去噪器，但在有限扩散网络中表现可能非常不同。对于噪声标准差为 σ_t 的含噪输入 Y_t，噪声预测器 f_{ε,t} 诱导出干净图像估计 X̂₁ = Y_t − σ_t f_{ε,t}(Y_t)。这揭示了一种噪声传输负担：预测器必须保留用于抵消输入损坏所需的信息。在对 d 维数据施加高斯损坏时，秩为 r<d 的线性输入瓶颈即使下游计算不受限制，也会施加 σ_t²(d−r) 的去噪误差下限，而图像预测对应的最优风险仍然有界。EDM 在高噪声下衰减该瓶颈贡献；精确预条件则在每个噪声水平上将其抵消。在没有输入瓶颈的情况下，假设从模型表示精确仿射恢复含噪块，我们刻画了线性去噪之外的修正的协方差谱如何决定在图像块维度之上所需的宽度。使用 JiT 的 ImageNet 实验表明，测得的去噪损失反映了预测的瓶颈下限及其通过预条件实现的衰减或抵消。进一步加宽会在低噪声下带来更大的相对去噪增益，此时线性去噪之外的修正散布在更多方向上，这与我们对所需模型宽度的谱刻画一致。总之，这些结果为不同预测目标、预条件选择和模型宽度下的实际生成质量差距提供了基于去噪的解释。

## Abstract
Clean-image prediction ($x$-prediction) and noise prediction yield equivalent population-optimal denoisers, yet can perform very differently in finite diffusion networks. For noisy input $Y_t$ with noise standard deviation $σ_t$, a noise predictor $f_{\varepsilon,t}$ induces the clean-image estimate $\widehat X_1=Y_t-σ_t f_{\varepsilon,t}(Y_t)$. This reveals a noise-transmission burden: the predictor must preserve information needed to cancel the input corruption. Under Gaussian corruption of $d$-dimensional data, a linear input bottleneck of rank $r<d$ imposes a denoising-error floor $σ_t^2(d-r)$ even with unrestricted downstream computation, while the corresponding optimal risk for image prediction remains bounded. EDM attenuates this bottleneck contribution at high noise; exact preconditioning cancels it at every noise level. Without an input bottleneck, assuming exact affine recovery of the noisy patch from the model representation, we characterize how the covariance spectrum of corrections beyond linear denoising determines the width required above the patch dimension. ImageNet experiments with JiT show that measured denoising losses reflect the predicted bottleneck floor and its attenuation or cancellation through preconditioning. Further widening yields larger relative denoising gains at low noise, where corrections beyond linear denoising spread across more directions, consistent with our spectral characterization of the required model width. Together, these results provide a denoising-based account of practical generation-quality gaps across prediction targets, preconditioning choices, and model widths.

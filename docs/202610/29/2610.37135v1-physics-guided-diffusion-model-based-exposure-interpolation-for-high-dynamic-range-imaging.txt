Physics Guided Diffusion Model Based Exposure
Interpolation for High Dynamic Range Imaging
Zhengguo Li, Fellow, IEEE, and Chaobing Zheng, Member IEEE
Abstract—Ghosting artifacts are an open problem for popular
multi-shot based high dynamic range (HDR) imaging. An ef-
fective solution is to capture two large-exposure-ratio (LER)
standard dynamic range (SDR) images simultaneously via a
single-shot. Brightness order reversal (BOR) artifacts are gen-
erated when the two LER images are directly fused together.
It is crucial to interpolate an image with an exposure time
in between the exposure times of two LER images. In this
paper, a novel physics-guided diffusion model based exposure
interpolation algorithm is proposed to address the BOR artifacts.
An initial image is interpolated by a physics-driven component,
and the residual high-frequency details are generated by a new
diffusion model based component. The new diffusion model
is based on the correlation analysis of generative models and
includes a new predicted target to reduce the variation of the
Pearson correlation between the noisy data and the target, as well
as the amplification factor of the fitting error. Compared with
existing neural augmented exposure interpolation algorithms
on top of convolution neural networks, the proposed exposure
interpolation algorithm has an inherent advantage to generate
more high frequency information. Experiment results validate
the proposed exposure interpolation algorithm.
Index Terms—High dynamic range imaging, dual large-exposure-
ratio images, brightness order reversal artifacts, exposure inter-
polation, physics guided diffusion model.
I. INTRODUCTION
Many real-world scenes are with high dynamic ranges (HDRs)
while a standard dynamic range (SDR) image by an existing
camera can only capture limited dynamic range of these real-
world scenes. Fine details in highlight/shadow regions of these
scenes cannot be captured simultaneously by the existing cam-
era via a single-shot. There are over-exposed/under-exposed
regions in an SDR image captured by the existing camera,
and the saturation could affect subsequent tasks such as image
classification, object detection, image segmentation, etc. An
effective solution is to capture multiple SDR images with
different exposures for an HDR scene [1]. The differently
exposed SDR images are usually captured sequentially by
multiple shots. Both camera movements and moving objects
are issues for such an HDR imaging system. It is not difficult
to address the former while ghosting artifacts caused by the
moving objects is an open problem for multi-shot HDR imag-
ing [2] even though there are many ghost removal algorithms
[3]–[7].

Two large-exposure-ratio (LER) SDR images can be captured
simultaneously by a single-shot using the special devices
in [8]–[10]. Ghosting artifacts are not an issue for such
single-shot HDR imaging. However, there are brightness order
reversal (BOR) artifacts when the two LER images are directly
fused together via conventional filter-based multi-scale expo-
sure fusion (MEF) algorithms [11]–[13]. Many data-driven
single-scale exposure fusion (SEF) algorithms are proposed to
address the BOR artifacts for the fusion of two LER images
[14]–[17]. Unfortunately, the data-driven SEF algorithms have
a difficulty in preserving the scene depth of real-world HDR
scene in the fused image. An alternative solution is to interpo-
late an image with an exposure time in between the two LER
images [18] and fuse the two LER images and interpolation
image using a conventional filter based MEF algorithm or
a data-driven MEF algorithm. It is thus highly demanded to
develop an efficient exposure interpolation algorithm.
In this paper, a novel exposure interpolation algorithm is
proposed through seamless integration of physics-driven and
generative AI-based methods. Similar to [19], [20], an image
is first interpolated from the two LER images using a physics-
driven exposure interpolation component. The image is then
refined via a generative AI-based component which is different
from the data-driven components in [19], [20]. Our generative
AI-based component has a potential to generate more high-
frequency information than the the data-driven components in
[19], [20]. Instead of directly using exiting diffusion models
[21]–[28], a new diffusion model is proposed on top of
theoretical analysis results in [29]. Each diffusion model has
a forward process and a reverse process. The forward process
begins with a ground truth data sample and evolves to a noise
sample by progressively adding the Gaussian noise to the
data sample. A predicted target can be defined in the forward
process. A neural network is trained to map the noisy data to
the predicted target during the forward process. The reverse
process starts from the noise sample and converts it back to
the corresponding data sample through progressively reducing
the Gaussian noise from the noisy data. The noisy data and the
output of the neural network at the current moment are utilized
to estimate the ground-truth data and the Gaussian noise in the
reverse process. They are adopted to predict the noisy data at
the next moment. The reverse process is usually conducted
via tens to thousands steps. Fortunately, the sampling process
can be optimized using trajectory distillation [23]. On the
other hand, the fitting error of the neural network could be
amplified during the trajectory distillation [23]. In addition
to the well known amplification factor on the fitting error

of the neural network, theoretical analysis in [29] indicates
that the variation of the Pearson correlation between the noisy
data and the predicted target is also important to reduce the
overall fitting error. Therefore, the predicted target plays an
important role in the diffusion model. A new predicted target
is proposed in this paper to reduce the variation of the Pearson
correlation between the predicted target and the noise data as
well as the amplification factor of the fitting error. The new
predicted target outperforms the predicted target in [30]. A
new diffusion model is then built up using the new predicted
target. Finally, the new model is adopted to design a novel
physics-guided diffusion model based exposure interpolation
algorithm for HDR imaging. Experiment results show that
the proposed exposure interpolation algorithm outperforms the
exposure interpolation algorithms in [18]–[20]. Overall, the
main contributions of this paper are:
1) a new diffusion model is proposed to minimize the variation
of the Pearson correlation between the predicted target and the
noise data as well as the amplification factor of the fitting error.
The new model outperforms the model in [30];
2) the new diffusion model is adopted to develop a new neu-
ral augmented exposure interpolation algorithm. More high-
frequency information is preserved by the proposed exposure
interpolation algorithms.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: preliminary
knowledge is provided in section II. Problem formulation is
given in section III. Details of the proposed algorithm are
presented in section IV. Experiment results are given in section
V to validate the proposed algorithm. Finally, conclusion
remarks are provided in section VI.

in the neural augmented exposure interpolation algorithm is

the data-driven exposure interpolation algorithm is supposed
to learn the image Z4.
The data-driven components in [19], [20] are designed using
convolution neural networks (CNNs). Unfortunately, CNNs
have a fundamentally difficulty in learning high-frequency
information. A new neural-augmented exposure interpolation
algorithm is proposed in this paper using a new diffusion
model to replace the CNNs in [19], [20].

V. EXPERIMENTS
A. Experimental Settings
Experiments are conducted to validate the effectiveness of
the proposed exposure interpolation algorithm. The HDR
imaging datasets introduced in [10], [19] are adopted for
training and evaluation. Specifically, the dataset is divided
into three subsets, including 420 sequences for training, 40
sequences for validation, and 60 sequences for testing. To
ensure data consistency, camera motion and object movement
are strictly controlled, and sequences containing noticeable
misalignment are excluded.Data In addition, all images are
randomly cropped into patches of size 512 × 512 to augment
the training data and improve model generalization. Further-
more, the dataset from [34] is utilized solely for testing to
evaluate the network’s performance, with no involvement in
the training stage. During training, the batch size is set to
4. We adopt Swin-UNet [32] as the backbone network. The
initial learning rate is 5 × 10−5, which is gradually decreased
following a cosine annealing schedule. All experiments are
implemented in PyTorch and conducted on an NVIDIA A6000
GPU.

F. Application of Exposure Interpolation to Exposure Fusion
To verify the effectiveness of the proposed exposure interpola-
tion, the virtual image ˆZ4 and the two LER images Z1 and Z7
are fused together using GGIF [41]. The resultant MEF algo-
rithm is compared with GGIF [41], MSF [18], DeepFuse [14],
MEFNET [42], UltraFusion [7], and DiFuser [43]. The GGIF,
DeepFuse, MEFNET, UltraFusion, and DiFuser take the two
LER images Z1 and Z7 as their inputs. All seven algorithms
are first objectively compared. The MEF-SSIM [45] for each
HDR scene is calculated using the fused image ZF and the set
{Z1, Z2, Z3, Z4, Z5, Z6, Z7}. An HDR image is synthesized
from the set {Z1, Z2, Z3, Z4, Z5, Z6, Z7} using the algorithm
in [1]. The TQMI is calculated using the HDR image and the
fused image ZF . As such, MEF-SSIM [45] and TQMI [46]
measure the similarity between the fused image ZF and the
real world HDR scene. Since ground truth sRGB images are
available for the data set in [34], the seven algorithms are
also compared using PSNR and SSIM. It should be pointed
out that the ground truth image is obtained by fusing all the
images in the set {Z1, Z2, Z3, Z4, Z5, Z6, Z7}. This implies
that PSNR and SSIM also measure the similarity between the
fused image ZF and the HDR scene. As demonstrated in Table
V, the resultant MEF algorithm by the proposed exposure
interpolation algorithm outperforms the other six exposure
fusion algorithms from the MEF-SSIM, TMQI, SSIM and
PSNR points of view. The consistent improvements across
both datasets indicate that the proposed exposure interpolation
can provide useful intermediate-exposure information beyond
the two original LER inputs, thereby facilitating the generation
of fused images with improved structural fidelity. This exper-
iments further demonstrate the practical value of integrating
exposure interpolation into an existing fusion framework.
All seven algorithms are then subjectively compared. Readers
are encouraged to zoom in on the electronic version of the
figure to more clearly observe the differences among all
images. As shown in the top scene of Fig. 6, both GGIF
[41] and MEFNET [42] suffer from the BOR artifacts in the
sense that the floor is darker than the sky in the real world
HDR scene but it is brighter than the sky in the fused images.
The BOR artifacts are alleviated by MSF [18], DeepFuse [14],
UltraFusion [7], DiFuser [43], and our algorithm. DeepFuse
[14] has a difficulty in preserving fine details in the fused
images. UltraFusion [7] suffers from color distortion in the
bottom scene. DiFuser [43] does not preserve fine details in
the sky region of the bottom scene. All these problems are well
addressed by our algorithm. However, our algorithm could
suffer from halo artifacts because conventional filter based
MEF algorithms have a risk to produce halo artifacts.
VI. CONCLUSION REMARKS
This paper has presented a novel neural augmented exposure
interpolation algorithm for dual large-exposure-ratio (LER)
standard dynamic range (SDR) images from a high dynamic
range (HDR) real world scene. A physics-driven component
first interpolated an initial image, and a new diffusion model
based component then generates the residual high-frequency
details. In the proposed diffusion model, a new predicted
target is designed to reduce both the variation of the Pearson
correlation between the noisy data and the target and the
amplification factor of the fitting error. The proposed exposure
interpolation algorithm outperforms existing exposure interpo-

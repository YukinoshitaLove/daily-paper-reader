How do diffusion transformers (DiTs) represent image content and learn rules for reusing it to generalize?
We develop an aggregator–predictor view of DiTs as input-adaptive local denoisers, in which context
aggregation and patch prediction provide functional roles for interpreting attention and MLPs. We
first establish that, for Gaussian Markov random field (GMRF) priors, the global Bayes denoiser can be
approximated using only a subset of context patches, with error decaying exponentially in context radius.
We further show that patchwise linear denoisers, often invoked to explain diffusion generalization,
recover the empirical local denoiser with sufficiently informative aggregation. Within this view,
AdaLN-modulated attention naturally parameterizes an input- and noise-dependent aggregator, while
the MLP admits a learned key–value representation for tokenwise patch prediction. Our experiments
support this view. DiTs trained on real images exhibit noise-dependent denoising contexts resembling
those in the analytic GMRF setting, while attention mass exhibits a qualitatively corresponding
broadening pattern. In a controlled CelebA64 study, shared-memory patch predictors used in place
of DiT MLPs achieve competitive denoising and generative performance.
Finally, cross-dataset
component transplantation reveals compatibility patterns consistent with the proposed roles of QK in
spatial weighting, VO in feature transport, and MLPs in patch prediction. Together, these results
provide a component-level account of DiT denoising and motivate architecture-specific fine-tuning
and controlled intervention.

Introduction
Diffusion and flow-matching models are widely used for image generation (Song et al., 2021; Lipman et al.,
2023), with Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) becoming a standard backbone (Peebles and Xie, 2023; Esser et al.,
2024; Xie et al., 2025; Li and He, 2026). Growing evidence connects their generalization to representations of
images and rules for reusing them. Diffusion features encode semantic and geometric information (Baranchuk
et al., 2022; Tang et al., 2023), and aligning them with pretrained representations improves training (Yu et al.,
2025). Models also generate combinations absent from the training data, as shown by analyses of synthetic
hierarchical data (Okawa et al., 2023; Favero et al., 2025). How components of DiTs realize these capabilities
remains unclear.
Existing explanations connect diffusion-model generalization to geometry-adaptive harmonic or balanced
representations (Kadkhodaie et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2026), and inductive biases toward spatial locality and
translation equivariance (Kamb and Ganguli, 2025; Niedoba et al., 2025). These accounts leave open how
DiT components divide the denoising computation. Rosu et al. (2025) show that attention can implement
exact score-based denoising, but they consider whole-image tokens, leaving interactions among spatial patches
unresolved. An et al. (2025) associate DiT generalization with attention mimicking CNN-like spatial locality,
but attention can also select input-dependent, spatially non-contiguous context (Sec. 5.1). We ask: How do
attention, MLPs, and adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN) divide the work of representing image content
and learning context-dependent rules for generalization?

We address this question by proposing an aggregator–predictor view of DiTs as input-adaptive local denoisers,
supported by a statistical foundation and a set of architectural and empirical observations:
(1) A statistical foundation for local denoising. We justify local denoiser, in which each patch is denoised using
only a subset of context patches, by identifying its statistical target and establishing when it approximates
the global Bayes denoiser. Specifically, for Gaussian Markov random field (GMRF) image priors, the global
denoiser can be approximated by a radius-r local denoiser with error decaying exponentially in r (Thm. 1).
We further connect existing patchwise retrieval-based explanations of generalization to this target by showing
that, with sufficiently informative aggregation, a patchwise linear denoiser exactly recovers the empirical local
denoiser (Thm. 3).
(2) An aggregator–predictor interpretation of DiT components. We decompose the local denoising into context
aggregation followed by patch prediction (Def. 2). Under this view, the algebraic structure of AdaLN-modulated
attention implements an input- and noise-dependent aggregation rule: QK parameterizes spatial compatibility
weights, while VO transforms the corresponding features (Sec. 4.1). In turn, MLP admits a key–value memory
interpretation and operates on the resulting contextualized token (Sec. 4.2). These algebraic correspondences
are exact for the isolated branches considered in Sec. 4; in a multilayer residual stack, the proposed roles
interact and need not remain functionally separable (Appendix D.5). This view extends existing fixed
spatial-locality accounts by allowing the relevant context to depend on the image and noise level and to
include spatially non-contiguous patches. It further suggests the possibility of replacing DiT MLPs with more
interpretable memory-based components, as supported by our experiments (Sec. 5.2).
(3) Empirical evidence consistent with the proposed component roles. Across real-image datasets, DiT
sensitivity fields exhibit noise-dependent context broadening resembling the GMRF setting, while attention
mass shows a related qualitative trend (Sec. 5.1).

5.1
Real-image denoising echoes GMRF locality
An exact analysis as in Fig. 2 is unavailable for real images. Instead, we identify context sets using sensitivity
weights, which quantify how the prediction of patch m responds to perturbations of input patch l, based on
a trained DiT’s Jacobian, similar to Niedoba et al. (2025). The population denoiser’s Jacobian measures
posterior dependence between clean patches given the noisy patches (Dytso et al., 2021). Based on these
weights, we define the q-sensitivity radius as the smallest radius of a square centered on patch m containing
at least a fraction q of the total weight. The sensitivity context set contains the fewest patches carrying at
least that fraction.
Denoising sensitivity exhibits GMRF-like broadening; attention shows a related trend. For the synthetic
GMRF where the true Jacobian is available, Fig. 2(c) shows that sensitivity spreads over broader contexts
as noise increases. Denoising real images echoes this pattern: Fig. 3 shows concentrated sensitivity at low
noise and broader, sometimes disconnected contexts at higher noise, together with increasing sensitivity
radii. Notably, replacing sensitivity weights with attention mass yields analogous attention contexts and
radii, which also generally broaden with noise. Together, these observations support the GMRF setting as
a tractable reference model for denoising locality and the interpretation of attention as an input-adaptive
context aggregator. Sensitivity and attention mass measure different quantities, so the comparison concerns a
shared qualitative trend rather than identical context sets. Additional results appear in Appendix E.2.

5.2
Shared-memory predictors can replace MLPs in a controlled setting
We replace DiT MLPs with LPLD, SCA, and SMLP on CelebA64. We compare similar parameter budgets
using a common backbone architecture, with modest models and one training seed per configuration for
unconditional 64 × 64 face generation. Figure 4 reports held-out velocity loss, denoising error, and FID at
epoch 400. Three patterns appear; first, the patch-shared SCA and SMLP banks outperform the position-
specific LPLD bank at comparable parameter budgets, showing that competitive prediction need not assign
separate memory for every patch position. Second, SMLP improves on SCA, supporting unnormalized GELU
retrieval as an effective alternative to softmax. Third, SMLP with H = 8 achieves competitive denoising
and lower FID than the ordinary MLP, despite sharing its base memory across layers. Multiple predictor
heads generally improve performance, suggesting that multiple projections of the shared bank can be useful.
Together, these results demonstrate that an explicit, layer-shared memory bank can support competitive
patch prediction in this controlled setting. We note that these results by themselves do not establish that
layerwise MLP parameters generally encode a single shared memory or that this design transfers unchanged
to larger conditional DiTs. Training details and limitations are in Appendix E.3.

5.3
Component transplants support the aggregator–predictor view
Transplanting jointly adapted components probes compatibility and coadaptation. To examine compatibility
patterns in relation to the proposed component roles, we fine-tune a pretrained source model on a target
dataset. We then transplant combinations of the adapted components into the source checkpoint. When the
datasets have similar spatial structure, our framework suggests that source QK may remain compatible with
adapted VO and MLP parameters. Larger spatial shifts may be associated with stronger coadaptation. We
study twelve transfer directions between face and natural-image datasets. For clarity, we jointly fine-tune only
QK, VO, and MLP, leaving AdaLN and all other parameters fixed, assuming that the learned time conditioning
transfers across domains. Each of the eight resulting hybrids is labeled by QK/VO/MLP bits: 0 for source
weights and 1 for jointly adapted weights. These transplants probe the compatibility and coadaptation
of parameters learned jointly; they do not measure the performance obtainable by training QK-, VO-, or
MLP-only models. Separately, under the same target-domain update budget, QK-only and QK+VO-only
fine-tuning generally fall further behind joint adaptation on larger domain shifts (Appendix E.4.5).
Following work on patch self-similarity and spatial normalization (Tumanyan et al., 2022; Singh et al., 2026),
we measure spatial distance between datasets using MMD2 of patch self-similarity descriptors. We compare
each hybrid’s outputs with the fully adapted model’s using matched initial noise. We measure this agreement
using DINOv2 global features for endpoint agreement (EA) and patch self-similarity patterns for spatial
agreement (SA). Both scores assign 0 to the source and 1 to the fully adapted model. Full protocols and
results are in Appendix E.4.

Conclusion and limitations
We presented an aggregator–predictor view of diffusion transformers as input-adaptive local denoisers. Our
theory justifies local denoising under GMRF priors and connects patchwise linear denoisers to the empirical
local denoising target. Within this framework, QK determines spatial context weights, VO transports features,
and MLPs perform contextualized patch prediction. Experiments provide complementary evidence through
noise-dependent denoising contexts, competitive shared-memory predictors, and cross-domain component
compatibility patterns. Together, these findings offer a statistical and architectural perspective on DiT
denoising and motivate further work on interpretable predictors and targeted model adaptation.
Our experiments focus on modest-sized, unconditional DiTs operating on 64 × 64 images across a limited
collection of face and natural-image datasets. In particular, the shared-memory predictor comparison uses
CelebA64 with one training seed per configuration, leaving its robustness across datasets, model scales, and
training seeds unresolved. The transfer study examines twelve directions while keeping AdaLN and global
parameters fixed; its component transplants probe compatibility among jointly adapted weights and do not
uniquely identify the proposed functional roles. Testing these patterns in larger conditional models, at higher
resolutions, and under broader adaptation protocols is an important next step.

A
Related Literature
Representations and compositional reuse.
Diffusion models learn internal features useful for semantic segmen-
tation and discriminative probing (Baranchuk et al., 2022; Xiang et al., 2023), and semantic and geometric
correspondence can emerge without correspondence supervision (Tang et al., 2023). Chen et al. (2025) connect
this ability to classical denoising autoencoders by simplifying diffusion models and studying which components
support self-supervised representation learning. Han et al. (2025a) provide theoretical and empirical evidence
that denoising encourages representations covering multiple data features, including features that classifiers
may neglect. REPA connects representation quality to generative training by aligning hidden states with
pretrained visual features in diffusion and flow-based transformers (Yu et al., 2025). Singh et al. (2026) further
identify the spatial structure of the alignment target as a strong predictor of generation performance.
Compositional generation provides evidence of reuse: Okawa et al. (2023) demonstrate unseen combinations in
a synthetic task, and Favero et al. (2025) analyze the learning of composition rules in a hierarchical grammar,
where longer-range dependencies require more data. The extent of this ability depends on the task; Han et al.
(2025b) find that diffusion models can learn coarse relationships while failing finer dependencies, including
reflection and lighting consistency. These findings motivate the introduction’s representation-and-reuse
framing. In our denoising account, a reuse rule has a specific meaning: it determines which context patches
supply evidence for predicting a clean patch from a new noisy image.
Memorization and generalization.
Under a finite empirical prior, the squared-error denoising objective is
minimized by a weighted average of training images, with weights determined by whole-image comparisons as in
(2). With the corresponding terminal initialization, exact reverse dynamics recover the empirical distribution
(Pidstrigach, 2022; Yoon et al., 2023; Gu et al., 2025; Biroli et al., 2024; Kamb and Ganguli, 2025). Several
explanations address why learned denoisers nevertheless generalize. Training dynamics can favor generalizing
solutions before memorization, with larger datasets delaying memorization (Bonnaire et al., 2025); large
learning rates can also steer training away from the memorizing solution (Wu et al., 2025). Other accounts
characterize the learned estimator: Kadkhodaie et al. (2024) connect generalization to geometry-adaptive
harmonic representations, while Zhang et al. (2026) prove a distinction between sample storage and balanced
representations of local statistics for a two-layer ReLU denoising autoencoder, and investigate analogous
structure in larger models. Our framework concerns how DiT components implement denoising computations
compatible with such statistical structure.

Local denoising and its statistical target.
Local empirical denoisers explain novel generation through patch
recombination. Niedoba et al. (2025) use cropped neighborhoods to approximate trained denoisers; Kamb
and Ganguli (2025) derive estimators from locality and translation equivariance; and Lukoianov et al. (2025)
choose context from the sensitivity of the optimal linear denoiser, emphasizing the role of data statistics.
These constructions motivate the patchwise linear denoiser (PLD) in Section 2. In (3), a softmax comparison
through Sm,t determines how stored training patches x(i)

hence permit recombination.
Our statistical contribution identifies a population target for this construction. Theorem 1 bounds the
discrepancy between population local and global denoisers under a GMRF prior. Theorem 3 identifies when
sufficient aggregation lets a PLD recover the empirical local conditional mean exactly; this identity alone
is not a finite-sample bound on population risk. Together, the results motivate learning both the context
aggregator and the patch predictor. The stored patches and context comparisons in the PLD provide an
explicit example of content and reuse; Section 4 develops their learned counterparts in a DiT.
Architectural accounts of denoising.
Mei (2025) show how U-Net encoder–decoder paths and skip connections
can implement belief-propagation denoising in hierarchical graphical models, providing a direct precedent
for interpreting architecture through a statistical denoising algorithm. For transformers, Rosu et al. (2025)
show that attention can implement exact score-based denoising; their image experiments use whole-image
tokens, although an anisotropic extension permits patchwise pixel updates. An et al. (2025) associate DiT
generalization with local attention in early layers and show benefits from restricting attention windows with
limited training data. Gan et al. (2025) link concentrated large feature activations to AdaLN and use channel
modulation to improve DiT features for correspondence. These works explain architectural or conditioning
mechanisms relevant to denoising and representation learning. Our account connects attention, MLPs, and
AdaLN through context aggregation followed by patch prediction, allowing context to vary with the input
and noise level, including the non-contiguous patterns in Section 5.1.
MLPs as learned memory.
Language-model MLPs have been interpreted as key–value memories that store
and retrieve learned associations (Petroni et al., 2019; Geva et al., 2021, 2022); Sukhbaatar et al. (2019) express
them as attention over persistent memory vectors. This interpretation has motivated locating and editing
facts through MLP weights (Dai et al., 2022; Meng et al., 2022, 2023), with related analyses and interventions
in multimodal models (Schwettmann et al., 2023; Pan et al., 2024a; Basu et al., 2024a; Pan et al., 2024b).
At the level of the generative process, Hoover et al. (2023) survey connections between diffusion dynamics
and retrieval in energy-based associative memories. Our DiT account assigns a memory role specifically to
the MLP’s patch prediction from aggregated context (Section 4.2). Replacing layerwise MLPs with explicit
memory shared across layers tests this interpretation at the level of the predictor component (Section 5.2).
The representation may be distributed across learned memory entries; the interpretation does not require
individual entries to correspond to named concepts.
Localization, editing, and component interventions.
Several analyses of text-to-image diffusion localize visual
attributes in text-encoder self-attention or U-Net cross-attention (Basu et al., 2024c,b), including neurons
associated with concepts or memorized training images (Liu et al., 2023; Hintersdorf et al., 2024). For DiTs,
Zarei et al. (2025) identify blocks associated with specific concepts and use localized updates for personalization
and unlearning. Their component ablations also intervene on self-attention, normalization, and MLP outputs,
with cross-attention giving the strongest localization signal in the tested PixArt setting. Thus, component
intervention already has direct precedents in DiTs.
Related editing methods illustrate the practical value of these distinctions. Custom Diffusion adapts selected
parameters for concept customization (Kumari et al., 2023); Plug-and-Play Diffusion Features injects spatial
features and self-attention to preserve layout during translation (Tumanyan et al., 2023); and Shin et al. (2025)
analyze text–image attention blocks to design editing interventions for multimodal DiTs. Turri et al. (2026)
further show that transferring high-magnitude hidden-state channels between prompt-conditioned trajectories
can steer image semantics in DiT and related flow-based models. These activation interventions complement
our parameter transplants, which ask which QK, VO, and MLP computations remain compatible across image
17


domains, guided by the aggregator–predictor account (Section 5.3). The transplants probe jointly adapted
components with AdaLN fixed, so their outcomes measure reuse and coadaptation under that intervention
protocol (Appendix E.4).
Gaussian and Markov image priors.
The GMRF prior in Theorem 1 combines joint Gaussian statistics
with conditional independence between non-neighboring patches given their graph neighbors. Markov image
models developed from spatial statistics (Besag, 1974) into methods for restoration, texture modeling, and
segmentation (Geman and Geman, 1984; Li, 2009), with higher-order models extending interactions to larger
neighborhoods and patches (Roth and Black, 2005; Weiss and Freeman, 2007; Chen et al., 2014). Gaussian
MRFs (Rue and Held, 2005) have been used for texture modeling (Chellappa and Chatterjee, 1985), Bayesian
restoration (Shouno, 2015; Yasuda et al., 2018; Monma et al., 2022), and learned conditional denoising (Tappen
et al., 2007). The Gaussian optimal denoiser is the Wiener filter (Wiener, 1949; Andrews and Hunt, 1977); its
tractability also supports exact analyses of diffusion sampling (Pierret and Galerne, 2025) and approximations
to learned scores at high noise (Wang and Vastola, 2024). We use this tractable prior with patches as variables
on an arbitrary graph to establish a locality guarantee, and examine the corresponding qualitative patterns
on real images separately.

We emphasize that (28) motivates interpreting the residual stack as iterative denoising in feature space:

projection and positional embedding are applied once at network entry; later layers operate on already
contextualized tokens. Although the shared-channel-operator decomposition of attention still holds layerwise,
a token can depend on many original patches, and VO or predictor updates can alter later QK routing. The
division of labor between QK and VO is therefore less clear-cut than in a single layer. Similarly, the MLP in
one layer can affect attention in subsequent layers, and attention can in turn affect subsequent MLP updates.

E.3
Experiment 2: Replacing MLPs with shared-memory predictors
E.3.1
Motivation
Our aggregator–predictor framework decomposes the local conditional mean into a local aggregator and a
patch predictor (Def. 2). Sections 4.1 and 4.2 associate these operations with attention and the DiT MLP,
respectively. This interpretation motivates replacing the MLP with more interpretable predictors based
on retrieval from a reusable bank of learned representations, as introduced in Sec. 4.2, with the aim of
attaining comparable denoising and generative performance at a similar trainable-parameter budget. If the
proposed alternatives were to fail to do so, this would indicate that their representation or training omits
useful properties of an ordinary MLP for denoising. Conversely, a successful replacement provides evidence
that the framework supports a useful, explicitly structured alternative. Additionally, this comparison lets
us examine the effects of three design choices on empirical performance: patch-specific versus patch-shared
storage, softmax cross-attention versus unnormalized GELU retrieval, and a base memory shared across layers
versus independent layerwise memory. We take a baseline DiT and replace only its patch predictor with an
alternative predictor, leaving everything else fixed; the following section provides the details.

Scope.
We close by noting the scope of this experiment. It is a controlled study of unconditional face
generation at 64 × 64 resolution, with modest model sizes and one training seed per configuration. These
limits constrain how broadly the findings of our experiment can be applied. We therefore do not claim that
all DiT MLPs should share memory. Instead, the results support considering an explicit shared memory
architecture when a common representation bank would be useful for inspection or future intervention. Our
experiment shows that this architecture is feasible at least in the setting studied here.

E.4
Experiment 3: Component compatibility in cross-dataset adaptation
Motivation and design.
The aggregator–predictor framework separates forming a patch’s local context from
predicting its clean value using learned representations. Section 4.1 associates the first operation with attention
and Sec. 4.2 associates the second with the MLP. Experiment 1 finds that DiT sensitivity and attention maps
exhibit related noise-dependent broadening on real images; Experiment 2 shows that explicit retrieval from a
shared memory supports competitive denoising and generation quality in a controlled CelebA64 study. We
now ask whether component compatibility across image domains is consistent with the proposed attention
and MLP roles. This question also matters when target data or adaptation compute are limited: fine-tuning
methods must decide which pretrained parameters to update, and the best choice can depend on the source
and target distributions (Kumari et al., 2023; Choi et al., 2024).
The attention decomposition in (7) gives a more specific hypothesis. Within an isolated branch, QK controls
spatial weights and VO transforms and transports features; the MLP admits a key–value representation for
tokenwise updates. This motivates the hypothesis that source QK may remain compatible with adapted
VO and MLP parameters when source and target have similar spatial context, with larger shifts associated
with reduced compatibility. Source-QK compatibility may also be directional: a broadly trained source may
support contextual relations needed by a narrower target, whereas the reverse transfer need not hold. We
probe these hypotheses by adapting QK, VO, and MLP jointly to each target, then replacing one or more
adapted groups with their source weights without further optimization. The eight resulting hybrids show how
much each subset of jointly learned changes matters for reproducing the jointly adapted model.
Training and endpoint selection.
We use the 20M-class DiT of Appendix E.2 on 64×64 RGB images: L = 10,
dh = 320, ˜H = 10, and MLP hidden width 1280. The twelve directions are CelebA→AAHQ/FFHQ/AFHQ-
v2/STL-10, STL-10→AAHQ/FFHQ/AFHQ-v2/CelebA, and ImageNet64→AAHQ/FFHQ/AFHQ-v2/CelebA.
Source training uses seed 0; adaptation starts from EMA checkpoints at source epochs 400, 800, and 800,
respectively, and is repeated with two adaptation seeds. Only the attention QK and VO projections along
with MLP block weights are updated jointly. Patch, position, and time embeddings, AdaLN, the output
head, and other parameters stay fixed. Freezing AdaLN keeps its learned time conditioning while holding the
denoising schedule constant; it assumes that this aspect of time dependence transfers across datasets.
Training uses the common VP objective, global batch 32, AdamW with learning rate 2 × 10−4, and a
fresh optimizer for adaptation. For source and target training-set sizes Nsrc, Ntgt, one reference epoch is
sref = min(⌊Nsrc/32⌋, ⌊Ntgt/32⌋) updates; adaptation stops by 200 reference epochs, with checkpoints every
25. For each direction, we select the checkpoint minimizing the joint model’s two-seed mean FID-50k on
the monitored target split, with earlier epochs breaking ties, and use that same reference epoch for both
seeds (Table 4). Each eight-state hybrid cube uses the selected checkpoints, EMA weights, and the same
1,024 Gaussian initial latents per state, sampled in FP32 with 50-step VP Heun. Note that since the three
architectural components are adapted jointly, these eight-state swaps measure compatibility among jointly
adapted components; they do not measure what independently fine-tuned restricted models could learn.
Scope.
Component transplantation diagnoses compatibility among parameter groups obtained by joint adap-
tation; it neither determines their performance when optimized in isolation nor uniquely assigns computation
to components in a multilayer residual network. Separate restricted-training comparisons are reported in
Appendix E.4.5. Spatial MMD, EA, and SA are descriptive proxies for dataset geometry and endpoint
agreement, not direct measurements of denoising context. The twelve transfer directions share datasets and
pretrained sources, and the component contrasts share cube terms; their correlations are descriptive rather
than independent-sample estimates of a population relationship.

independent fine-tuning restricted to QK or QK+VO, using the same target-domain update budget (Figs. 16–

fine-tuning generally lag further behind on larger domain shifts under the tested training protocol and update

LARGE-SIZE MULTIVARIATE SCALE-FREE TEXTURE GENERATION FROM
SMALL-SIZE LEARNING SETS WITH SELF-ATTENTION WASSERSTEIN-GAN
Jhonatan Ancco Cerda, Kinan Abbas, Stephane G. Roux and Patrice Abry
CNRS, ENS de Lyon, LPENSL, UMR5672, 69342 Lyon Cedex 07, France
{firstname.lastname}@ens-lyon.fr
ABSTRACT
Multivariate scale-free texture images are nowadays central in
numerous modern applications very different in nature.
A com-
mon challenging issue consists of being able to generate such mul-
tivariate textures at any arbitrary size, from a learning set of (pos-
sibly much) smaller size. Parametric models often turn out to be
too restrictive for the generation of realistic textures, thus prompt-
ing neural network training-based generation. While several neural
network approaches propose generation at sizes larger than training,
they do not explicitly assess the quality of the generation by quan-
tifying how well multiscale or scale-free statistics in textures are
reproduced and rather rely on perceptual assessment. The present
work combines convolutional Wasserstein-GAN architectures with
self-attention layers to generate textures of arbitrary size while fo-
cusing on reproducing multiscale statistics, without coding a model
into the loss function. The relevance of using a self-attention layer
is studied. Quantitative assessment conducted on multifractal tex-
tures, representative of a broad class of multiscale textures, over ten
independent trainings shows that the proposed architecture is able to
generate textures with accurate statistics for sizes 8 × 8 larger than
the originals. The results show that placing self-attention in the critic
provides the most consistent performance across output sizes.
Index Terms— Wasserstein GAN, self-attention layer, large
size extrapolation, scale-free textures
1. INTRODUCTION
Context. Textures, i.e., images with no geometry and thus char-
acterized by their joint statistical distributions, have always been
considered essential in image processing. Nowadays, in most recent
and modern applications, with the multiplication of sensors, most
textures are multivariate (several measured textures characterize
the system of interest) and multiscale (their statistics follow some
evolution rule with respect to the analysis scale). For instance, mul-
tivariate scale-free textures have statistics that are jointly covariant
under dilation of the analysis scale [2]. Classical stochastic process-
based parametric models usually suffer from not being versatile
enough for the generation of rich enough textures, often limited to
Gaussian statistics, or too rigid non Gaussian models. This triggered
massive neural network training based generation, yet not often
considering situations when only small size ground truth images
are available. Further, most works do not usually explicitly seek to
reproduce multiscale or scale-free non Gaussian statistics and rely

mostly on perceptual rather than quantitative performance assess-
ment. The present work aims to address such limitations.
Related work.
Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN), intro-
duced in [3], marked a major advance in image synthesis and
demonstrated strong performance in capturing geometric and se-
mantic structures [4]. GAN for texture generation has also been
explored [5].
The synthesis of spatially extended textures from
observations available only on a smaller spatial support has a long
history.
Classical non-parametric methods construct arbitrarily
large realizations by resampling pixels or patches from a reference
texture [6]. Neural approaches replaced explicit resampling with
learned representations [7], while convolutional GAN permit gen-
eration with arbitrary spatial dimensions [8, 9, 10]. Most of these
works, however, rely on local or fixed-scale statistics [11, 12] and
are thus not well suited to reproducing multiscale statistics.
Recently, diffusion models have renewed interest in generation
beyond the spatial dimensions of the training set [13, 14, 15]. How-
ever, much of such works focus on geometric images, do not con-
sider multiscale textures (see a contrario [16]) and rely on per com-
ponent (rather than multivariate) perceptual quality, semantic con-
sistency, and avoidance of duplicated objects.
Goals, Contributions, and Outline. The goal of this work is to de-
sign and assess fully convolutional Wasserstein-GAN architectures
trained on images of size NT ×NT and able to generate multivariate
scale-free textures of arbitrary size N ×N, with N > NT . The main
contribution is to assess the ability of these architectures to preserve
multiscale and cross-component statistical properties when gener-
ating textures larger than those used for training, and to study the
impact of placing self-attention in the generator, in the critic, or in
both networks. The proposed architectures are detailed in Section 2.
In this work, multivariate multifractal textures are used as ref-
erence models for scale-free textures because they exhibit strongly
non-Gaussian statistics, rich component-wise distributions, and
complex inter-component dependencies. They are not considered
here for their specific applications, but rather as well-documented
reference models for multivariate multiscale textures.
Their def-
initions and analysis are recalled in Section 3.
Their statistical
properties are assumed not known and not involved in the loss
used for training. Section 4 presents the assessment protocol and
compares the statistical performance of the different self-attention
placements.

Spatial-support extrapolation. The classical WGAN architecture
suffers from a major restriction: the fully connected layer, used as
the first layer of the generator, imposes that generated textures have
one same and unique size, that of the training set.
For univariate unidimensional time series, it was proposed in
[10] to replace that fully-connected input layer by a convolutional
layer. This results in a convolutional generator for which the spa-
tial support of the input noise can be changed at inference time:
The learned parameters can be used to generate samples on supports
larger than those available during training.
We extend this principle here from one-dimensional univariate
time series to two-dimensional multivariate textures. The present
work explores the potential critical impact of the convolutional ker-
nel size in the performance of the proposed generator. Compared
performance are reported and discussed in Section 4.

In the generator, self-attention is inserted immediately after the

4. PERFORMANCE ASSESSMENT
4.1. Experimental setup
Training dataset. The dataset consists of 10, 000 independent bi-
variate (M = 2) MMRW images of size 128×128, of which 8, 000
are used for training and 2, 000 for validation. The images are gener-
ated using the circular matrix embedding toolbox described in [28].
The two components have different marginal multifractal properties,
with self-similarity parameters H = [0.8, 0.6] and multifractality
parameters λ = [
√
0.02,
√
0.04]. Inter-component dependencies are
set to ρ1,2 = −0.4 for second-order correlations and ρmf
1,2 = 0.6 for
cross-multifractal dependencies. This setting is chosen because gen-
erating MMRW with different multifractal structures for each com-
ponent is more demanding than the settings considered in [5, 10],
since the neural network must jointly reproduce distinct component-
wise multiscale properties and inter-component dependencies. Also,
the dependence structure is challenging as the second order correla-
tion is negative (global anti-correlation) while the multifractal corre-
lation is positive (positively correlated transient or local structures).
Training settings. Four configurations are considered: a WGAN
without self-attention (W), self-attention in the generator only (SA-
G), self-attention in the critic only (SA-C), and self-attention in both
networks (SA-GC). All configurations use the same training settings.
We use Adam with learning rate 10−4, β1 = 0, and β2 = 0.9, a gra-
dient penalty coefficient λGP = 10, and a batch size of 8. Five critic
updates are performed for each generator update. All configurations
are trained for 25 epochs.
Evaluation protocol. The networks are trained using 128 × 128
textures only. At inference, the spatial size Nz × Nz of the in-
put noise is varied to generate textures of size N × N, with N =
128, 256, 512, 1024, and 2048. The corresponding input noise sizes
are 2 × Nz × Nz, with Nz = 18, 34, 66, 130, and 258, respec-
tively. For each configuration, training run, and output size, 25 tex-
tures are generated. The multiscale statistics introduced in Section 3
are computed and used to estimate c1, c2, ρ, and ρmf. To account
for training variability, the complete experiment is repeated over 10
independent trainings from scratch, using the same training set and
training conditions. Bias, RMSE, and standard deviation are com-
puted for each training and then averaged over the 10 trainings.

Architecture design and complexity. A comparison of 3×3, 9×9,
and 15 × 15 first-layer convolutional kernel sizes showed that the
3 × 3 kernel provided the best overall validation performance while
requiring the lowest parameter and computational costs. Detailed
results are omitted due to space constraints, and all reported perfor-
mances use the 3 × 3 kernel. This indicates that a small receptive
field is sufficient to reproduce local bursty bivariate structures.
Spatial-support extrapolation performance. Table 1 compares the
statistical performance of the four attention configurations over the
different generated texture sizes. Among the 30 combinations of out-
put size and statistical descriptor, SA-C achieves the lowest RMSE
in 15 cases and ties for the lowest RMSE in one additional case. Its
advantage is particularly clear at N = 1024, where it provides the
lowest RMSE for five of the six descriptors. The models generate
textures up to N = 1024, eight times the training size along each
spatial dimension, with good overall agreement across the evaluated
descriptors. At N = 2048, the RMSE increases for the first-order
scaling coefficients and the wavelet correlation in all configurations.

5. CONCLUSIONS AND PERSPECTIVES
This work addressed the generation of multivariate multifractal
scale-free textures on spatial supports larger than those available
during training. A fully convolutional WGAN trained on 128 × 128
textures reproduces the targeted multiscale and cross-statistical
properties up to 1024 × 1024, without including these properties
in the training loss. Among the evaluated self-attention placements,
critic-only attention provides the most consistent performance while
introducing no additional cost during generation.
Performance
degradation at 2048 × 2048 indicates the current extrapolation
limit. Future work will consider higher-dimensional textures and
statistically informed learning objectives.

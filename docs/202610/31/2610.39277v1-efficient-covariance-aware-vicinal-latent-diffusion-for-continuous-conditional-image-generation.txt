# Efficient Covariance-Aware Vicinal Latent Diffusion for Continuous Conditional Image Generation 

Xin Ding, Shuo Huang, Zhenglong Ding, Sen Zhang, Nenglun Chen, Peibei Cao, Yongwei Wang, and Fei Wu 

Abstract —Continuous Conditional Generative Modeling (CCGM) aims to synthesize high-dimensional data conditioned on continuously valued scalar labels. However, existing continuous diffusion models face severe data sparsity under imbalanced and long-tailed label distributions, which can lead to visual artifacts and condition inconsistency. Moreover, inference-time classifier-free guidance and dense pixel-space integration introduce substantial sampling overhead, limiting practical deployment. To address these challenges, we propose CAViLD, an efficient latent diffusion framework for continuous conditional generation. CAViLD first constructs a purified condition-aware latent space through density-adaptive vicinal learning, auxiliary semantic regularization, and NIQE-guided negative data augmentation. It then internalizes the conditional guidance direction through an EMA teacher, enabling single-branch conditional inference, and introduces covariance-aware progressive distillation for label-dependent probability-flow ODEs, supporting sampling down to four NFEs. Experiments on RC-49, UTKFace, and Steering Angle demonstrate a favorable trade-off among generation fidelity, continuous condition consistency, and sampling efficiency. 

Index Terms —Continuous Conditional Generation, Latent Diffusion Models, Negative Data Augmentation, Model-Guidance, Progressive Distillation 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Continuous conditional generative modeling (CCGM) aims to learn high-dimensional distributions conditioned on continu-ous scalar labels, such as viewpoints, ages, steering angles, and physical parameters [1]–[3]. It supports engineering inverse design [4]–[6], remote-sensing and hyperspectral generation [7]–[9], and point-cloud or continuously controllable image syn-thesis [10], [11]. More broadly, recent studies have emphasized the importance of information representation, transformation, and organization in the design of intelligent systems [12]–[14]. Unlike discrete class conditioning, exact continuous labels are sparsely observed in finite datasets, making local conditional distributions difficult to estimate, particularly in long-tailed regions [1], [3], [15].

Recent methods, including CCDM [3] and its matrix-form extension iCCDM [16], improve continuous conditional generation through label-dependent diffusion processes and covariance schedules. Although iCCDM reformulates DDPM-style diffusion [17], [18] within the EDM paradigm [19], both methods perform iterative reverse diffusion in pixel space, resulting in higher computational cost than latent diffusion models [5], [20]. This limitation grows at higher resolutions and restricts deployment. Moving continuous conditional diffusion into latent space raises three challenges. First, standard conditional autoencoders do not explicitly address sparse-label neighborhoods and may learn unreliable latent representations [15], [21]. Second, Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) requires conditional and un-conditional denoiser evaluations at each solver step, while large guidance scales may introduce artifacts or reduce diversity [22]– [24]. Third, few-step distillation methods are mainly developed for isotropic diffusion [25], [26] and do not explicitly account for the label-dependent anisotropic covariance of matrix-form continuous diffusion [16]. Efficient latent CCGM therefore requires jointly addressing representation quality, conditional guidance, and trajectory compression. To this end, we propose CAViLD , a Covariance-Aware Vicinal Latent Diffusion framework with internalized guidance for continuous conditional generation, building upon the arXiv:2610.39277v1 [cs.CV] 29 Oct 2026 2

matrix-form formulation of iCCDM [16]. First, we learn a compact conditional latent space using a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) [21]. Density-aware vicinal learning [15] improves sample coverage in sparse label regions, while NIQE-guided Negative Data Augmentation (NDA) [11] provides explicit supervision against low-quality reconstruction modes. Second, we incorporate Model-Guidance (MG) [27] into matrix-form latent diffusion. During training, an exponential moving average teacher transfers the conditional–unconditional guidance direction into the denoising target. The trained model requires one conditional denoiser evaluation per solver step, eliminating the additional unconditional forward pass used by inference-time CFG. Third, we introduce covariance-aware progressive distillation for the probability-flow ODE [28], [29]. A condition-dependent precision-weighted objective accounts for the anisotropic covariance, and step-halving [25] compresses the trajectory to four NFEs. Experiments on RC-49, UTKFace, and Steering Angle show a favorable trade-off among generation quality, label consistency, and sampling efficiency. Compared with prior CcGAN-, CCDM-, and iCCDM-based approaches [1]–[3], [11], [15], [16], the framework jointly studies continuous conditional latent representation, CFG-free single-branch inference, and covariance-aware few-step sampling. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:  

> •

Continuous conditional latent diffusion. We formulate matrix-form continuous conditional diffusion in a com-pact CVAE latent space and adapt density-aware vicinal learning and NIQE-guided NDA to improve representation quality under imbalanced labels.  

> •

Model-guided denoising. We transfer the conditional guidance direction into the training target, eliminating the additional unconditional denoiser evaluation required by inference-time CFG.  

> •

Covariance-aware few-step distillation. We derive a precision-weighted progressive distillation objective for label-dependent probability-flow ODEs, supporting sam-pling down to four NFEs. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Continuous Conditional Image Generation 

Conditional image synthesis is well established for discrete categories [30], [31] or text prompts [20], [32], whereas extending it to Continuous Conditional Generative Modeling (CCGM) with continuously valued scalar attributes y ∈ R, such as design parameters and aspect angles [4], [7], introduces a fundamental data-sparsity problem. In a finite regression dataset, few or no observations may share an exact target label, making direct estimation of the local conditional distribution unreliable, especially in long-tailed regions [2], [3], [15]. CcGANs address this issue through vicinal empirical losses that borrow information from neighboring labels [1], [2]; Dual-NDA [11] and CcGAN-AVAR [15] further improve negative supervision and density-adaptive neighborhood construction. Diffusion alternatives, including CcDPM [5], CCDM [3], and iCCDM [16], replace adversarial optimization with iterative denoising. In particular, CCDM introduces label-dependent perturbation, while iCCDM reformulates it using a matrix-form covariance Σ(t, y ) and anisotropic diffusion dynamics. Nevertheless, long-tailed labels can still cause condition mis-alignment, and pixel-space simulation remains computationally expensive. Latent modeling reduces the per-step computational cost by performing diffusion in a compact representation space [20]. However, in continuous conditional generation, latent compression must preserve both reconstruction fidelity and fine-grained condition semantics, particularly in sparsely sampled or imbalanced label regions where the learned representation may otherwise become unreliable [15], [21]. 

B. Classifier-Free Guidance and Distillation 

Conditional diffusion commonly employs classifier guidance [33] or Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) [22], with CFG ex-trapolating conditional predictions away from an unconditional baseline. Evaluating both the conditional denoiser Dθ (zt, t, y )

and its unconditional counterpart Dθ (zt, t, ∅) at each integration point approximately doubles the network evaluations. Moreover, large CFG scales may move sampling trajectories away from the learned manifold, producing mode collapse, oversaturation, artifacts, or diversity loss [23], [24]. Model-Guidance (MG) [27] avoids the inference-time uncon-ditional branch by internalizing the conditional–unconditional direction into the training objective. Sampling acceleration has also been studied through implicit sampling [34], high-order guided solvers [35], consistency modeling [36], and progressive distillation [25]. The latter trains a student to reproduce two consecutive teacher transitions with one step. However, these methods are generally developed for scalar or isotropic noise schedules and do not account for the continuous, condition-dependent anisotropic geometry Σ(t, y ). This motivates a covariance-aware model-guided distillation formulation. III. M ETHOD 

To improve the generation quality and sampling efficiency of iCCDM [16], we extend its matrix-form continuous conditional diffusion framework from pixel space to a compact latent space. We retain the label-dependent covariance formulation, matrix-form probability-flow dynamics, covariance embedding, and denoiser preconditioning of iCCDM, while adapting its density-aware hard-vicinity principle to latent-space construc-tion. Relative to this backbone, CAViLD introduces three extensions: a purified condition-aware CVAE latent space, internalized model guidance for single-branch conditional inference, and covariance-aware progressive distillation for few-step sampling. Specifically, the CVAE is first trained and frozen; an EMA teacher then transfers the conditional–null guidance direction into the latent denoiser; finally, the resulting single-branch model is progressively distilled using covariance-aware endpoint matching. To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness and efficiency of our proposed CAViLD framework, we conduct extensive experiments on standard continuous conditional generative modeling benchmarks. We investigate three aspects: generation fidelity and continuous label consistency, sampling efficiency, and the quality of the learned latent representation under imbalanced continuous conditions. 

A. Experimental Setup 

Datasets. We evaluate our proposed CAViLD on three widely used continuous conditional generative modeling benchmarks following previous CCGM protocols [2], [16]. These datasets cover diverse continuous control scenarios, including object pose manipulation, age-conditioned face synthesis, and au-tonomous driving, with image resolutions ranging from 64 × 64 

to 256 × 256 .

Fig. 3: Effect of NDA on CVAE reconstruction quality. CVAE+NDA achieves lower NIQE across most steering-angle conditions. 

RC-49 [2] is a pose-controlled image generation benchmark containing chair images conditioned on continuous azimuth angles. It is widely adopted for evaluating continuous viewpoint-conditioned generation. UTKFace [40] consists of RGB face images with continuous age labels ranging from 1 to 60 years. The naturally imbal-anced age distribution makes it a challenging benchmark for evaluating continuous label consistency and robustness. The Steering Angle dataset [41], [42], preprocessed following Ding et al. [2], contains real-world driving images conditioned on continuous steering angles. It provides a practical physical control scenario with complex visual variations and non-uniform continuous label distributions.

Implementation Setup. 

The CVAE-based latent manifold is first trained with the proposed purification strategy and then frozen for diffusion training. The latent spatial compression factor is selected according to the image resolution, using either 4× or 8×

compression. Unless otherwise specified, the shared diffusion and optimization settings follow iCCDM [16]. For auxiliary semantic regularization, continuous labels are min–max normalized to [0 , 1] . We use dataset-independent settings across all experiments, with λaux = 1 .0 and ϵaux =0.05 , where ϵaux corresponds to a tolerance of 5% of the normalized label range. For adaptive vicinal modeling, NAV is selected per dataset according to its continuous label distribution, following CcGAN-AVAR [15]. NDA uses the worst 20% of NIQE-ranked reconstructions as negatives and follows the Dual-NDA penalty configuration [11]. During diffusion training, an EMA teacher with decay 0.9999 provides Model-Guidance targets. The coefficient wMG is an empirical training parameter, distinct from the inference-time CFG scale. The algebraic correspondence in Eq. (10) does not imply matched guidance strength for the reported MG and CFG configurations. For efficient sampling, the 32-step teacher is progressively distilled down to a 4-step student [25]. For the text-to-image baselines, we fine-tune checkpoints of SD 3 Medium, FLUX.1, Qwen-Image, and MIRO using LoRA [44] on the corresponding RGB datasets under the same evaluation protocol. 

B. Experimental Results 

We evaluate CAViLD on eight experimental settings covering three continuous conditional generation benchmarks with resolutions ranging from 64 × 64 to 256 × 256 . Quantitative comparisons are summarized in Tables I and II. 

Overall Performance. CAViLD achieves competitive or superior performance across most experimental settings, demon-strating the effectiveness of latent-space modeling, Model-Guidance, and progressive distillation for continuous condi-tional generation. Compared with pixel-space diffusion baselines, CAViLD substantially improves sampling efficiency while preserving generation fidelity. For example, on Steering Angle 64 × 64 ,our method achieves an SFID of 0.358 with 609.76 img/sec, providing a significant acceleration over iCCDM. On UTKFace 

64 × 64 , CAViLD further improves SFID from 0.353 (iCCDM) to 0.344 while achieving much higher throughput. The framework also scales well to higher resolutions. On UTKFace 256 × 256 , CAViLD achieves an SFID of 0.196, matching the strongest competitors while retaining diffusion-based controllability. On Steering Angle 256 ×256 , it obtains an SFID of 0.371 and the best Label Score among diffusion-based methods, indicating strong continuous condition adherence. Compared with GAN- and diffusion-based CCGM baselines, CAViLD offers a favorable quality–efficiency balance while substantially reducing iterative sampling cost. Overall, these results demonstrate an effective trade-off among generation fidelity, continuous condition controllability, and practical sampling efficiency. 

C. Ablation Study 

In addition to the main comparisons, we conduct ablation studies on Steering Angle and UTKFace to evaluate latent-space modeling, vicinal learning, auxiliary regularization, Model-Guidance (MG), progressive distillation, and Negative Data Augmentation (NDA). Table III shows that introducing the CVAE latent representation substantially improves both generation quality and sampling efficiency over pixel-space iCCDM, while auxiliary regularization further improves label consistency. Additional CVAE-level results in Appendix B further directly isolate the effect of vicinal learning on representation quality and label consistency. MG reduces the required NFEs by internalizing the guidance direction during training, and progressive distillation compresses the sampler from 32 to 4 steps while maintaining overall competitive quality. Further reducing the sampler to 2 steps causes clear degradation in SFID and Label Score, indicating the limit of trajectory compression. Table IV confirms that MG achieves comparable or better conditional generation with fewer NFEs than Classifier-Free Guidance, while Table VI shows a favorable quality–efficiency trade-off over LCM [45] and PCM [46]. Finally, Table V shows that NDA improves generation quality at 128 ×128 . These combined results support the joint use of the proposed components in CAViLD, with the final configurations selected primarily according to SFID and sampling efficiency. V. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we propose CAViLD, a latent diffusion frame-work for efficient continuous conditional image generation. By transferring the generation process from pixel space to a purified CVAE latent space, CAViLD improves the quality and condition awareness of the learned latent representation under sparse and imbalanced conditions. Furthermore, Model-Guidance is incorporated into the training process to eliminate the additional unconditional inference required by conventional classifier-free guidance, while progressive distillation further accelerates sampling. Extensive experiments on RC-49, UTKFace, and Steering Angle datasets with resolutions up to 256 × 256 demonstrate that CAViLD achieves a favorable balance between generation fidelity, continuous condition consistency, and sampling effi-ciency.

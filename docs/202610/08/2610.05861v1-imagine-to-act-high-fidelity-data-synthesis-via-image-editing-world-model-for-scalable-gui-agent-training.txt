## ABSTRACT 

Graphical User Interface (GUI) agents have emerged as a promising paradigm for automating complex digital workflows across diverse applications. However, training highly capable and generalizable agents fundamentally relies on massive, high-fidelity visual-action trajectories, which are notoriously difficult to acquire. While human demonstrations are unscalable, existing GUI world models rely on text descriptions or HTML rendering, discarding crucial pixel-level visual details like icons and layout styles. To address this issue, we introduce Infinite-Dreamer, a simulation-free data synthesis method powered by a pixel-level Image Editing World Model. By conceptualizing GUI transitions as image editing tasks, we leverage Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to describe action-induced UI changes as structured delta-text. We then fine-tune an image editing backbone to control-lably synthesize realistic screenshot transitions. We utilize this model to generate both single-frame visual robustness data and multi-step imaginary trajectories. To validate the effectiveness of our approach, we fine-tune the Qwen3-VL baseline solely on the synthesized data to obtain Infinite-Actor, and evaluate it on Android-World, MobileWorld, and AndroidControl-Curated benchmarks. Infinite-Actor consistently outperforms the Qwen3-VL baselines across scales: Infinite-Actor-8B improves AndroidWorld Pass@1 by +4.45 and nearly doubles the MobileWorld Pass@3 success rate, while Infinite-Actor-2B improves Pass@1 by +9.05. Code is available at https://github.com/swaydy-n/Infinite-Dreamer .

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Graphical User Interface (GUI) agents have rapidly emerged as a pivotal frontier in artificial intel-ligence, promising to seamlessly automate increasingly complex digital workflows across diverse operating systems and web applications. Unlike text-based language agents, GUI agents must perceive rich visual screens, effectively reason over layout structures, and accurately execute precise actions just like human users. To achieve robust generalizability across infinitely varied real-world digital environments, these agents must master the intricate mapping between natural language instructions, dynamic screen layouts, and sequential action executions. Ultimately, the development of such highly capable and adaptable GUI agents is fundamentally contingent upon the availability of massive, diverse, and high-fidelity annotated visual-action training data. Despite its critical importance, acquiring high-quality trajectory data annotated with explicit action intents remains exceptionally difficult. Existing efforts to obtain such data generally suffer from two major limitations: (i) Human-driven demonstration is not only financially prohibitive and labor-intensive, but it is also severely bottlenecked by environmental constraints, such as the technical friction of configuring heterogeneous device platforms and the inherent risks of exposing sensitive user privacy during the recording process; (ii) Automated environment exploration typically relies on heuristic search or random-walk strategies. Lacking a long-term semantic objective, these automated 1arXiv:2610.05861v1 [cs.CV] 5 Oct 2026 Preprint Ground Truth  HTML-vision  FireRed-Image-Edit-1.0  Infinite-Dreamer-9B (Ours)

Consequently, such exploration-based approaches heavily bias the collected data toward short, repetitive interactions, making them incapable of scaling to complex, multi-step workflows and utterly failing to maintain goal coherence over long execution sequences. Therefore, how to scalably synthesize GUI interaction trajectories while improving data efficiency has emerged as a core challenge in training advanced GUI agents. To address this challenge, the paradigm of world models offers a powerful solution for data synthesis. Fundamentally, world models are specifically designed to internalize the underlying transition dynamics of an environment, predicting subsequent states by learning the causal laws that govern how the world responds to specific actions. In the digital ecosystem, a robust GUI world model acts as an unconstrained virtual simulator, emulating how software applications evolve after a click or a scroll. While recent pioneering GUI world models, such as Qwen-AgentWorld and Code2World, attempt to simulate environments via text descriptions or HTML rendering, they inherently struggle to capture diverse layouts and fine-grained visual features, such as custom icons, banners, and dynamic animations (Figure 1). In contrast, pixel-level screenshots represent the natural, raw state space of GUIs, directly reflecting the visual reality experienced by human users. A pixel-level world model can implicitly learn the visual regularities of UI evolution, such as a loading spinner disappearing or a drop-down menu unfolding, thereby preserving both rich graphical details and complex software execution logic. Therefore, leveraging image editing models directly as GUI world models offers a more natural, direct, and high-fidelity paradigm for realistic data synthesis. To this end, we introduce Infinite-Dreamer, a high-fidelity, simulation-free data synthesis pipeline that generates pixel-level screenshots and continuous action trajectories without relying on complex and restrictive simulation environments. First, we construct a specialized image editing dataset built upon AndroidControl. By employing a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to describe the transitions of UI elements before and after an action, we generate fine-grained delta-text that precisely captures added or removed interface elements. Second, we apply fine-tuning to a generative image editing backbone. The resulting model serves as a pixel-level GUI world model, capable of editing a pre-action screenshot into a post-action screenshot conditioned on the delta-text. Finally, to demonstrate how our synthesized data enhances downstream agent performance, we generate two distinct categories of samples: (i) visual robustness data, where we augment single screenshots with diverse layouts, colors, and styles to boost the agent’s adaptability, and (ii) multi-step imaginary trajectories, where the world model continuously edits an initial screenshot to ”imagine” multi-step task executions, expanding state coverage beyond the limits of real-world datasets. To summarize, our major contributions are: • A Controllable, Pixel-Level GUI World Model. We repurpose an image editing model into a screenshot-to-screenshot GUI world model conditioned on structured delta-text. This approach effectively enables the controllable generation of diverse, high-fidelity GUI screens without relying on complex costly simulators. 2Preprint • Enhanced Data Efficiency and Generalization. We demonstrate that our synthesis model significantly boosts the utility of existing trajectories. By blending synthetic and real-world data, the trained agent generalizes far better to unseen UI layouts and novel interaction patterns, outperforming agents trained purely on real demonstrations. • Substantial Downstream Performance Gains. We train a vision-language agent, called Infinite-Actor, using our newly synthesized data. Evaluation on several challenging online benchmarks reveals substantial dramatic performance gains; notably, it nearly doubles the MobileWorld Pass@3 success rate and yields consistent Pass@1 improvements on AndroidWorld across all three model scales (up to +9.20 points). 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Synthetic Data for GUI Agents. Training GUI agents is bottlenecked by the scarcity of high-quality trajectories, since manual annotation is costly and hard to scale. A first line of work derives trajectories from active environment interaction, using random-walk or exploratory policies to collect step-level actions and state transitions, as in OS-Genesis Sun et al. (2025) and GUIrilla Garkot et al. (2026), with GUI-ReWalk Lin et al. (2025) adding reasoning-guided exploration and WebSynthesis Gao et al. (2025b) simulating transitions via an LLM world model with Monte Carlo Tree Search. A second line reuses existing human knowledge artifacts: AgentTrek harvests and filters web tutorials into structured task specifications replayed by a VLM agent Xu et al. (2025), while Video2GUI mines unlabeled Internet videos to extract grounded interaction trajectories Xiong et al. (2026). A third line trains agents in synthetic environments with verifiable rewards, combining auto-synthesized apps with code-native assertions Cao et al. (2026b), self-supervised pre-training Wu et al. (2026), few-shot demonstrations He et al. (2026), and multi-turn reinforcement learning Wang et al. (2025). In contrast, Infinite-Dreamer synthesizes pixel-level trajectories by editing screenshots directly, bypassing both environment interaction and reward design. 

World Models for Visual Environments and GUIs. World models learn environment dynamics to support downstream planning, representation learning, and synthetic rollouts. Beyond classical latent-space formulations (Hafner et al., 2022; 2024), recent general world models leverage expressive diffusion models to synthesize high-fidelity visual frames for video generation (Wang et al., 2024; Bruce et al., 2024; Xiang et al., 2024; Che et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2025a), video editing (Rigter et al., 2024), and diverse interactive environment simulation. Some simulate the environment through text or HTML rendering (Zuo et al., 2026), which discards the visual dynamics that a screenshot-based agent actually observes. Others train visual simulators from scratch: ViMo (Luo et al., 2025) builds a generative visual world model for mobile app agents, MobileDreamer (Cao et al., 2026a) uses sketch-based abstractions for efficient rollout imagination, and NeuralOS (Rivard et al., 2026) simulates OS-scale dynamics via recurrent state tracking and neural rendering. 

Image Editing Models. Recently, diffusion-based generative models have achieved photorealistic text-to-image synthesis (Rombach et al., 2022; Betker et al., 2023; Black Forest Labs, 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Brooks et al., 2023). Compared with standard text-to-image models, image editing models additionally accept one or more reference images as auxiliary conditioning signals alongside the text prompt, enabling fine-grained controlled modification of existing visuals. This paradigm has rapidly advanced with strong instruction-following editors such as FLUX.1 (Labs et al., 2025), FLUX.2 (Black Forest Labs, 2025), and Qwen-Image-Edit (Wu et al., 2025). However, we observe that while these models can synthesize photorealistic GUI screenshots, they still lack the underlying procedural knowledge of how apps actually operate within GUI environments, thereby precluding them from serving as genuine GUI world models.

## 4 EXPERIMENT 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Datasets. The world model is fine-tuned on the AndroidControl (Li et al., 2024) train split and evaluated on its validation split by GLM-4.7v model (using the template in Appendix E.1). The GUI Agent Infinite-Actor is trained solely on the Infinite-Dreamer synthesis data (composition in Appendix A.5), without using any real trajectories. We evaluate our model on AndroidWorld (Rawles et al., 2025) and MobileWorld (Kong et al., 2025), two online benchmarks that require completing end-to-end tasks in dynamic mobile environments, and on AndroidControl-Curated-Hard/Easy (Leung et al., 2025), an additional offline step-level benchmark. 

Baselines. For the world model, to evaluate it, we compare five general-purpose image editors (FLUX.1 Kontext, Step1X-Edit-v1.2, LongCat-Image-Edit, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, FireRed-Image-Edit-1.0) and the un-fine-tuned FLUX.2-Klein backbone (4B/9B), also the starting point for our Infinite-Dreamer-4B/9B; we fine-tune the latter on UI screenshot pairs with delta-text. Importantly, the un-fine-tuned backbone isolates the gain from delta-text fine-tuning, while the general-purpose editors serve as off-the-shelf alternatives. Similarly, for the GUI agent, we compare general-purpose VLMs (Claude-Sonnet-4.5, Gemini-3-Flash, MiniMax-M3, MiMo-V2.5, Kimi-K2.6), GUI-specialized GUI-Owl-1.5 (2B/4B/8B), and base-model Qwen3-VL (2B/4B/8B). Implementation details for all baselines are in Appendix A.7. 

Metrics. For world-model synthesis quality, we report four VLM-judged dimensions: Accuracy (Acc.), Completeness (Comp.), Contextual alignment (Cont.), and Visual preservation (Vis.), measur-ing whether the change is correctly applied, contextually sensible, and leaves unedited regions intact. For the GUI agent, we evaluate from two complementary perspectives: task success rate in dynamic environments and step-level accuracy on static datasets. Detailed evaluation protocols and world-model inference configurations are in Appendices A.6 and A.2. 4.2 WORLD MODEL QUALITY 

Single-step Synthesis Fidelity. As Table 2 shows, Infinite-Dreamer-9B leads on all four metrics, outperforming both the unfine-tuned FLUX.2 Klein 9B backbone and the strongest baseline, FireRed-Image-Edit-1.0. The 4B variant also surpasses its FLUX.2 Klein 4B counterpart, confirming that delta-text-guided fine-tuning on UI data improves instruction fidelity and layout preservation. Qualitative single-step editing comparisons against all baselines are shown in Appendix F.1.

## 5 CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we present Infinite-Dreamer, a simulation-free data synthesis framework leveraging an image-editing world model for scalable GUI agent training. By conditioning generation on structured delta-text, our framework controllably synthesizes screenshot transitions and multi-step rollouts. Extensive experiments demonstrate that this synthetic supervision substantially enhances agent gener-9Preprint alization, yielding large task-level gains for Infinite-Actor on AndroidWorld and MobileWorld. More broadly, our work highlights a promising direction where generative world models and autonomous agents co-evolve: high-fidelity world models expand state coverage, while agents identify transitions critical for long-horizon competence. This paper uses large vision-language models as instruments inside the proposed method. Concretely, GLM-4.7v serves as the delta-text extractor that produces the conditioning descriptions (Section 3.2) and as the judge for the synthesis-quality metrics (Appendix E.1), and Qwen3.5-397B-A17B serves as the quality filter applied to generated trajectories (Appendix E). All of these components are described in the Method and in Appendix A, and every model output that enters the training data is filtered and was reviewed by the authors. This paper studies data synthesis for GUI agent training. All screenshots and trajectories used in the ex-periments come from the public AndroidControl, AndroidWorld, MobileWorld, and AndroidControl-Curated benchmarks. We collect no new human data and conduct no user studies, so no IRB approval was required. The synthesized screenshots are derived from these public sources and will be released under a research-permissive license. Two risks deserve note. First, synthesized screenshots inherit the distribution of their source data, so the application coverage and language mix of our synthesized corpus reflect those of AndroidControl; agents trained on it may therefore generalize unevenly across regions and applications. Second, a world model that predicts GUI transitions at pixel level is dual-use, since the same capability that supports agent training can also automate harmful workflows. We restrict the study to standard mobile-assistant benchmarks and release only the synthesized screenshots together with the generation scripts, not any device-control harness. A.9 CONCEPTUAL DISENTANGLEMENT : W ORLD MODEL VS . C ONDITIONAL GUI R ENDERER 

A central conceptual question is whether our image-editing model acts merely as a conditional graphic renderer rather than a genuine world model. We systematically distinguish between these two paradigms across operational mechanisms, representations, and functional roles in agent training, summarized in Table 9. 

1. Coordinate-Free Spatial Generalization. Conventional graphical rendering pipelines (e.g., Blink, Gecko, or the Android ViewServer) are deterministic rasterization engines. They require com-plete, unambiguous specifications of every visual element, including exact screen coordinates, width, height, margin, padding, border radius, and color codes. In stark contrast, delta-text conditioning provides only high-level semantic intent (e.g., “Added: search results dropdown; Removed: search suggestion pill”). The image-editing model fϕ must infer where and how to render these elements: it predicts valid spatial coordinates, typography, responsive element reflows, and background dimming purely from visual context. This requires internalizing the underlying spatial grammar and visual physics of digital software. 

2. Causal State Persistence and Invariance. A critical requirement of an environmental world model is state persistence: when an action affects a localized region of the screen, the rest of the environment must remain causally invariant. A conventional renderer treats every render pass as an evaluation of the full DOM. In contrast, fϕ conditions on the prior visual observation st and updates only causally relevant regions, so UI areas unrelated to the action (e.g., device status bars, system navigation icons, background cards) stay visually consistent with the previous frame. 

3. Hierarchical Decoupling in World Modeling. Rather than conflating high-level task logic with low-level pixel synthesis, our framework adopts a hierarchical world modeling formulation:

## E QUALITY FILTERING DETAILS 

To retain only trajectories whose synthesized screenshots are actionable, we apply a VLM-based quality filter. For each generated step the VLM receives the task goal, the task progress, the intended step action, a reference action type, and the synthesized screenshot, and must emit a mobile use 

tool call of that same action type together with a confidence score from 0 to 100 reflecting how clearly the screenshot supports executing the action. Steps with low confidence—screenshots that lack the UI elements needed to carry out the reference action—are discarded, and trajectories containing such steps are removed from the training set. We set the threshold to η = 90 (on a 0–100 scale), discarding steps whose confidence falls below this value. The full filter template is included below. 31 Preprint

From Measured Outcomes to Mechanisms. Table 12 describes how Hard episodes fail, not which property of the synthetic corpus caused the failure. We consider four candidate mechanisms; each would require a controlled study to confirm. First, coordinate displacement : on AC-Curated, Infinite-Actor-4B’s predicted regions are displaced from the annotations rather than marginally noisy, and the loss concentrates on compact targets (Section 4). That comparison changes both fine-tuning and the data sources, so it is consistent with, but does not isolate, an effect of synthetic supervision; separating boundary changes in the generated screenshots from the policy’s visual encoding would require a controlled study. Its behavioural symptom in the Hard logs would be ineffective clicks, which occur in 3/17 failures. Second, long-horizon causal noise : world-model fidelity decays with rollout length (Table 2), and only 9.3% of trajectories that reach step 10 survive quality filtering intact (Table 11), so long synthetic rollouts are both scarce and biased toward easy horizons. A policy trained on such supervision can adopt over-optimistic beliefs about progress and termination, which is consistent with the dominance of budget exhaustion and false completion above. Third, 

dense-UI confusion : as shown in Figure 11b, the world model can merge adjacent widgets or misplace insertions in tightly packed layouts, which in principle teaches incorrect element attributes. Fourth, 

transient system-dialog inconsistency : ephemeral runtime permission dialogs and system keyboards are unlikely to appear in imagined screenshots, so a policy trained on such data may mishandle them on a real device. The evaluation logs record actions but not whether a dialog was on screen, and they contain no element-level annotations, so the third and fourth mechanisms are not measurable from them and remain hypotheses. 

Constructive Mitigation Strategies. These findings point to actionable avenues for advancing generative GUI world models: • Termination calibration and progress verification: Since false completion accounts for 35 .3% of Hard failures, training should verify task post-conditions before accepting a 

terminate call, for example by re-checking the environment state or by filtering synthetic trajectories whose terminal state was never observed in real data. • Box-preserving attention guidance: Incorporating explicit bounding-box coordinate losses or masking during world-model fine-tuning can anchor element boundaries more tightly and reduce the coordinate displacement of predicted regions. • Keyframe re-anchoring in long horizons: Since only 9.3% of rollouts that reach step 10 remain fully valid, periodically re-anchoring the visual state to real seed screenshots or a template library can prevent compounding visual and causal drift before it exhausts the horizon. 43 Preprint (a) Long-horizon drift.

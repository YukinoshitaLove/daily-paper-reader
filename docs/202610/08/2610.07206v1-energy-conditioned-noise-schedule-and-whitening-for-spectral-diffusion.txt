A B S T R A C T 

This paper introduces an energy-adaptive noise scheduling and whitening strategy for transform-domain diffusion models. Existing spectral diffusion methods account for the non-uniform statistics of transform coefficients through coefficient scaling, normalization, or frequency prioritization, while the forward diffusion noise schedule remains largely independent of the underlying spectral-energy distribution. We investigate whether the temporal evolution of the forward diffusion process should also follow the spectral organization of natural images. The proposed formulation combines global spectral whitening with energy-conditioned noise allocation that jointly modulates the injected noise according to the energy of individual transform coefficients and an image-dependent energy path over diffusion time. The resulting forward process preserves Gaussian transitions with closed-form marginals and remains compatible with standard DDPM and DDIM procedures without modifying the diffusion architecture. Experiments on CIFAR-10 demonstrate the contribution of the proposed energy-conditioned noise schedule and spectral whitening, reducing Fréchet Inception Distance from 142.48 for a compact DCTdiff U-Net variant to 100.45. 

## 1. Introduction 

Denoising diffusion models [1–3] formulate image gen-eration as the reverse of a stochastic process that progres-sively transforms data into Gaussian noise. Denoising Dif-fusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) [2] and Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) [3] provide stochastic and deterministic realizations of this principle, respectively, while continuous-time formulations have been investigated in [4]. Most existing diffusion models operate directly on pixel representations, where the forward diffusion process applies an identical noise schedule to every image coordi-nate. Recently, several studies have investigated diffusion in transform-domain representations, including the Fourier [5, 6] and discrete cosine transform (DCT) [7] domains [8, 9]. Unlike pixel representations, transform coefficients pos-sess well-defined spectral meaning and exhibit highly non-uniform statistical properties. In natural images, most of the signal energy is concentrated in a relatively small number of low-frequency DCT coefficients, whereas higher-frequency components contain substantially less energy. Consequently, the transform domain is characterized by a pronounced imbalance between spectral components.

Recent transform-domain diffusion models explicitly ac-count for this imbalance. DCTDiff [8] introduces coeffi-cient scaling, signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) scaling, and fre-quency prioritization to improve optimization in the DCT domain. Moreover, the authors show that image diffusion exhibits a spectral autoregressive behavior, indicating that transform coefficients contribute unequally throughout the diffusion process. These observations suggest that spectral diffusion fundamentally differs from pixel-domain diffusion and should exploit the underlying spectral organization of natural images. Motivated by these observations, we investigate a com-plementary question that, to the best of our knowledge, has not been addressed previously: should the forward diffusion noise schedule itself depend on the spectral-energy distri-bution? Existing transform-domain diffusion methods adapt the representation, coefficient scaling, or optimization strat-egy, whereas the temporal evolution of the forward diffusion process remains largely independent of the spectral energy associated with individual transform coefficients. This paper introduces an energy-conditioned noise schedul-ing and whitening strategy for transform-domain diffusion models. Rather than proposing a new diffusion architec-ture, the objective is to investigate spectral-energy-aware noise scheduling as an independent component of exist-ing transform-domain diffusion frameworks. The proposed schedule depends on diffusion time, coefficient energy, and image-dependent spectral energy, assigning smaller perturbations to dominant spectral components while mod-ulating their temporal evolution according to the spectral-energy distribution of each image. The resulting formulation preserves the standard Gaussian diffusion framework and

## 2. Baseline Spectral Diffusion Framework 

This section introduces the baseline transform-domain diffusion framework adopted throughout this work. Follow-ing the JPEG coding pipeline [10] and the block-DCT repre-sentation introduced in DCTdiff [8], the image is converted into compact spectral coefficients before diffusion. Unlike DCTdiff, which employs a Transformer back-bone, the present work uses the resulting spectral repre-sentation as the input to a conventional U-Net diffusion model. This baseline serves as the reference framework upon which the proposed Energy-Conditioned Spectral Diffusion (ECSdiff) is introduced in the next section.

## 3. Energy-Conditioned Spectral Diffusion 

The baseline framework described in the previous sec-tion follows the standard DDPM formulation with a uni-form forward noise schedule. Recent theoretical analysis of transform-domain diffusion has shown that the diffusion process inherently exhibits spectral autoregressive behav-ior, whereby high-frequency components progressively lose their signal-to-noise ratio earlier than low-frequency com-ponents under isotropic Gaussian perturbations [8]. This behavior follows directly from the power-law energy profile of natural images and provides a theoretical justification for treating individual spectral components differently during diffusion. Rather than modifying the diffusion architecture, the proposed ECSdiff explicitly incorporates this observation into the forward process by introducing an energy-conditioned noise schedule. The proposed formulation consists of three consecutive stages. First, the compact spectral representa-tion is whitened to reduce the statistical imbalance between spectral components. Second, the spectral-energy distribu-tion is estimated from the original transform coefficients. Finally, the forward noise schedule is modulated according to the estimated spectral energy.

DCTdiff compensates for spectral imbalance through Entropy-Consistent Scaling (ECS), Entropy-Based Frequency Reweighting (EBFR), and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) scal-ing.

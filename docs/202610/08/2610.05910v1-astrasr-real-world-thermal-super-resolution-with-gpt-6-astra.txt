# AstraSR: Real-World Thermal Super-Resolution with GPT-6 Astra 

Mengyuan Li 1, Changhong Fu 1,∗, Jun Zhang 2, Ziyu Lu 1, Yuhang Zhang 1, and Haobo Zuo 3

Abstract —Real-world thermal super-resolution (SR) is con-strained by limited sensor resolution and the difficulty of obtaining corresponding high-resolution (HR) observations for direct model supervision. Conventional SR methods typically construct training pairs by treating captured thermal images with real-world degradations as HR references and applying predefined degradation to generate synthetic low-resolution (LR) inputs. Such a construction not only introduces a domain gap between synthetic and captured LR observations but also retains acquisition degradations in the supervision. To address this issue, we propose AstraSR, a real-world thermal SR method guided by GPT-6 Astra, a frontier multimodal generative model endowed with emergent and transformative visual capabilities. Specifically, we construct a dataset of image pairs by using captured LR thermal images to condition GPT-based HR reference. We develop a direct generative supervision strategy that learns from captured thermal inputs paired with GPT-generated HR refer-ences. Pixel, gradient, and perceptual losses jointly supervise the transfer of intensity patterns, structural boundaries, and visual details from the generated references. Qualitative comparisons with seven existing state-of-the-art real-world SR methods show continuous object contours, distinct structural boundaries, and smooth intensity transitions in the thermal scenes. These results demonstrate that AstraSR outperforms existing real-world SR methods in both thermal clarity and structural coherence. 

Index Terms —Real-World thermal super-resolution, multi-modal foundation models, physics-informed hallucination sup-pression, cross-modal generative prior, GPT Astra-powered UAV perception. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# THERMAL imaging provides useful scene information when visible illumination is limited or thermal contrast is informative. Applications such as nighttime observation, search and rescue, and infrastructure inspection benefit from being able to distinguish references and their surrounding structures [1], [2]. However, the available spatial detail is constrained by the thermal sensor and imaging optics. During image acquisition, motion, vibration, and sensor noise can further obscure boundaries that already occupy few pixels. Enlarging such images by interpolation increases their display size but does not resolve the underlying ambiguity. Therefore, learning to recover reliable high-resolution (HR) details from real captured thermal observations is essential for practical thermal super-resolution (SR).

Supervised single-image SR learns a mapping from a low-resolution (LR) image to a HR reference. Convolutional models established the effectiveness of learning this mapping directly, and subsequent residual and attention-based networks increased its representational capacity [3]–[5]. In a common training formulation, a predefined operator produces the LR input from an HR image, with bicubic downsampling pro-viding a convenient and reproducible example. As illustrated in Fig. 1(a), applying this construction to captured thermal images makes the original image the HR reference and its downsampled version the LR input. Noise and blur in that original image are therefore also present in the reference. Although this formulation yields well-aligned training pairs, the learned mapping remains constrained by the assumed degradation process and the quality of the captured reference. Hence, it is imperative to construct training pairs that faith-fully preserve the characteristics of captured thermal inputs throughout training [6], [7]. Real-world SR extends this formulation to more complex and unknown input degradations. Existing approaches gen-erally improve reconstruction robustness by broadening syn-thetic degradation distributions [8], [9], incorporating learned high-quality image priors [10], [11], or exploiting genera-arXiv:2610.05910v1 [cs.CV] 5 Oct 2026 2

tive models for perceptual reconstruction [12]–[15]. These advances substantially broaden the available approaches to processing real photographs. Despite this progress, transferring such methods to real-world thermal imagery presents unre-solved difficulties. First, even a broad synthetic degradation distribution may not reproduce the joint image characteristics produced by a particular thermal camera under real acquisition conditions. Second, the released models considered here were developed primarily with natural-image training data, whose appearance differs from the grayscale intensity patterns of thermal imagery. Larger generative priors do not by themselves establish that an output detail is supported by a thermal observation. Third, generating LR inputs from an HR source changes the training input distribution: the network learns from simulated observations rather than the measurements it will receive in use. Direct supervision with captured LR images would avoid this particular substitution, but obtaining aligned sensor-captured HR thermal references remains difficult. This setting calls for a teacher that can interpret the scene structure contained in a degraded thermal observation while generating a higher-resolution representation under explicit restoration instructions. GPT-6 Astra [16], as a frontier general-purpose multimodal model, combines visual understanding, instruction following, and image generation within a unified framework, making it well suited to this role. Rather than synthesizing an independent scene from text alone, Astra can condition gen-eration on the captured thermal observation and use its broad visual priors to recover structures that are weakly represented at the sensor resolution [17]. This capability motivates our use of Astra as an offline generative teacher for constructing HR supervision from captured thermal inputs. To address these challenges, we propose AstraSR, a GPT-guided method for real-world thermal SR. The main contribu-tions are as follows:  

> •

We propose AstraSR, which transfers the visual detail provided by a generative teacher to a thermal SR model. By using GPT only during offline reference construction, AstraSR enables direct inference on captured thermal images without querying the generative model.  

> •

We construct a real-world thermal SR dataset using GPT-6 Astra, pairing captured LR thermal images with corresponding GPT-generated HR images. These pairs provide supervision for learning fine structures and object boundaries from real thermal observations.  

> •

We develop a direct real-to-teacher training strategy that retains captured images as LR inputs without synthetic downsampling. The strategy combines pixel, gradient, and perceptual losses to learn the teacher-provided inten-sity patterns, local contours, and visual details for thermal SR. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Real-World Image Super-Resolution 

Real-world SR addresses inputs for which the degradation is not known in advance. A prominent approach broadens the synthetic training distribution. BSRGAN [8] shuffles blur, resizing, and noise operations, whereas Real-ESRGAN [9] models repeated degradation and includes ringing and over-shoot effects. However, expanding a synthetic degradation pipeline does not ensure that its training inputs reproduce the sensor-dependent characteristics of real captured ther-mal images. Learned high-quality image priors complement degradation modeling. FeMaSR [11] represents such a prior with a vector-quantized codebook and reconstructs images by matching degraded features to codebook entries. Real-world variants of SwinIR [10] use an attention-based back-bone within a perception-oriented training setting. Diffusion methods provide a further family of real-world SR models. ResShift [12] constructs a short diffusion process around the residual between LR and HR representations. SinSR [13] learns a deterministic single-step mapping from a diffusion teacher. PiSA-SR [14] separates pixel-level and semantic-level adaptation into two LoRA components, while AdcSR [15] uses adversarial distillation to compress a pretrained diffusion model. Nevertheless, stronger natural-image priors alone do not ensure that reconstructed textures and boundaries remain consistent with the content of a thermal observation. 

B. Large Generative Models for Image Generation 

Large generative models have expanded the ways in which language and images can condition image synthesis. La-tent diffusion models [18] perform generation in a learned compressed representation, supporting HR synthesis with flexible conditioning. Work on DALL-E 3 [19] shows that improved training captions can strengthen correspondence between instructions and generated content. GPT-4o image generation [20] further illustrates an interface that combines language instructions, supplied images, and iterative image editing. Compared with task-specific generative models, GPT-6 Astra [16] provides a more general multimodal capability that integrates visual understanding, instruction following, and image-conditioned generation. This combination makes it particularly suitable as a generative teacher for thermal SR, where weak scene structures must first be interpreted before a detailed HR reference can be produced. Accordingly, GPT-6 Astra offers a suitable means of constructing task-specific HR supervision for thermal SR. III. P ROPOSED METHOD

(3) The overall framework of AstraSR is presented in Fig. 2, illustrating how the proposed learning formulation is realized through offline HR reference construction, supervised SR training, and independent inference. In step 1, the captured thermal image is processed by GPT-6 Astra under a fidelity-constrained restoration instruction. Leveraging its multimodal generative capabilities, GPT-6 Astra constructs a structure-preserving HR reference while maintaining scene geometry and thermal fidelity, encouraging conservative detail recovery and suppressing unsupported content generation. This stage is performed offline only, and the generated HR reference is subsequently used as supervision rather than participating in inference. In step 2, a learnable thermal SR model takes the captured thermal image as input and predicts a dense HR reconstruction. The prediction is optimized against the gener-ated HR reference using pixel, gradient, and perceptual losses, with gradients propagated only through the SR model. After training, the SR model is frozen and directly restores captured thermal inputs into degradation-suppressed HR thermal images in step 3.

B. GPT-Guided HR Thermal Image Generation 

We use GPT-6 Astra to construct HR training references from existing real captured thermal images. The source image provides the scene content, while text instructions guide the generation toward clearer boundaries and more visible fine structures. The aim is to produce an HR representation of the same thermal scene that can serve as supervision for the SR model. Generation focuses on structures that are difficult to distinguish in the captured input, including thin lines, small foreground objects, and repeated geometric patterns. The desired references combine more distinct local contours with smoother intensity transitions in broad regions, while retaining the overall organization of the source scene. Each generated HR reference is further examined image by image against its corresponding captured LR input to verify that the recovered details remain consistent with the observable scene structure and do not introduce unsupported content. These properties motivate the pixel, gradient, and perceptual objectives introduced in Section III-C. C. Qualitative Comparisons 

We compare AstraSR with seven existing real-world super-resolution methods. These include Real-ESRGAN [9], SwinIR [10], FeMaSR [11], ResShift [12], SinSR [13], PiSA-SR [14], and AdcSR [15]. Fig. 4 presents direct 4× en-largement of captured thermal images, together with matched crops from the regions marked by red boxes. The examples cover object contours, repeated thin structures, and geometric boundaries, allowing comparison of both local detail and intensity transitions. Real-ESRGAN and SwinIR improve edge definition, making major contours and straight lines easier to distinguish. FeMaSR produces more segmented intensity regions and additional local variation in some crops. ResShift and SinSR delineate the main structures, although several nar-row openings and boundary transitions remain uneven or soft. PiSA-SR and AdcSR render more surface variation in parts of the displayed scenes, with some regions appearing more strongly textured or partitioned. These differences show that stronger local contrast does not always coincide with smoother surfaces or more regular fine structures. Across the displayed examples, AstraSR combines continuous object contours and distinct structural boundaries with smoother intensity transi-tions across broad surfaces. Repeated elements and rectangular outlines remain visually prominent, while adjacent regions exhibit a more consistent appearance. This balance makes the main structures easier to distinguish without emphasizing texture throughout the image. These results demonstrate that AstraSR achieves more coherent structural reconstruction, with clearer boundaries, more continuous contours, and smoother intensity transitions than competing methods. V. C ONCLUSION 

We presented AstraSR for real-world thermal super-resolution and constructed a dataset pairing captured LR thermal images with GPT-6 Astra-generated HR references. By using captured images directly as inputs, the method avoids constructing LR training samples through synthetic down-sampling. Our direct real-to-teacher strategy trains a single-channel SR model with pixel, gradient, and perceptual losses, enabling 4× inference without querying GPT. Qualitative comparisons show clearer object contours and more distinct structural boundaries alongside smoother intensity transitions in the illustrated scenes. These observations support the use of GPT-generated HR supervision for learning the appearance of enhanced thermal images. Future work will explore an active learning paradigm where the lightweight SR model identifies challenging degradation cases and iteratively queries the offline Astra model with adaptive prompts, dynamically generating informative positive and negative training pairs to continuously evolve the training distribution.

Concept Acupuncturist: An Efficient and Precise
Concept Erasure Method for Diffusion Models via
Sparse Autoencoders
Yiran Xiahou∗, Siliang Qin∗, Kai Chen∗, Shengzhi Zhang†, Renyang Liu‡
∗Institute of Information Engineering, Chinese Academy of Sciences, China
†Department of Computer Science, Metropolitan College, Boston University, USA
‡Institute of Data Science, NationalUniversity of SingaporeSingapore, Singapore
Abstract—Diffusion-based image generation models produce
high-quality images but may generate harmful or policy-violating
content, motivating machine unlearning (MU) to selectively remove
target concepts while preserving general capabilities. However,
existing MU approaches often struggle with the distributed and
entangled nature of concept representations, leading to inefficient
optimization and imprecise edits when applied through large-scale
fine-tuning over full diffusion trajectories. To bridge this gap,
we propose Concept Acupuncturist (CONCEPTACP), a novel MU
framework that integrates interpretability-guided analysis with
inference-free fine-tuning for efficient and precise concept removal.
By establishing mappings between semantic concepts and cross-
attention blocks in the U-Net denoiser and localizing the most
concept-relevant block for targeted optimization, CONCEPTACP
avoids full diffusion trajectory optimization, thereby improving
fine-tuning efficiency. To mitigate concept entanglement, we
propose a feature-level correction mechanism based on Sparse
Autoencoders (SAEs), where SAE-derived sparse, semantically
meaningful feature activations are manipulated to construct
targeted correction signals, which are then projected back to the
output space of the corresponding block and used as supervision
for precise concept removal. Consequently, CONCEPTACP elimi-
nates the need for external inference-time modules while achieving
efficient and fine-grained unlearning. Instantiated on SDXL-Turbo,
CONCEPTACP improves the forgetting success rate by 36.34
percentage points in the standard one-step generation setting
on average over the best-performing baseline, while preserving
image quality and prompt fidelity, and generalizes effectively
across other Stable Diffusion backbones. Our code is available at
https://github.com/Eran-Xia/ConceptACP.

I. INTRODUCTION
Text-to-image diffusion models [1], [2], [3], [4], [5] have
demonstrated strong capabilities in image synthesis [6], [2]
and editing [7], [8]. Training these models on large-scale web
datasets improves performance, but also exposes them to diverse
and weakly curated content. Consequently, such models may
produce harmful or policy-violating content, e.g., Not-Safe-
For-Work (NSFW) imagery [9], [10] or infringing outputs
that replicate protected identities or artistic styles without
authorization [11]. These concerns motivate the development
of various strategies for mitigating unsafe content, including
dataset curation, post-processing and direct model modification.
However, retraining on filtered datasets is often computationally
expensive [12], [13], and post-processing remains vulnerable to
prompt manipulation [14], [15]. Approaches for direct model
modification, particularly machine unlearning (MU), offer a
more direct and controllable way to remove specific concepts
without full retraining [16], [17].
Existing MU methods can be broadly divided into three
categories: inference-time intervention, model editing and
fine-tuning-based unlearning. Inference-time approaches [18],
[19], [20], [21], [22], [23], [24] typically suppress undesirable
concepts by attaching external, removable modules during
inference, which modify model outputs without altering model
parameters and thus leave concept-related representations
encoded within the model. Such interventions may introduce ar-
tifacts and degrade generation quality [25], while their inference
overhead becomes non-negligible under large-scale deployment.
Model editing methods [26], [27], [28] directly modify model
parameters through closed-form or localized updates to remove
target concepts. However, the highly distributed and entangled
representations in diffusion models [1], [2], [3], [5] make
precise parameter localization difficult. More fundamentally,
existing methods focus on finding low-interference update
directions rather than identifying the parameters or internal
features that encode the target concept [28].
Fine-tuning-based MU methods, in contrast, directly modify
model parameters without additional inference overhead, mak-
ing them widely adopted for safer image generation. However,
they still face two fundamental challenges: (i) limited edit
precision, since global or layer-wise updates often interfere
with unrelated concepts due to the polysemantic nature of
neurons [29], [30], [31], making it difficult to precisely
remove a target concept without affecting others; and (ii) high
computational cost, as they often update a large portion of
model parameters (e.g., all cross-attention layers or global
weights [9], [32]) and require executing the denoising process
up to an intermediate timestep to obtain supervision [9] due to
the iterative nature of diffusion models, thereby incurring full-
trajectory cost even for local updates and leading to substantial
training overhead, especially when scaling to multiple concepts
or iterative updates.
To overcome these limitations, we propose Concept Acupunc-
turist (CONCEPTACP), a structured fine-tuning framework that
arXiv:2610.32059v1  [cs.CV]  23 Oct 2026


enables inference-free and localized updates for parameter
optimization. Inspired by recent interpretability studies showing
that concept-related information can be decomposed into
structured and semantically meaningful components [33],
we introduce Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs), an interpretable
tool that decomposes neuron activations into sparse feature
representations within the U-Net denoiser, to enable fine-
grained concept removal. We further reformulate diffusion fine-
tuning as a localized block-level learning problem, avoiding
full denoising trajectories during optimization. Nevertheless,
realizing this design presents two technical challenges:
C1: difficulty in internalizing feature-level interventions
into model parameters. Existing SAE-based methods typically
treat SAEs as inference-time intervention modules [22], [23].
A natural question is therefore whether the benefits of such
feature-level editing can be directly embedded into model
parameters. However, SAEs operate on the residual updates of
corresponding blocks and require explicit feature manipulation
to achieve forgetting, making their direct translation into
parameter updates non-trivial.
C2: difficulty in identifying the target block and con-
structing reliable supervision signals. Concept representations
in diffusion models are distributed across multiple cross-
attention blocks and evolve over the denoising trajectory,
making it challenging to identify an appropriate block and
derive consistent supervision for localized fine-tuning.
To address C1, we move beyond treating SAEs as external
modules but as a mechanism for constructing supervision
signals. Given prompts containing the target concept, we
identify concept-relevant features and suppress them according
to their relevance scores via a feature selection procedure. The
modified features are then projected back to the output space
of the corresponding cross-attention block. This process yields
target outputs that are equivalent to performing feature-level
editing, but expressed in the model’s native representation space.
By pairing these corrected outputs with the original inputs, we
construct a supervision dataset that enables internalization of
feature-level interventions into model parameters.
To address C2, we reformulate diffusion fine-tuning as
a localized block-level learning problem. Specifically, we
establish mappings between semantic concepts and cross-
attention blocks using a pool of pretrained SAEs, building upon
prior analysis [33]. Based on the mappings, we identify one
block most strongly associated with the target concept together
with its relevant features and then construct a block-level input–
output supervision dataset from a small set of text prompts
at selected timesteps. The target outputs are obtained via the
feature-level correction process described above. Importantly,
we collect the block’s activations across timesteps in a single
forward pass, avoiding repeated denoising during fine-tuning
while retaining further flexible feature manipulation through
the cached activations.
We implement CONCEPTACP on SDXL-Turbo and evaluate
it on the EvalIGMU [34] benchmark across four representative
concepts. Experiments show that CONCEPTACP achieves
strong balance between forgetting effectiveness and preser-
vation of unrelated concepts, improving forgetting success rate
by 36.34 percentage points on average under the standard
one-step setting over the best-performing baseline. We further
observe consistent gains on SDXL and SD v1.4, demonstrating
transferability across backbones. In terms of efficiency, our
block-level, inference-free fine-tuning reduces computational
cost by 46.4% compared to full diffusion-based method without
this design. In addition, we introduce two metrics, Feature
Overlap Score and Feature Activation Correlation Score,
to characterize internal feature dynamics before and after
unlearning. Results show that our method induces feature-
level changes consistent with the intended concept suppression
and non-target preservation, providing internal evidence for its
precise unlearning. Additional results demonstrate preserved
image quality and robustness against adversarial prompts.
Our main contributions are outlined below:
• We propose CONCEPTACP, a novel unlearning framework
that integrates interpretability-guided feature analysis into
parameter-level fine-tuning for diffusion models. To the
best of our knowledge, this is the first work to use SAE-
derived feature corrections as supervision for localized and
internalized fine-tuning in diffusion model unlearning.
• We introduce a structured fine-tuning paradigm that jointly
improves edit precision and efficiency. For precision, we use
SAE-derived feature corrections as fine-grained supervision,
while for efficiency, we construct block-level input–output
supervision to enable inference-free fine-tuning of a single
cross-attention block.
• Extensive experiments show that CONCEPTACP effectively
removes target concepts while preserving model utility and
substantially reducing fine-tuning computational cost.
II. BACKGROUND AND RELATED WORK
A. Text-to-Image Models
Diffusion models [1], [2], [3], [4] synthesize realistic images
by gradually denoising random noise through a learned reverse
diffusion process. These models typically consist of three key
components: a text encoder that converts input prompts into
semantic embeddings, a U-Net denoiser that conditions on the
text embedding and iteratively refines a latent representation,
and a decoder that maps the latent representation back to the
image space. Among these components, the U-Net serves as
the core of the denoising process, comprising downsampling
and upsampling pathways connected through residual blocks,
often interleaved with attention or transformer modules.
Traditional Stable Diffusion models, such as the 1.x and
2.x versions, typically require tens of denoising steps (e.g.,
50) to generate high-quality images. To improve image fidelity
and prompt adherence, Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL) [5] was
introduced. SDXL-Turbo [35], on the other hand, is a distilled
variant of SDXL, trained with techniques such as adversarial
and progressive distillation to accelerate generation. It typically
requires only 1–4 steps to generate an image while maintaining
competitive visual quality. Architecturally, the U-Net in SDXL-
Turbo comprises a downsampling path, a bottleneck section and
2


an upsampling path with transformer blocks to capture global
context. With growing demand for real-time and interactive
text-to-image generation, SDXL-Turbo has gained significant
attention for its fast inference.
B. Machine Unlearning
MU aims to remove the influence of specific subsets of data
or learned knowledge from a trained model [36], [37]. Typically,
existing methods for T2I models either erase individual samples
or suppress undesired concepts. The latter, which has received
substantial attention in recent studies, aims to significantly
suppress the T2I model’s ability to generate particular undesired
concepts while preserving its performance on unrelated content.
The unlearning targets explored in prior research include:
(1) semantics (e.g., nudity, violence, and gore), (2) stylistic
attributes (e.g., Van Gogh), and (3) objects (e.g., church and
parachute). Existing MU approaches can be broadly categorized
into three strategies:
Model Fine-tuning. Fine-tuning-based methods achieve un-
learning by updating model parameters to erase target con-
cepts. For example, ESD [9] shifts predictions from concept-
conditioned to unconditional outputs, AC [38] align distribu-
tions for concept removal, SA [39] replaces forgotten concepts
with alternative distributions, attention redirection [11] fine-
tunes cross-attention layers, MACE [40] combines attention
editing with LoRA for multi-concept erasure, and TRACE [32]
constrains edits to later denoising stages.
Model Editing. Model editing-based approaches aim to remove
concepts without additional training by directly modifying
model weights. RECE [26] iteratively eliminates residual
embeddings, and ConceptPrune [27] prunes FFN neurons
correlated with the target concept. SPEED [28] further improves
prior preservation by introducing null-space constrained param-
eter editing, where model updates are restricted to directions
that minimize interference with non-target concepts.
Inference-time Intervention. Inference intervention-based
methods manipulate prompts or feature activations during
inference. SLD [18] appends concept descriptors to prompts,
SAFREE [19] projects toxic token embeddings orthogonally to
harmful concepts, TraSCE [20] provides trajectory-level guid-
ance, and SafeRedir [21] redirects the prompt embeddings of
unsafe concepts. Based on Sparse Autoencoders, SAEUron [22]
trains them on U-Net feature activations, ConceptSteerers [23]
applies SAEs to the text encoder, and SAEmnesia [24] uses
supervised SAE training for clearer concept-neuron mapping.

D. Threat Model
With the rapid progress of generative artificial intelligence,
diffusion-based image generation models have been widely
deployed in online platforms and local applications. However,
this flexibility also raises safety and copyright concerns, as
users may intentionally generate inappropriate content (e.g.,
nudity, violence) or unintentionally produce images that mimic
copyrighted artistic styles (e.g., Van Gogh). We consider two
realistic deployment scenarios for T2I diffusion models in
practical deployment settings.
S1: Online Deployment with Controlled Access. In this
setting, the service provider deploys the diffusion model online
and exposes its interface for user interaction under controlled
conditions. The adversaries seek to induce the model to
3


generate unsafe or policy-violating images. Their access to
the model is strictly black-box: they can only interact with
the system through text prompts and observe the generated
outputs. As a result, adversaries may attempt prompt-based
attacks, such as carefully crafted or obfuscated prompts, to
bypass safety filters in online deployments.
S2: Public Release of Model Checkpoints. We assume
that the model providers who host or distribute text-to-image
diffusion models are trusted. Their goal is to maintain a
balance between safety and utility, preventing the generation
of harmful or undesired content while preserving the model’s
ability to produce benign and creative outputs. In practice,
such providers may share model checkpoints with the research
community. Adversaries can download the released model
weights and deploy the model locally with full control. They
can attempt low-cost strategies to bypass safety mechanisms,
e.g., by removing external modules or directly querying the base
model without the added controls. Under this setting, the model
provider does not bear responsibility for any consequences
resulting from downstream modification to the model weights.
III. EMPIRICAL STUDY
MU methods in diffusion models have evolved rapidly
in recent years. However, many design choices, including
localization strategy, editing paradigm, optimization scope and
evaluation protocol, remain largely empirical. In this section,
we systematically investigate these design choices through a
series of empirical studies. Our findings not only reveal the
limitations of existing approaches but also motivate the design
principles adopted in CONCEPTACP.
A. The Limitations of Neuron-level Localization
Unlearning methods must first determine where to edit, as
precise localization enables effective concept removal with
minimal interference to unrelated concepts. Existing approaches
perform localization at different levels of the network. For ex-
ample, ESD [9] modifies model parameters globally, UCE [42]
edits concept representations through cross-attention layers, and
ConceptPrune [27] identifies and suppresses concept-related
neurons in the FFN modules of the U-Net.
However, these approaches still rely on neuron-level or
architecture-level localization, which faces fundamental chal-
lenges due to the polysemantic nature of neurons [29], [30],
[31], where a single neuron may encode information about
multiple concepts, hindering selective concept removal. As
shown in Table III, although existing methods achieve consid-
erable forgetting success rates, they often degrade image quality,
suggesting that coarse localization can affect representations
beyond the target concept.
While recent methods such as SPEED [28] improve the
preservation of unrelated concepts by constraining parameter
updates to less interfering subspaces, they mainly focus on how
to edit after selecting the editing space. However, achieving
precise unlearning also requires addressing where to edit, i.e.,
accurately identifying the internal representations associated
with the target concept.

B. The Fragility of Inference-time Steering
Instead of modifying model parameters, inference-time un-
learning methods perform concept suppression during inference
by introducing external modules or modifying the generation
pipeline. However, such approaches remain fragile due to
their bypassability in open-source settings and the additional
computation incurred for each generation.

one-time cost with zero inference overhead, whereas inference-

C. The Inefficiency of Large-scale Fine-tuning
Existing fine-tuning-based unlearning methods [9], [40],
[32] suffer from substantial computational overhead due to
both large-scale parameter updates and the iterative nature
of diffusion models. These methods typically modify a large
portion of model parameters (e.g., multiple cross-attention
layers or global weights), while obtaining supervision at a
specific denoising timestep requires executing all preceding
steps. Consequently, updating even a local module remains
coupled with the full denoising trajectory, leading to inefficient
optimization.
As shown in Table VIII, fine-tuning a single cross-attention
block without executing the complete denoising trajectory
reduces the per-iteration training time to 53.6% of the original
approach. This demonstrates that a significant portion of the
optimization cost comes from the dependency on the full
diffusion trajectory rather than the actual parameters being
updated. This highlights the substantial computational overhead
of large-scale fine-tuning, which is not necessarily accompanied
by improved concept forgetting.
Observation 3: Existing fine-tuning-based unlearning
methods incur substantial optimization costs due to large-
scale parameter updates and full-trajectory dependency,
while local updates may achieve comparable forgetting
with substantially lower cost.
D. The Limitations of Output-based Evaluation
Existing diffusion unlearning methods are typically evalu-
ated through generated outputs, where classifiers are used
to determine whether the target concept remains present
after unlearning. However, classifier-based evaluation may not

E. The Generalization Gap Across Diffusion Architectures
Most existing MU methods were developed for Stable
Diffusion v1.4/1.5. However, modern diffusion models such
as SDXL-Turbo introduce substantial differences in model
architecture and inference, including deeper U-Net structures
and significantly fewer sampling steps. When directly applied
to SDXL-Turbo, existing methods exhibit notable performance
degradation (e.g., ESD), which we hypothesize is partly due
to its fast-sampling regime (1–4 vs. tens of steps in standard
SD). Quantitative results and visualizations (see §VI-B) further
support these limitations under fast-sampling conditions.
Observation 5: Existing unlearning methods, particu-
larly timestep-wise interventions, show limited cross-
architecture generalization, as SD1.x-based approaches
degrade significantly on modern fast-sampling models.
Overall, these observations highlight the need for unlearning
approaches that are both precise and effective across different
diffusion paradigms, including traditional multi-step diffusion
models as well as modern large-scale, fast-sampling ones, as
addressed by the method proposed in this work.

IV. CONCEPT ACUPUNCTURIST
A. Overview
Based on the empirical analysis in §III, we identify the
need for a more precise and efficient fine-tuning approach. Our
key idea is to leverage SAEs for feature-level localization and
intervention, while internalizing their corrections into model
parameters through localized fine-tuning. Unlike prior methods
that attach SAEs to the final model during inference [22], [23],
[43], we instead use them to construct supervision signals for
parameter optimization. This design introduces no additional
inference overhead while embedding the unlearning effect
directly into model parameters, thereby addressing S1 and
supporting S2, while requiring attackers to perform weight-
level manipulation rather than merely bypass inference-time
interventions. Meanwhile, we localize the update to a single
cross-attention block most relevant to the target concept,
thus avoiding end-to-end optimization along the full diffusion
trajectory and reducing the computational cost of fine-tuning.
An overview of our approach is shown in Figure 3. We begin
with feature localization to identify the block most strongly
associated with the target concept, along with its corresponding
features. We establish mappings between semantic concepts
and cross-attention blocks, and apply the feature localization
algorithm introduced in §IV-B to automatically identify both
the target block and the corresponding features to be modified.
This joint localization substantially narrows the fine-tuning
scope, enabling faster and more targeted updates.
Subsequently, we construct fine-tuning datasets by mod-
ifying the localized features controlled by a scaling factor 𝑎
(defined in §IV-C) and projecting them back to the output space
of the corresponding cross-attention block. These corrected
representations serve as supervision signals for subsequent
parameter optimization. Crucially, instead of performing end-to-
end diffusion inference during training, we only need a single
forward pass to record input–output pairs of the identified
cross-attention block along the denoising trajectory, enabling
inference-free fine-tuning.
Finally, we perform block fine-tuning using a joint objective
(Eq. (11) in §IV-D) that balances target concept suppression
and preservation of generation quality. The resulting parameter
updates internalize the SAE-based feature corrections into the
model, enabling precise concept erasure without additional
inference-time components. The updated cross-attention block
is then used in place of the original block during generation.
Overall, this framework enables precise and interpretable
unlearning across different concept categories.
B. Feature Localization
As shown in the first panel of Figure 3, CONCEPTACP begins
with feature localization. To remove a specific concept, we first
identify its corresponding features and the cross-attention block
in which they are most strongly represented. Prior work [33]
reports that four SAEs trained on cross-attention blocks provide
interpretable representations of distinct visual elements. Guided
by this observation, we use publicly available general-purpose
SAEs as the candidate pool to localize the features and target

Although it is possible to identify features related to a
given concept by examining the feature heatmaps or manip-
ulating feature activations to observe the resulting changes
in generated images, this process is time-consuming and
unreliable, particularly when concept-related activations are
weak or ambiguous. Moreover, while SAEs promote sparse
representations, a given concept may still correspond to multiple
features, particularly when training data for that concept is
limited or when the concept involves diverse visual elements.
As shown in Figure 4, our preliminary analysis indicates that
feature #4913 on down.2.1 can effectively remove Nudity,
whereas suppressing any one of the top-5 most activated
features individually fails to fully eliminate Church.
To address these challenges, inspired by the feature selection
strategy in SAEUron [22], we further develop an automated
feature localization algorithm to locate relevant features and
the target block. Specifically, we compute the statistical
activations of features over subsets of relevant and irrelevant
data, assigning each feature a score based on the differences in
their activation distributions, where a higher score indicates a
stronger correlation with the target concept. Using this scoring

C. Fine-tuning Dataset Generation
As shown in the second panel of Figure 3, after identifying
the features to suppress, we attenuate their activations via the
SAE by multiplying them with a negative factor and decode the
modified output, which represents the residual update between
the output and input of the corresponding cross-attention block.
Combining this residual with the original block input yields
the concept-erased output for 𝑝𝑐, which forms the input-output
pairs as part of the fine-tuning data to update the parameters.
However, before this process, a key question arises: by how
much should feature activations be suppressed? As shown
in Figure 5, preliminary experiments indicate that different
suppression factors produce distinct visual effects. Smaller
factors weaken concept removal but reduce interference with
unrelated content, whereas larger factors enhance removal but
may distort the image or impair unrelated concepts due to over-
suppression or inadvertent activation. Furthermore, concepts
linked to multiple features may require different suppression
magnitudes for each feature.

V. NEW METRICS
As discussed in §III-D, existing MU methods primarily
rely on output-level metrics, such as the Forgetting Success
Rate, which may not fully reflect changes in internal repre-
sentations due to non-standardized evaluation datasets [34]
and discrepancies between classifier predictions and human
perception [44]. Consequently, output-level metrics provide
limited insight into whether the target concept has been erased
from internal representations, making it difficult to understand
the internal mechanisms behind unlearning.
To complement output-level evaluation, we consider an
internal perspective that examines how model representations
change during unlearning. Rather than introducing a standalone
benchmark, our goal is to provide a post-hoc analysis tool
that can be applied to different unlearning methods, helping
interpret their effectiveness from the perspective of internal
representation changes.
To this end, we use SAEs as a unified analysis tool for
probing intermediate representations. SAEs model the residual
between input and output latents of a given block at a diffusion
timestep, learning a sparse and interpretable feature basis. Prior
work [33] shows that these learned features generalize across
closely related models; for example, SAEs trained on SDXL
can be directly applied to SDXL-Turbo at the same block,
suggesting that they capture stable and semantically meaningful
patterns rather than model-specific artifacts.
Based on this property, we treat SAEs as a fixed feature
space for analyzing model behavior before and after unlearning,
independent of the specific MU method. Since the semantic
8


meaning of SAE features remains largely consistent, changes
in their activations can directly reflect how unlearning affects
internal representations.
From this perspective, we define two complementary internal
metrics that quantify changes in feature activations induced by
unlearning. These metrics serve as diagnostic tools rather than
replacements for output-level evaluation, as they are measured
in an SAE-derived feature space and may thus inherently favor
SAE-based approaches.

VI. EVALUATION
In this section, we present the experimental setup, including
the datasets, baselines and evaluation metrics, followed by the
detailed results. Through extensive experiments, we address
the following research questions (RQs):
• RQ1: How effective is CONCEPTACP in unlearning com-
pared with other methods? (§VI-B)
• RQ2: How well does CONCEPTACP preserve its generation
capability for concepts unrelated to the forgotten target
compared with other methods? (§VI-C)
• RQ3: How precisely does CONCEPTACP remove target
concepts from the model’s internal representations? (§VI-D)
• RQ4: How robust is CONCEPTACP when forgetting given
concepts? (§VI-E)
• RQ5: How does CONCEPTACP perform in multi-concept
unlearning? (§VI-F)
• RQ6: How do the hyperparameters affect the performance
of CONCEPTACP? (§ VI-G)
A. Setup
Unlearning Tasks. Following prior works [45], [46], [34], [9],
[42], we consider three representative unlearning scenarios:
global-abstract-style, local-abstract-style and local-concrete-
objects, followed by four specific tasks, i.e., Nudity, VanGogh,
Church and Parachute. These four concepts are commonly
adopted as representative unlearning targets, with Church and
Parachute in particular being widely used due to their known
difficulty to forget effectively. Other unlearning tasks that can
be classified into the above three categories are treated as the
same type and handled with the same pipeline.
Models. We conduct our main experiments on SDXL-Turbo,
which supports high-quality image generation with 1–4 in-
ference steps. We evaluate both 1-step and 4-step generation,
denoted as T1 and T4, respectively, and further evaluate our
method on multi-step models, including SDXL with 30 steps
and SD v1.4 with 50 steps.
Datasets. For feature analysis and fine-tuning data construction,
we use a synthetic prompt dataset generated with ChatGPT,
containing 50 prompts per concept. For benchmarking concept
unlearning, we adopt EvalIGMU benchmark [34], which covers
four representative concepts: Nudity, VanGogh, Church and
Parachute. For robustness evaluation, we further consider
three safety-related benchmarks: Inappropriate Image Prompts

Baselines. We compare our approach against representative

Metrics. We evaluate unlearning performance from four aspects.
Unlearning effectiveness is measured using Forgetting Success

and CLIP Accuracy (CLIP Acc.), which assesses alignment

Success Rate (ASR) under adversarial prompting. Finally, we
measure unlearning precision using two internal metrics: FO
Score and FAC Score proposed in §V, which provide a post-hoc
diagnostic of changes in the model’s internal representations
during unlearning. Details can be found in Appendix B-C.
B. Unlearning Effectiveness
Table II reports the FSR and CLIP Acc. across four repre-
sentative unlearning tasks, and Figure 6 illustrates generated
images. Additional visual comparisons with the baselines can
be found in Figure 14 in Appendix C-A. As shown in Table II,
CONCEPTACP achieves the highest average FSR and the lowest
average CLIP Acc. under both T1 and T4, demonstrating
strong unlearning effectiveness. At T1, it improves FSR by
36.34 and reduces CLIP Acc. by 32.10 compared to the
best baseline. At T4, it maintains its advantage, improving
FSR by 24.10 and reducing CLIP Acc. by 12.48. These
results demonstrate that CONCEPTACP effectively erases target
concepts while preserving unrelated content, even under fast-
sampling conditions.
Observations. We summarize four key observations from our
experimental results:
First, ESD underperforms mainly due to its dependence on
interventions across denoising timesteps. While it typically fine-
tunes 50-100 denoising steps on models such as SD v1.4, the
1–4 sampling steps in SDXL-Turbo limit its ability to suppress
target concepts, resulting in suboptimal forgetting. In contrast,
although trained with single-step generation, CONCEPTACP
generalizes well to multi-step inference, achieving the highest
average FSR under T4. Moreover, as shown in Figure 10 in
Appendix C-A, the forgetting effect persists across extended
generation schedules of up to 30 steps, demonstrating effec-
tiveness across different generation lengths. This is consistent
with prior findings [41] that early timesteps establish coarse
layouts and scene composition, making interventions at this
stage effective for controlling generation.
Second, forgetting for Parachute is less consistent. As
illustrated in Figure 6, CONCEPTACP removes most instances
in many cases, although residual fragments may remain; a
detailed analysis is provided in §VII. Other methods often
retain substantial portions of the parachute, indicating weaker
erasure. Notably, ESD achieves an unusually high FSR of
82.80 under T4, but manual inspection reveals dense, abstract,
and cartoonish outputs that are less realistic and semantically
coherent than those of CONCEPTACP. This suggests that a high
FSR does not necessarily correspond to better visual quality
or more faithful concept removal.
Third, for VanGogh in the one-step generation case, we
observe that the main subject of the generated images can
be affected in Figure 14 in Appendix C-A. This phenomenon
can be attributed to two factors: (i) the visualized examples
are selected under a trade-off between FSR and FID, and
the VanGogh classifier does not always perfectly align with
human perception; and (ii) VanGogh represents a global style
that inherently influences the entire image. Therefore, for
global style concepts such as VanGogh, we do not recommend
using an excessively large suppression coefficient 𝑎in practice.
We provide a more detailed analysis of this issue in §VI-G.
For other concepts and under T4, CONCEPTACP consistently
achieves strong forgetting with comparable image quality and
no noticeable degradation.
Finally, compared with SAEUron which employs SAEs as
external modules, our method achieves notably higher FSRs
for Nudity, VanGogh, Church and Parachute, demonstrating
the effectiveness of our feature-level fine-tuning strategy.
Effectiveness Across Diffusion Backbones. We further eval-
uate CONCEPTACP on SDXL and SD v1.4, with results
reported in Table III. CONCEPTACP achieves effective concept
forgetting across diverse target concepts on both backbones,
demonstrating its generalization beyond the SDXL-Turbo.
These results suggest that our representation-guided editing
strategy is robust to different diffusion architectures and
sampling configurations without requiring architecture-specific
modifications. Details are provided in Appendix C-A.

VII. DISCUSSION
SAE Generality and Concept-Specific Features. Our exper-
iments directly use publicly available general-purpose SAEs,
which already provide sufficiently informative features for
effective concept localization and unlearning. As shown in
Table X in Appendix D, Church and Parachute involve more
numerous and dispersed features than Nudity and VanGogh,
with Parachute showing relatively weaker forgetting, suggesting
that its representation is less cleanly decomposed into sparse
features. This suggests an opportunity for further improving
feature localization through concept-specific SAE training [24],
which may yield more concentrated concept-relevant features.
Concept Entanglement in Nudity Unlearning. We observe
that on the EvalIGMU benchmark, CONCEPTACP sometimes
removes the entire human subject when suppressing Nudity.
This is partly influenced by the suppression strength, but
more fundamentally, unlike methods [9], [42] that steer
the target toward a predefined alternative direction (e.g.,
Clothed), CONCEPTACP directly locates and suppresses the
features associated with the target concept without specifying
a replacement representation, which may lead the model to
avoid generating human subjects in some cases to prevent
nudity. Importantly, this does not indicate systematic over-
forgetting: human subjects remain intact for other prompts,
and unrelated-concept evaluations show that human figures
can still be generated normally. The observed removal is
therefore input-dependent and can be alleviated with milder
suppression, as discussed in §VI-G and as shown in Figure 12
in Appendix C-D. Moreover, as noted by SafeGen [44], nudity
and sexually explicit content are not necessarily equivalent;
our unlearning target is specifically nudity rather than sexually
explicit content as a whole. This is visually evident in the
fourth row of Figure 7, where generated images still contain
sexually suggestive elements, demonstrating the fine-grained
and targeted nature of our unlearning.
VIII. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
Conclusion. In this work, we present CONCEPTACP, a
novel unlearning method that transforms interpretability-guided
feature interventions into supervision signals for fine-tuning and
internalizes their effects into model parameters. By localizing
the update to a single cross-attention block, CONCEPTACP
enables efficient and precise concept forgetting without repeat-
edly running diffusion trajectories or introducing inference-
time overhead. Experiments show that CONCEPTACP achieves
superior forgetting performance over SOTA baselines while
maintaining image quality, and substantially improves fine-
tuning efficiency. Overall, this work bridges interpretability and
fine-tuning-based MU, advancing safer and more controllable
generative models.
Future Work. Future work could explore targeted SAE training
approaches that make features related to the target concept more
concentrated, as well as feature-selection strategies that adapt
across layers and concept types, and strengthen the robustness
against stronger adversarial attacks.
ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS
This paper studies concept erasure for text-to-image diffusion
models to reduce the generation of undesired content (e.g.,
NSFW concepts, copyrighted styles, or specific objects) while
limiting collateral effects on unrelated content. As with other
model-editing techniques, the proposed approach is dual-
use: feature localization and targeted modification could be
misapplied to disable safety mechanisms or to suppress benign
or protected attributes. We therefore frame CONCEPTACP
as a compliance- and safety-oriented unlearning technique
and encourage practitioners to apply appropriate governance
measures when deploying edited models. Our experiments may
involve sensitive prompts and generated outputs; we minimize
manual inspection, rely primarily on automated filtering and
quantitative evaluation, and avoid displaying explicit content
in qualitative examples. This work does not involve new
data collection from human subjects or user studies; we use
established benchmarks and follow their intended research use
and licensing constraints.

In this section, we present a formal, symbolic description
of our feature localization algorithm.
Specifically, given a target concept 𝑐to be erased, we
categorize it into two types: attribute-like concepts 𝑐𝑎(e.g.,
Nudity, VanGogh style) and object-like concepts 𝑐𝑜(e.g.,
Church, Parachute). For attribute-like concepts 𝑐𝑎, let D𝑐𝑎
be a dataset of prompts that contain 𝑐𝑎and D¬𝑐𝑎a paired
dataset of prompts that do not contain 𝑐𝑎. For object-like

𝑐𝑜and D¬𝑐𝑜a dataset of prompts that exclude 𝑐𝑜but may
include other objects; in this case, the two sets are not pairwise
aligned. For convenience, we denote the dataset containing
the target concept as D𝑐and the dataset excluding it as D¬𝑐,
unifying the notation across concept types.
For each feature 𝑓in an interpretable block 𝑖, we compute
a relevance score 𝑆(𝑐, D, 𝑖, 𝑓) that quantifies how strongly
the feature encodes the target concept across the dataset,
with higher scores indicating stronger association. For each
block 𝑖, we record the maximum relevance score 𝑠𝑖

B. Evaluation Baselines
We compare our approach against representative unlearning
methods spanning fine-tuning, model editing, and inference-
time forgetting, and re-implement these methods on SDXL-
16


Turbo for evaluation. The descriptions and implementation
details are as follows.
• ESD [9]. A fine-tuning-based unlearning method that use
the frozen original model to produce predictions, which are
then linearly combined to form a negative guidance signal
for updating the new model parameters. For style concepts,
ESD fine-tunes the cross-attention parameters, whereas for
nudity and object concepts, it fine-tunes the unconditional
parameters. We reproduce ESD on SDXL-Turbo under both
one-step and four-step fine-tuning settings, and evaluate it
correspondingly in one-step and four-step generation.
• UCE [42]. A model-editing based unlearning method that
requires no additional training. It modifies the parameters of
cross-attention via a closed-form update. The work evaluates
concept erasure on attributes such as artistic styles, nudity
and gender bias. We reproduce UCE on SDXL-Turbo for
evaluation.
• ConceptPrune [27]. A neuron pruning unlearning method
that alters model parameters by removing relevant neurons
based on an importance score computed for each weight
during generating of a specific concept. This work evaluates
concept erasure on attributes such as artistic styles, nudity
and gender bias. We reproduce ConceptPrune on SDXL-
Turbo with four-step generation and use it for both one-step
and four-step evaluations.
• SAEUron [22]. An inference-time unlearning method that
mounts SAEs on the cross-attention blocks of the SD v1.4
U-Net to suppress target concepts. It focuses on forgetting
objects and artistic styles rather than safety-related concepts.
We reproduce SAEUron on SDXL-Turbo for evaluation.
C. Metrics
We adopt four evaluation metrics to assess the performance
of unlearning methods:
• [Unlearning Effectiveness] FSR. The Forgetting Suc-
cess Rate (FSR) is the proportion of generated images
that no longer contain the target concept, as determined
by a concept detector. The content detectors used here
are IGMU BI MULTIC [34] for Nudity and Style, and
IGMU MULTI MULTIC [34] for Object. A higher FSR
indicates stronger unlearning effectiveness.
• [Unlearning Effectiveness] CLIP Acc. Following prior
work [50], [51], [38], we also use CLIP Accuracy (CLIP
Acc.) to quantify unlearning effectiveness. For each concept
we define two text anchors: a positive anchor 𝐴that describes
the concept and a negative anchor 𝐵that is unrelated. Given
a generated image 𝐼, let
𝑠𝐴= CLIP(𝐼, 𝐴),
𝑠𝐵= CLIP(𝐼, 𝐵).
We classify 𝐼as containing the concept if 𝑠𝐴> 𝑠𝐵; otherwise
it is classified as not containing the concept. CLIP Accuracy
is the fraction of images correctly classified under this rule.
In our experiments, the positive anchors are “naked”, “van
gogh style”, “church” and “parachute” for the four tasks
respectively, and the negative anchor is the empty string
“𝑁𝑈𝐿𝐿”.
• [Image Quality] FID Score. Fr´echet Inception Distance
(FID) [52] measures the distributional distance between
generated and reference images. Lower FID indicates better
image quality and fidelity after unlearning. Specifically, the
generated images are obtained from the fine-tuned model
on D¬𝑐, while the reference images are from the original
model on D¬𝑐.
• [Robustness] ASR. The Attack Success Rate (ASR) is
the proportion of adversarial prompts that successfully
reactivate the forgotten concept. Lower ASR implies stronger
robustness against adversarial reactivation.
To further evaluate unlearning precision beyond output-
level metrics, we report two complementary internal metrics
introduced in Section V:
• [Unlearning Precision] FO Score. Measures the overlap
between the top-𝐾activated SAE features before and after
unlearning for both the target concept and unrelated concepts,
capturing whether target-related features are effectively
removed while non-target features are preserved.
• [Unlearning Precision] FAC Score. Measures the cosine
similarity between SAE feature activation distributions before
and after unlearning on target and non-target datasets, re-
flecting distribution-level changes in internal representations.

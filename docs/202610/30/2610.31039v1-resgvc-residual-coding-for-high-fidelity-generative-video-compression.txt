ABSTRACT 

Generative video compression (GVC) methods usually employ decoder-side diffu-sion models to denoise reconstructed signals based on generative priors. Besides, most of these methods perform denoising in latent space rather than directly in pixel domain. In this paper, we propose ResGVC , a residual coding framework for GVC that guides denoising toward original pictures by transmitting residual signals. ResGVC first encodes the original pictures within a learned video codec, and then decomposes the distortion between the codec reconstruction and the orig-inal pictures into a sequence of residual segments, where each residual segment is acquired at each denoising step. Residual segments can further be compressed by a novel residual codec with a diffusion transformer as an entropy model. Unlike prior methods, ResGVC introduces explicit source constraints throughout denois-ing rather than relying on decoder-side generation alone. The diffusion-based en-tropy model also enables more effective distribution modeling capability based on the powerful generative priors. Moreover, denoising directly in pixel space avoids the irreversible information loss caused by latent representation bottlenecks. Ex-perimental results show that, compared with the previous state-of-the-art method, ResGVC achieves 63.7% and 83.8% BD-rate reductions in terms of LPIPS and PSNR, respectively, while also delivering superior visual quality. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Traditional video coding standards (Wiegand et al., 2003; Sullivan et al., 2012; Bross et al., 2021) and recent neural video compression (NVC) methods (Li et al., 2021; 2024; Jia et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026c) have shown non-trivial improvements in rate-distortion performance. As the optimization prioritizes signal fidelity, these codecs may lose fine textures or produce blurry results at low bitrates, which deviates from human perception. This limitation has motivated generative video compression (GVC), where generative models are integrated into codecs to synthesize plausible details that are not explicitly transmitted. Recent GVC methods commonly employ decoder-side diffusion mod-els (Rombach et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2025; Wan et al., 2025) to denoise reconstructed signals and improve perceptual quality (Ma & Chen, 2025a; Xue et al., 2026; Mao et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026d). Moreover, most work performs denoising in latent space rather than directly in pixel domain. However, in existing GVC methods, the decoder-side denoising process is largely driven by gener-ative priors without sufficient source constraints along the denoising path. As a result, the denoised signal may drift from the original signal during iterative denoising. Besides, because these methods typically perform latent denoising coupled with latent autoencoders, the bottleneck discards fine de-tails that cannot be reliably recovered at the decoder (Kingma & Welling, 2013). These limitations suggest that faithful GVC requires explicit source constraints throughout denoising and performs denoising directly in pixel space without relying on latent autoencoders. ResGVC selectively transmits fidelity-critical residual segments, which serve as source constraints for reconstructing the denoising path at the decoder side. Besides, our denoising is performed directly in pixel space, thereby avoiding the irreversible information loss introduced by latent autoencoders. For efficient residual coding, we propose a residual codec by extending a pixel-space video diffusion transformer from a generator to a conditional probability estimator. This diffusion model estimates the distribution of each residual segment, allowing fidelity-critical residual segments to be entropy-coded while predicting the remaining residual segments from the previously transmitted ones. Thus, residual coding is reserved for information that must be faithfully transmitted to preserve recon-struction quality, whereas residual generation is employed for content that can be well predicted by the generative model to reduce bitrate. By jointly applying residual coding and residual generation along the denoising path, ResGVC achieves an improved perception-fidelity-rate trade-off. To the best of our knowledge, this work is the first to explore residual coding in GVC and to re-purpose a diffusion model for distribution modeling in both residual coding and residual generation. Experiments demonstrate that ResGVC achieves state-of-the-art compression performance across multiple metrics. Compared with the latest GVC method, ResGVC achieves 63.7% and 83.8% BD-rate reductions in terms of LPIPS and PSNR, respectively, while producing superior visual results. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose ResGVC, a residual coding framework for GVC that guides diffusion denois-ing toward original pictures by transmitting residuals. It decomposes the distortion be-tween the codec reconstruction and the original pictures into residual segments, which can be transmitted to introduce explicit source constraints across different denoising steps. • We design a novel residual codec with a pixel-space video diffusion transformer as an en-tropy model. This diffusion model is capable of effective distribution modeling for residual signals, enabling entropy coding for selectively transmitted residual segments while pre-serving generative capability for non-transmitted ones. • Compared with existing methods, ResGVC achieves superior compression performance in terms of both perception quality and signal fidelity, while delivering better visual quality. 

2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 GENERATIVE VIDEO COMPRESSION 

Generative video compression (GVC) incorporates generative models into codecs to improve per-ceptual quality beyond the signal-fidelity objective of traditional video coding and fidelity-oriented NVC. Early GVC methods (Yang et al., 2022; Mentzer et al., 2022) mainly introduced generative 2Preprint adversarial networks (GANs) and perceptual losses into neural codecs. Although these methods improved perceptual realism, they often suffered from training instability and visual artifacts. Other work explored generative reconstruction on latent or tokenized representations. For example, GLC-Video (Qi et al., 2025) performed compression in latent space of a vector-quantized variational autoencoder, while ProGVC (Li et al., 2026b) proposed a progressive generative codec based on hierarchical multi-scale residual token maps. However, these methods remained constrained by the representational capacity of their latent or tokenized bottlenecks. Recently, emerging diffusion models provide strong generative priors for GVC due to their ability to synthesize realistic spatial and temporal details. Existing diffusion-based methods (Ma & Chen, 2025a; Mao et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026d;a; Ma & Chen, 2025b; 2026) typically adopt a latent com-pression pipeline, where the original video is encoded into a compact latent representation and a diffusion model is employed in the decoder to remove compression distortions from the decoded latent. DiffVC (Ma & Chen, 2025a) follows this paradigm by compressing videos in the latent do-main and using an image diffusion model to enhance decoded latent frames. GNVC-VD (Mao et al., 2026) further introduces a video diffusion transformer to jointly enhance decoded latent representa-tions. YODA (Li et al., 2026d) explores one-step diffusion-based video compression by embedding multiscale temporal reference features for both latent coding and latent denoising. Despite their promising perceptual quality, these methods still primarily perform decoder-side de-noising in latent space. Their denoising process is largely guided by generative priors, while explicit source constraints along the denoising path remain weak. Moreover, when built upon latent autoen-coders, they inherit an irreversible reconstruction bottleneck that discards fine details. In contrast, our method introduces residual coding into pixel-space denoising, enabling explicit source con-straints on the denoising process. 2.2 DIFFUSION MODEL WITH FLOW MATCHING 

Diffusion models have become a popular paradigm for visual generation because they progressively transform noisy samples into clean data using learned denoising dynamics (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021a;b). Recent work has developed diffusion architectures based on U-Net (Ronneberger et al., 2015) and transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) backbones for high-quality image and video generation (Peebles & Xie, 2023; Wan et al., 2025). To improve efficiency, many methods (Rombach et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2024) perform denoising in a compressed latent space which is produced by a variational autoencoder (VAE) (Kingma & Welling, 2013). While this design substantially reduces computation, it inherits the reconstruction bottleneck of the autoencoder. In contrast, pixel-space diffusion models directly denoise visual signals in the original signal space and can better preserve fine-grained details (Li & He, 2026; Wang et al., 2026; Yu et al., 2026; Ma et al., 2026). Flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023) provides an effective formulation for diffusion-based generative modeling by learning a continuous denoising path from a random noise sample to clean data. 3 METHODOLOGY 

ResGVC represents a residual coding framework for high-fidelity GVC. It first compresses original pictures within a learned video codec (Li et al., 2026c), and then refines the decoded pictures using residual information through iterative residual coding. The whole diffusion process is performed di-rectly in pixel domain, avoiding the irreversible bottleneck of latent space. Concretely, the distortion between the decoded pictures and the original pictures is decomposed into a sequence of residual segments, each obtained at a denoising step. To encode these segments efficiently, we design a resid-ual codec in which a pixel-space video diffusion model serves as a conditional probability estimator for entropy coding. Section 3.1 formulates the residual segments from the codec reconstruction to the original pictures. Section 3.2 presents the proposed residual codec and its diffusion-based en-tropy model. Section 3.3 describes a skip mode for residual coding, which skips residual coding at selected denoising steps and replaces it with decoder-side residual generation for reducing bitrate. 3.1 FROM DENOISING PATH TO RESIDUAL CODING

ResGVC supports a skip mode, in which residual coding is applied only to selected denoising steps. For the skipped steps, the corresponding residual segments are not transmitted but are instead gener-ated at the decoder without incurring additional bitrate or quantization error, as illustrated in Fig. 4. Specifically, each residual segment ˆRt can be recovered at the decoder in one of two modes: resid-ual coding , in which ˆRt is reconstructed from the bitstream, and residual generation , in which ˆRt is predicted by the diffusion model, i.e. , VideoPiT. Residual coding is employed in the earlier denoising steps, which are more crucial to the final reconstruction quality, to faithfully transmit fidelity-critical residual segments. In the later steps, by leveraging the informative context from previously decoded residual signals, VideoPiT serves as a generator to estimate μt and σt without bitstream constraints. The estimated μt is directly used as the predicted ˆRt, while σt is discarded. Therefore, given a total of T denoising steps, the number of residual-coding steps determines the trade-off between bitrate and reconstruction quality, and its effect is further analyzed in Section 4.3. Although transmitted and generated residual segments are obtained in different ways, they are in-corporated using the same state update rule in Eq. 5. 4 EXPERIMENT 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL 

Training details. ResGVC is trained on the OpenVid-1M dataset (Nan et al., 2025) with a rate-distortion loss function, i.e. , L = R + λD . R denotes the total bitrate cost from the learned video codec and the proposed residual codec. D denotes the reconstruction distortion, computed as the sum of L2 loss, LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018), and DISTS (Ding et al., 2020). To evaluate compres-sion performance at multiple bitrates, we train four independent models with trade-off parameter

5 CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we present ResGVC, a residual coding framework for generative video compression that guides diffusion denoising toward the original pictures by transmitting residual signals. Res-GVC first encodes the original pictures with a learned video codec, and then decomposes the distor-tion between the codec reconstruction and the original pictures into a sequence of residual segments over denoising steps. These residual segments can further by compressed by a novel residual codec with a diffusion transformer as an entropy model, enabling explicit source constraints to be intro-duced throughout denoising rather than relying on decoder-side generation alone. By denoising directly in pixel space, ResGVC also avoids the irreversible information loss caused by latent rep-resentation bottlenecks. Experimental results demonstrate that ResGVC achieves state-of-the-art compression performance, reducing BD-rate by 63.7% in LPIPS and 83.8% in PSNR compared with the previous state-of-the-art method, while also delivering superior visual quality. A.1 MODEL IMPLEMENTATIONS 

Analysis Transform and Synthesis Transform. The proposed residual codec employs analysis transform ga and synthesis transform gs to reduce the redundancy within each residual segment. 

ga and gs are built upon the encoder and decoder architecture of the inter model in DCVC-UF HT-L (Li et al., 2026c), with two principal distinctions. First, our ga and gs are shared by both intra-coding and inter-coding, with the adapter introduced for the intra-frame to align the input channels of ga. By contrast, DCVC-UF employs two separate models for intra- and inter-coding. Second, we employ the previously reconstructed GOP ˆxp0 as condition information, whereas DCVC-UF relies on memory updated using features from decoded frames. 

Diffusion-based Entropy Model. In our framework, the video pixel diffusion transformer (Video-PiT) serves as an entropy model, in which each PiT block is built upon the T2I variant of Pix-elDiT (Yu et al., 2026). We employ the previously reconstructed GOP ˆxp0 as the conditioning input, whereas PixelDiT conditions on features derived from the text embedding. In VideoPiT, the input 

ˆxt first undergoes 16 × 16 patchification, followed by six PiT blocks. The output feature of the final PiT block is linearly projected to produce the probability parameters μt and σt for entropy coding. Notably, both μt and σt have spatial dimensions downsampled by a factor of 16 relative to ˆxt.A.2 MORE TRAINING DETAILS

Among the GVC methods, the proposed ResGVC demonstrates clear superiority over the other baselines, achieving approximately 10% bitrate savings on each test set compared with the second-best method GNVC-VD. Notably, the performance gap between DCVC-FM and ResGVC is relatively small, with the BD-rate difference remaining below 10% across all three test sets. A.4 ADDITIONAL ANALYSIS 

A.4.1 EMPLOYING OUR FRAMEWORK IN PIXEL SPACE vs. LATENT SPACE 

The proposed ResGVC is employed in pixel space rather than in latent space produced by autoen-coders commonly adopted in previous GVC methods. In Section 4.3, we conduct an ablation study in which residual coding is instead performed in latent space by inserting ResGVC between the VAE encoder and decoder. Here, we provide the implementation details. Following (Mao et al., 2026), we adopt the VAE from Wan2.1 (Wan et al., 2025) as the autoencoder, which performs 8× spatial downsampling for all frames and 4× temporal downsampling for inter-frames only. As described in Section 3.2, ga transforms the to-be-encoded ˆRt into ˆyt for subsequent entropy coding. The spatial resolution of ˆyt is reduced by a factor of 16 relative to ˆRt through an 

8 × 8 patchification and a convolutional layer with 2× downsampling within ga. To shift ResGVC to latent space, we encode two latent frames into ˆyt, corresponding to eight frames as a GOP in pixel space, and remove the patchification to ensure that ˆyt retains the same dimensions as in pixel-space residual coding. Besides, 16 ×16 patchification in the VideoPiT is replaced with 2×2 patchification.

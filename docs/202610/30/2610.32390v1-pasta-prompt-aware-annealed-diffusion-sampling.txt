PASTA: Prompt-Aware Shared-Latent
Training-Free Annealed Diffusion Sampling

Abstract. While Diffusion models produce high-quality images, real-
world visual design requires intentionally preserving clean regions for
headlines, logos, or UI elements. Existing layout-aware text-to-image
methods struggle to strictly preserve reserved regions, often introduc-
ing unwanted content that complicates downstream editing. Existing
approaches either manipulate attention to displace content or suppress
conditioning via noise-level interventions. However, these methods sacri-
fice visual coherence, producing flat regions or noticeable style inconsis-
tencies between reserved and non-reserved areas. To address this limita-
tion, we propose a Prompt-Aware Shared-Latent Training-Free Annealed
Diffusion Sampling (PASTA), a training-free framework that enforces
reserved-region constraints by selectively applying semantic guidance to
object-allowed regions during sampling. PASTA operates on a shared
latent trajectory, where full-prompt and context-prompt guidance are
fused through mask-guided sampling. An annealed schedule suppresses
semantic leakage during early denoising stages while gradually restoring
visual consistency in later stages. Extensive experiments demonstrate
that PASTA improves reserved-region fidelity while preserving overall
image realism and perceptual consistency.
Keywords: Space-Aware Text-to-Image, Image Generation, Diffusion

place objects but also to reserve clean regions for text, logos, or interface ele-
ments, such as headlines, UI components, or subtitles. These reserved regions
should remain free of salient content while visually matching the surrounding
background. Existing layout-aware text-to-image methods do not treat reserved

Several recent works have explored space-aware text-to-image generation to
address this need. TextCenGen [14] manipulates cross-attention to push object
evidence away from a target region and free space for text placement. While
effective when only mild repositioning is needed, attention relocation may have
difficulty satisfying large, strictly reserved regions. SAWNA [20] injects non-
reactive noise into reserved regions, ensuring that no objects are generated from
the start. Although this strategy reliably enforces empty space, suppression pro-
duces monotone regions, leading to noticeable visual inconsistencies between re-
served and non-reserved areas. As illustrated in Fig. 1, existing methods either
allow foreground content to intrude into the reserved region or produce visually
inconsistent backgrounds.
To address this gap, we propose a novel Prompt-Aware Shared-Latent Training-
Free Annealed Diffusion Sampling (PASTA) that explicitly enforces reserved-
region constraints while preserving background naturalness via annealed mask-
guided fusion of full-prompt and context-prompt guidance. At each diffusion
step, PASTA evaluates a shared latent under negative-prompt, full-prompt, and
context-prompt conditions, derives guidance directions relative to the negative-
prompt prediction, and spatially fuses them using a user-defined reserved-region
mask. An annealed blending schedule further prevents early semantic leakage
while enabling natural refinement in later steps.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that PASTA improves fidelity in reserved
regions while maintaining competitive performance in both quantitative and
perceptual evaluations. Our contributions are summarized as follows:
– We propose PASTA, a prompt-aware shared-latent training-free diffusion
sampling framework that spatially fuses guidance residuals to suppress fore-
ground semantic leakage in reserved regions while preserving generation con-
sistency in editable regions.
– We introduce an annealed guidance blending schedule that applies stronger
context-prompt guidance during early denoising stages and progressively re-

Related Work
2.1
Controllable Diffusion-based Image Generation
Diffusion models have become a dominant framework for high-fidelity image syn-
thesis by iteratively denoising Gaussian noise, and large-scale text-conditioned
variants enable photorealistic and diverse generation from prompts [26]. To
improve controllability, prior work has explored various conditioning strate-
gies, including structural cues (edges, sketches) [15,5], geometric cues (depth,
pose) [29,33], and low-level appearance constraints (color) [4,22]. Moreover, training-
free inference-time control methods manipulate guidance [9,3], attention [6,21],
or latent representations [18,19] to achieve flexible generation behavior without
additional training. Recent studies have further explored layered representations
that decompose images into editable foreground and background components,
aligning generation with designer-centric pipelines [32,10]. As layered genera-
tion matures, designers increasingly compose visuals by editing separate layers
for text and foreground objects. This workflow introduces a new requirement
for text-to-image generation: preserving clean reserved regions that remain free
from unintended foreground semantics during generation.
2.2
Spatially-aware Text-to-Image Generation
Layout-aware text-to-image generation improves compositional controllability
by conditioning generation on spatial constraints such as bounding boxes or
region masks [12,31]. Existing methods achieve spatial control either through
explicit conditioning mechanisms or by manipulating attention and guidance
during sampling in a training-free manner [1,30]. These methods mainly use
bounding boxes or region masks to specify where target objects should appear
in the generated image.
Our setting is fundamentally different. Instead of attracting foreground con-
tent into designated regions, the objective of reserved-region generation is to
prevent salient foreground semantics from occupying reserved regions. Although
layout-guided placement methods are related to spatial controllability, these
methods are not direct baselines for the reserved-region objective considered
in this work. In real design workflows, however, specifying object locations is of-
ten insufficient. Designers frequently need to reserve clean regions for text, logos,
or interface elements, which are typically added or edited outside the generative
model.

To address this problem, space-aware text-to-image generation has been ex-
plored to explicitly enforce empty regions during sampling. TextCenGen [14] re-
locates object-related attention away from regions reserved for text placement,
but may have difficulty satisfying large and strictly reserved regions. SAWNA [20]
instead suppresses generation from the start via non-reactive noise, preserving
space at the cost of producing flat or visually inconsistent reserved regions.
Motivated by these limitations, we propose a training-free sampling strategy
that enforces clean reserved regions while preserving background naturalness via
mask-guided fusion of full-prompt/context-prompt guidance with an annealed
schedule.
3
PASTA Framework
As shown in Fig. 2, PASTA is a training-free diffusion sampling framework that
spatially fuses shared-latent guidance residuals to suppress foreground semantic
leakage inside reserved regions. Given a full prompt Pfull and a reserved-region
mask M ∈{0, 1}h×w at the latent resolution, where M = 1 denotes the region
to keep free of salient foreground content, PASTA modifies the classifier-free
guidance direction during sampling.
We construct a context-prompt guidance Pctx by removing the main-object
descriptors from Pfull while preserving contextual descriptors such as background,
scene, and style. Specifically, we use ChatGPT 5.2 with temperature 0 and a fixed
decomposition template to split Pfull into a foreground phrase and a context
phrase, and set Pctx to the context phrase. Thus, Pctx provides context guidance
without explicitly encouraging the foreground object that should be excluded
from the reserved region.
3.1
Shared-Latent Residual Fusion
Let T denote the total number of inference steps, and let k ∈{1, . . . , T} denote
the index of the denoising step along the reverse sampling trajectory. Here, k = 1
corresponds to the first and noisiest denoising step, and k = T corresponds to the
final denoising step. We denote by z(k−1) the latent input to the k-th denoising
step.
At each denoising step k, PASTA evaluates the same UNet on the same
latent z(k−1) under three conditions: negative-prompt, full-prompt-conditioned,
and context-prompt-conditioned. Formally, we compute
ϵneg(k) = ϵθ(z(k−1), Pneg),

foreground semantic leakage inside reserved regions.
3.2
Mask-guided Annealed Guidance Blending
PASTA applies different guidance directions inside and outside the reserved re-
gion to selectively suppress foreground semantic leakage while preserving object
generation quality in editable regions. Inside the reserved region, we use ∆ctx(k)
to suppress foreground-object semantics while retaining contextual guidance.
Outside the reserved region, the full-prompt guidance should be preserved to
generate the foreground object. However, strong full-prompt guidance in early
denoising steps may cause object semantics to leak into the reserved region,
especially under large reserved masks.
To control this trade-off, we introduce an annealed mixing weight α(k). We
set the schedule boundaries as fixed fractions of the total number of inference
steps: u1 = 0.1T and u2 = 0.3T. The weight is kept at its maximum value
during the initial denoising stage, decayed in the middle stage, and set to zero

Experiments
4.1
Experimental Setup
We adopt Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL) in PASTA to generate images at 1024 ×
1024 resolution. We use UniPCMultistepScheduler with CFG scale s = 6.5 and

T = 50 inference steps. For annealing, we use Eq. (3) with αmax = 3, p = 2,
u1 = 0.1T, and u2 = 0.3T. With T = 50, this corresponds to u1 = 5 and u2 = 15.
These hyperparameters were selected empirically in preliminary experiments
and fixed for all reported results. We compare our method with space-aware
text-to-image methods, TextCenGen [14] and SAWNA [20]. All baselines are
evaluated with their released implementations and configurations. We compare
PASTA with TextCenGen and SAWNA, which directly address reserved-region
preservation or object exclusion for downstream text placement. Layout-guided
placement methods mainly use spatial conditions to encourage objects to appear
in specified regions, whereas our task uses the reserved-region mask to prevent
salient foreground objects from appearing in those regions. Therefore, our ex-
perimental comparisons focus on methods that share the same reserved-region
objective.
4.2
Dataset
We conduct experiments on the P2P [8] and SynPrompt [14] datasets accord-
ing to TextCenGen [14], which are benchmarks for space-aware text-to-image
generation, providing paired text prompts and reserved spatial masks. In the
original setup, each prompt is evaluated under a small-mask setting using five
reserved-region masks, whose average area ratio is approximately 3.8%.
While this setting reflects lightweight text-placement scenarios, it does not
capture practical design cases that require large clean regions. To evaluate ro-
bustness under stricter and more realistic constraints, we extend the evaluation
protocol by introducing an additional large-mask setting. Specifically, for each
prompt, we introduce ten reserved-region masks with an average area ratio of
approximately 30%, simulating scenarios that require substantial negative space.
4.3
Metrics
We evaluate overall generation quality and text–image alignment using CLIP
Score [24] and HPSv2 [28]. The CLIP Score measures the semantic consistency
between the generated image and the input prompt, where higher values indicate
better alignment. HPSv2 is a perceptual quality metric that aggregates human
preference by jointly considering visual quality and semantic plausibility. To
specifically evaluate reserved-region fidelity, we introduce an intrusion rate. For
each generated image, we first perform object detection with YOLO-World [2]
with the foreground object name specified in the prompt as a text query. De-
tected bounding boxes are then processed by SAM2 [25] to obtain pixel-level
foreground masks, which are aggregated across candidate masks and bounding
boxes into a single foreground mask. We compare this aggregated mask with
the predefined reserved-region mask, where white regions indicate areas where
objects should not appear. The intrusion rate is defined as the proportion of
foreground pixels falling inside the reserved region, with lower values indicating
better suppression. When YOLO-World detects no foreground objects, we ex-
clude the sample from the intrusion-rate computation. In our evaluation, such

Qualitative Results

4.5
Quantitative Results
Table 1 summarizes quantitative results on P2P and SynPrompt prompts under
small and large reserved-region masks. PASTA consistently achieves substan-
tially lower intrusion rates than SDXL and TextCenGen under the large-mask
setting, while maintaining higher CLIP and HPS scores than SAWNA. In con-
trast, TextCenGen shows higher intrusion rates than PASTA under large masks,
and the vanilla SDXL baseline violates the spatial constraint. Under the small-
mask setting, PASTA remains competitive with existing space-aware methods
in terms of CLIP and HPS scores. TextCenGen achieves higher CLIP and HPS
scores than PASTA under the large-mask setting on both datasets, while PASTA
achieves substantially lower intrusion rates. SAWNA yields the lowest intrusion
rates under large masks but shows lower CLIP and HPS scores. Overall, PASTA
provides a favorable balance between reserved-region enforcement and genera-
tion quality, substantially reducing intrusion under large masks while maintain-
ing competitive text-image alignment and perceptual quality across both P2P
and SynPrompt prompts.
4.6
Ablation Study
We conduct an ablation study to analyze the effect of the annealed mixing
weight α on P2P prompts and the large-mask setting, highlighting differences in
reserved-region fidelity.
Table 2 compares our full method with a variant that removes the annealed
weight schedule. Without α, the model achieves slightly higher CLIP and HPS
scores, indicating stronger text alignment and perceptual quality. However, this
comes at the cost of a substantially higher intrusion rate, leading to foreground
objects frequently leaking into the reserved region. In contrast, introducing the
annealed α dramatically reduces the intrusion rate, demonstrating the impor-
tance of time-dependent guidance control for enforcing clean reserved regions.
Although CLIP and HPS scores decrease slightly, the model remains competitive
while achieving better spatial fidelity. These results confirm that the annealed
weight schedule plays a crucial role in balancing semantic preservation and spa-
tial constraint enforcement, and is essential for robust performance under large
reserved-mask settings. To further clarify the visual effect of the annealed sched-
ule, Fig. 4 compares different choices of α using the same prompt, reserved-region
mask, and random seed. The qualitative results are consistent with Table 2.
Without α, the foreground object clearly intrudes into the reserved region, and
distracting vegetation-like structures remain inside the mask. A weak constant
weight α = 1 is still insufficient to produce a clean reserved region, while a
strong constant weight α = 5 suppresses the intrusion more aggressively but
also over-suppresses the foreground object, producing it at an undesirably small
scale. In contrast, the proposed annealed schedule preserves a cleaner reserved
region while maintaining an appropriate foreground-object size. This indicates
that the schedule does not simply increase suppression strength, but balances
reserved-region cleanliness and prompt-faithful foreground generation.

4.7
Limitation & Future work
While PASTA effectively handles a wide range of space-aware generation sce-
narios, it has limitations under extreme spatial constraints. In particular, when
the reserved region occupies a very large portion of the image (e.g., more than
80%) or when the remaining generation area becomes extremely narrow, the
model may fail to produce plausible content. In such cases, the denoising pro-
cess struggles to reconcile the strong spatial restriction with the learned image
priors.
We hypothesize that these failure cases arise because such configurations
are underrepresented or absent in the training data of large-scale text-to-image
models. Addressing these extreme scenarios may require explicitly curated train-
ing data or fine-tuning strategies tailored to highly constrained layouts. We leave
the exploration of data-driven or adaptive training approaches for handling these
rare but practically relevant cases as future work.

Conclusion
We proposed PASTA, a training-free diffusion sampler for space-aware text-to-
image generation. By operating on a shared latent trajectory and selectively
fusing semantic guidance with an annealed schedule, PASTA enforces reserved-
region constraints while preserving background naturalness. Experiments on
both P2P and SynPrompt show that PASTA significantly improves reserved-
region fidelity under large-mask settings while remaining competitive in text
alignment and perceptual quality. These results suggest that PASTA provides a
practical solution for design scenarios requiring clean regions for text or interface
elements.

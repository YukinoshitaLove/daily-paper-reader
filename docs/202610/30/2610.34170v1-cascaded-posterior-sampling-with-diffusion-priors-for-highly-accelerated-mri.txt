# CASCADED POSTERIOR SAMPLING WITH DIFFUSION PRIORS FOR HIGHLY ACCELERATED MRI 

# Jyothi Rikhab Chand, Sahil Murtaza, Patrick Nicholas Nowak, Mathews Jacob 

# Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Virginia, VA, USA 

ABSTRACT 

Diffusion models are powerful priors for inverse problems, in-cluding accelerated MRI. However, modeling the distribution of high-resolution images requires large networks with high computational cost. We propose a posterior sampling frame-work that uses cascaded diffusion models as multiscale priors. Conditioned on a coarse image, the distribution of fine-scale detail is local and well conditioned, and it can therefore be captured by a small network. Similarly, a small unconditional network is sufficient to represent low-resolution images, and it is used to recover the coarse image from the k-space cen-ter. This estimate then conditions the high-resolution prior, which is combined with all measurements to recover the final image. Both priors are trained on images alone, so they apply to any sampling pattern, and data consistency remains linear at every scale. In multi-coil brain MRI, the proposed method improves on the best single-scale diffusion baseline by 4–6 dB in PSNR at 15× acceleration, while each of its separately trained networks is about 10× smaller. The gain grows as the problem becomes more ill-posed, and ablations show that it arises from informative low-resolution conditioning. 

Index Terms — Cascaded Diffusion Models, Posterior Sampling, Multiscale Priors, MRI Reconstruction 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Diffusion models learn complex high-dimensional image dis-tributions [1, 2] and are now widely used as priors for inverse problems in imaging [3]. Because a trained diffusion model is not tied to a specific acquisition operator, the same model can be combined with different forward models in a plug-and-play (PnP) manner [4–6]. This flexibility is a key advantage over end-to-end (E2E) methods [7–9], which are trained for a particular acquisition setting. Most diffusion priors rely on a single network that op-erates on the full-resolution image, which is challenging for large images. The coarse image structure is formed at high noise levels, where the denoiser must capture long-range de-pendencies across the entire image. At full resolution, this requires large networks with global receptive fields [10], and hence high computational cost. Posterior sampling [3] adds a further difficulty. The guidance from the measurements is least accurate at high noise levels, which is where the coarse image structure is determined. Consequently, at high acceler-ation, the coarse anatomy may be recovered incorrectly, even when the the k-space center is densely sampled. Many inverse problems hence rely on latent diffusion models, which reduce the computational cost by operating in the lower-dimensional latent space of an autoencoder [11]. However, the reconstruc-tion accuracy is then bounded by the autoencoder, and the decoder makes the data-consistency update nonlinear [12]. In this work, we rely on multiscale diffusion models, which can overcome these challenges while preserving pixel-space data consistency. These models exploit the redundancy of images across scales, which had been widely used in image processing for image compression [13, 14]. Recent generative models extend this idea through coarse-to-fine factorizations [15, 16], in which a fine-scale diffusion model is conditioned a coarse-scale image. Given the coarse im-age, the distribution of fine-scale details is better conditioned and more local than that of the full image, and hence it can be captured by small networks with limited receptive fields. Only the coarse-scale model must capture the long-range structure that is formed at high noise levels. Because the coarse image is low-dimensional, this global model can also be small [10,17]. This factorization is particularly well suited to accelerated MRI, where sampling patterns acquire the cen-ter of k-space densely and the periphery sparsely [18]. Alow-resolution image can therefore be recovered from the central samples as a separate, much better-posed inverse problem, rather than through the high-noise steps of a full-resolution sampler. This coarse estimate provides reliable, measurement-derived information that constrains the fine-scale prior, where most of the ambiguity lies. In this paper, we introduce cascaded posterior sampling, which uses cascaded diffusion models [15] as measurement-informed multiscale priors. In the first stage, a low-resolution diffusion prior recovers a coarse image from the central k-space samples. In the second stage, this coarse estimate conditions a high-resolution diffusion prior, which is com-bined with all measurements to recover the final image. Each prior is about 10× smaller than a single-scale model. Because the priors are trained and used in inference separately, the peak memory and computational complexity are governed by one small network. In multi-coil MRI, the proposed method arXiv:2610.34170v1 [eess.IV] 25 Oct 2026 matches single-scale diffusion baselines at moderate acceler-ation and improves on the best baseline by 4–6 dB in PSNR at 15× acceleration. Denoising experiments confirm that the gain comes from informative low-resolution conditioning rather than from the additional network input. 

2. BACKGROUND 

The proposed framework applies broadly to computational imaging inverse problems; in this work, we focus on acceler-ated MRI. Let x ∈ Cm denote the unknown high-resolution MR image to be recovered from the acquired undersampled multi-coil k-space measurements y ∈ Cn. The measurement model is given by: 

y = Ax + n (1) where A = SFC denotes the multi-coil forward oper-ator, S is the sampling mask, F is the Fourier matrix, C

is the coil sensitivity map, and n ∼ N (0 , η 2I). Under a Bayesian formulation, one can recover an MR image by sam-pling from the posterior distribution p(x|y) ∝ p(y|x)p(x),where p(y|x) denotes the likelihood and p(x) denotes the MR image data distribution. Recently, diffusion models have emerged as powerful priors and have been adapted to sam-ple from the posterior distribution. Given a diffusion model trained on MR images, the score network provides an esti-mate of the score of the perturbed image distribution. Several diffusion-based posterior sampling algorithms have been pro-posed for solving inverse problems by combining the learned diffusion model with the measurement likelihood [4–6, 12]. For more details, see [3].

4. RESULTS 4.1. Dataset 

We evaluate the proposed cascaded diffusion framework on two-dimensional accelerated parallel T2-weighted MRI re-construction. The dataset was obtained from the publicly available fastMRI brain dataset [19]. It consists of sixteen-channel brain data with complex images of size 384 × 384 .We evaluate the reconstruction performance across different acceleration factors and sampling geometries, including one-dimensional Cartesian and two-dimensional undersampling patterns. 

4.2. Architecture and Implementation 

4.2.1. Cascaded Diffusion Model 

We trained a low-resolution diffusion prior on 96 × 96 im-ages obtained by applying 4 × 4 average pooling with a stride of 4 to the original 384 × 384 training images. We also trained a high-resolution diffusion prior conditioned on the corresponding low-resolution images. Each condition-ing image is interpolated to 384 × 384 and concatenated with the high-resolution image along the channel dimension. Following [15], we apply conditioning augmentation to the low-resolution inputs during training to improve robustness to imperfect low-resolution conditions at inference. Because the conditioning images are obtained directly from the train-ing data, the two models can be trained in parallel. Both

4.2.2. State-of-the-art (SOTA) methods for comparison 

We evaluate the performance of the cascaded diffusion model against the unconditional diffusion model-based algorithms. In particular, we compare with ALPS [6], DAPS [5], and DPS [4]. Each algorithm was implemented using the settings recommended in the respective paper. We used a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model that follows the DDPM++ architecture. We call this model unconditional, although it takes the noise level as input. This model was trained using the EDM hyperparameters, loss function, and noise schedule. The number of model parameters is 65.5 million, which is around 10 × the size of the cascaded networks and it operates at 384 × 384 image size.

5. CONCLUSION 

We introduced a cascaded posterior sampling framework that uses diffusion models as measurement-informed multiscale priors. The framework recovers a coarse image from the densely sampled center of k-space and uses it to condition a fine-scale prior. The coarse scale is thus well determined by the data, and the learned prior mainly resolves fine-scale de-tail, where the data are weak. In multi-coil MRI, the method improved on the best baseline by 4–6 dB in PSNR at 15 ×

acceleration, with networks that are each about 10 × smaller and faster to train. Because both priors are trained on images alone, they apply to any sampling pattern, and the data con-sistency remains linear at every scale. These properties make the approach attractive for 3D and high-resolution imaging, where full-resolution priors are costly to train and data are scarce. Future work will extend the cascade to more scales and to joint inference across scales.

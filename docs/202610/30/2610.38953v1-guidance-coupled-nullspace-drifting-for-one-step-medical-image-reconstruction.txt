# Guidance-Coupled Nullspace Drifting for One-Step Medical Image Reconstruction 

M. Tuna Alatan, A. Server Tapan, Saban Ozturk, M. Usama Mirza, Bilal Kabas, Ge Wang, and Tolga C ¸ ukur 

Abstract — Generative reconstruction can recover high-quality images from undersampled acquisitions, but state-of-the-art generative priors commonly rely on iterative sam-pling. Reducing the number of iterations lowers inference cost but also removes opportunities for progressive distri-butional refinement, making it difficult to retain image real-ism without sacrificing reconstruction fidelity. Drift model-ing offers an alternative by shifting progressive refinement to training, thereby enabling one-step sampling without an iterative inference trajectory. Existing drifting methods, however, do not account for the imaging operator when modeling distributional drift. We propose DriftRecon, a gen-erative method for one-step medical image reconstruction that performs drifting directly in the nullspace of the imag-ing operator. DriftRecon uses nullspace-restricted noise to construct training samples and a multiscale generator that incorporates measurement conditioning across resolution levels, with its generated component projected onto the operator nullspace. To control the fidelity–realism trade-off at inference, a conditional guidance parameter contin-uously steers the same generator between measurement-conditioned and unconditional generation. We derive aguidance-dependent similarity-kernel temperature sched-ule that preserves the guidance-weighted mass receiving strengthened supervision, thereby progressively localizing drift interactions as guidance increases to limit the associ-ated loss of reconstruction fidelity. Experiments in sparse-view CT and accelerated MRI reconstruction show that DriftRecon outperforms established generative reconstruc-tion methods and recent drifting models while producing reconstructions in a single forward pass. 

Index Terms — medical image reconstruction, nullspace, drifting, MRI, CT 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Medical image reconstruction aims to recover high-quality images from undersampled data acquired according to aprescribed imaging operator determined by the acquisition physics and sampling pattern [1], [2]. Because reconstruction is an underdetermined problem, reliable recovery depends jointly on physical guidance from the imaging operator and learned image priors that capture the anatomical structure and detailed characteristics of high-quality images [3]–[7]. In recent years, generative image priors based on diffusion and flow modeling have made substantial progress in this setting, recovering high-quality images from accelerated MRI [8]–[11]

By modeling a dis-tribution of plausible images rather than a single deterministic mapping, generative methods can also support different re-construction characteristics along the fidelity–realism trade-off [16], [17]. Despite these advantages, these methods commonly rely on iterative sampling processes that map noise or un-dersampled data toward reconstructions consistent with fully-sampled acquisitions through a sequence of smaller mappings [8], [10]–[12]. Through successive updates, iterative sam-pling progressively refines the reconstruction toward the target image distribution while providing repeated opportunities to correct errors in anatomical structure. The resulting inference process, however, incurs substantial computational cost, which limits the practical utility of generative reconstruction. Since reducing generative reconstruction to a single net-work evaluation could improve inference efficiency while, in principle, retaining the distributional benefits of learned generative priors, several acceleration strategies have been proposed in the literature. Trajectory-compression methods include implicit sampling that skips intermediate steps [18], [19], progressive distillation that transfers a multistep teacher sampler into successively shorter student samplers [20], and consistency learning that trains predictions from different stages of the sampling process to approach a common solution [21], [22]. Task-specific trajectory design instead constructs the generative process around the reconstruction problem so that shorter sampling trajectories are supported inherently [13], [23]. Regardless of the strategy, compressing sampling toward a single evaluation removes the successive updates through which generative reconstruction progressively refines the image distribution [24]. One-step generation instead has to recover measurement-consistent image structure and realistic unresolved detail within a single mapping, without subsequent opportunities for correction [25]. The central challenge is therefore to retain the realism afforded by generative recon-struction without sacrificing reconstruction fidelity under one-step inference. Drift modeling offers an emerging alternative by shifting progressive distribution refinement from inference to training, during which the generator distribution is iteratively moved toward the target image distribution [26]. The refinement normally accumulated over multiple inference steps is there-fore learned during training and realized in a single gener-ator evaluation. This training-time evolution is driven by an attraction–repulsion field constructed between synthetic and target samples, with similarity-kernel temperatures controlling the locality of the corresponding interactions. Recent medical imaging studies have begun to explore conditional drifting with promising results across low-dose CT denoising [27], CT arXiv:2610.38953v1 [eess.IV] 28 Oct 2026 2

image synthesis [28], and MRI reconstruction [29]. Despite these encouraging results, current approaches do not incor-porate the imaging operator into the modeled distributional drift. In [27], [28] the drift is learned without reference to the underlying imaging operator, whereas [29] performs residual drifting before posthoc data-consistency (DC) enforcement. The drift can therefore extend into the range space of the imaging operator, causing the generator to modify image components already determined by the acquired data, so part of the generative update is subsequently removed without contributing to the final reconstruction [30], [31]. Existing drifting methods also offer limited control over the fidelity– realism trade-off: separate models with different similarity-kernel temperatures provide discrete operating points [27], whereas conditional–unconditional guidance enables contin-uous control within a single model but has been used with a fixed kernel temperature [26]. Because recent theoretical anal-ysis suggests that guidance strength and kernel temperature jointly determine the conditional drifting field [32], we hy-pothesize that increasing guidance without adapting the kernel temperature can strengthen mismatched drift interactions and thereby reduce reconstruction fidelity. Here we propose DriftRecon, a generative method for one-step medical image reconstruction that models distributional drift directly in the nullspace of the imaging operator. By restricting the drift to components not determined by the acquired data, DriftRecon preserves measurement-supported information while focusing generative modeling on the un-resolved degrees of freedom of the reconstruction problem (Fig. 1). This is realized through nullspace-restricted noise for constructing training samples and a multiscale generator that incorporates measurement conditioning across resolution lev-els before projecting its generated component onto the operator nullspace (Fig. 2). The imaging operator therefore constrains the distributional drift itself rather than being applied posthoc. To provide continuous control over the fidelity–realism trade-off, DriftRecon steers its generator between measurement-conditioned and unconditional generation. Increasing guid-ance, however, also strengthens the influence of mismatched real samples on the drift, which can compromise recon-struction fidelity when similarity-kernel temperature remains fixed. We therefore derive a guidance-dependent temperature schedule by preserving the guidance-weighted mass receiving strengthened supervision, progressively localizing drift inter-actions as guidance increases (Fig. 3). Comprehensive experi-ments on sparse-view CT and accelerated MRI demonstrate the effectiveness of DriftRecon against data-driven and physics-based baselines as well as diffusion, flow, and recent drifting approaches, while enabling continuous fidelity–realism control from a single one-step model. Consistency learning directly targets this regime by training predictions from different stages of the sampling process to approach a common solution [21], [22]. Although this enables one-step generation, medical image reconstruction with a consis-tency prior can still require iterative conjugate-gradient data-consistency updates after the network evaluation, partially off-setting the efficiency benefit of one-step inference [22]. Drift modeling takes a different route by moving the progressive evolution of the generator distribution to training, allowing the learned reconstruction mapping itself to be executed in a single network evaluation [26]. 

B. Drift Modeling in Medical Imaging 

Several recent studies have considered drifting for image synthesis and reconstruction tasks. [27] formulates residual drifting between low- and normal-dose CT distributions. [28] constructs a conditional drift between source and target image distributions for CT image synthesis and reconstruction. [29] adopts residual drifting for MRI reconstruction, defining the drift over a reconstruction residual before posthoc DC en-forcement. These recent methods do not impose an explicit constraint that restricts the learned drifting process to the operator nullspace. Consequently, the resulting drift estimates can contain components orthogonal to the nullspace that conflict with the acquired data. DriftRecon instead constrains both training perturbations and predicted drift estimates to the operator nullspace, confining the learned distributional drift to degrees of freedom not determined by measurements. A second challenge is controlling the fidelity–realism trade-off without sacrificing reconstruction fidelity. Existing drifting formulations approach this in two ways. One uses separately configured models with different similarity-kernel tempera-tures to obtain distinct fidelity–realism balances [27], pro-viding only discrete control and requiring multiple trained models. The other uses conditional–unconditional guidance to vary this balance continuously within a single model [26], but retains a fixed kernel temperature across guidance strengths. Because kernel temperature determines the locality of the attraction–repulsion interactions, stronger guidance can disrupt the conditional–unconditional balance without adapting the locality of the drift interactions [32], which can degrade reconstruction fidelity. DriftRecon instead decreases kernel temperature with increasing guidance, progressively localizing drift interactions to limit fidelity loss while retaining continu-ous control over the fidelity–realism trade-off.

IV. M ETHODS 

A. Datasets 

CT experiments used 1-mm abdominal data from NIH– AAPM–Mayo Clinic Low Dose CT Grand Challenge [34]. Full-dose images were clipped to [−1000 , 2000] HU, scaled to [0 , 1] , and analyzed at 256 × 256 grid size. Training, validation, and test splits comprised 2,402, 305, and 263 cross-sections without subject overlap. Sparse-view sinograms were generated using a parallel-beam Radon transform with 364 detector bins and uniformly undersampled along the angular dimension at R = 6 , 12 .MRI experiments used multi-contrast brain MRI from M4Raw [35], acquired at 0.3 T with a four-channel coil. T 1-weighted, T 2-weighted, and FLAIR acquisitions were scaled to [0, 1] via maximum-intensity normalization and analyzed at 256 × 256 grid size, with coil sensitivities estimated using ESPIRiT [36]. Training, validation, and test splits comprised 18,432, 4,320, and 7,200 cross-sections without subject over-lap. Data were undersampled at R = 4 , 8 using 2D Gaussian variable-density patterns [37]. 

B. Implementation Details 

DriftRecon used a four-level multiscale convolutional gener-ator with feature dimensionalities of 64, 128, 256, and 512 and four residual blocks per stage [38]. Measurement conditioning was incorporated within each residual block as in [39], and 

α was provided through a learned embedding. For CT, drift supervision used L = 3, M = 2 similarity kernels with 

τm, 0 = {0.2, 1.0}, and λimg = 1 . For MRI, drift supervision used L = 0 , M = 1 with τ1,0 = 1 .0, and λimg = 0 .01 . For both modalities, σ = 0 .05 , β = 0 .5, and λdrift = 1 , while λnull 

was cosine annealed from 0.01 to 0. Guidance used α = 1 with probability 0.5 and otherwise α ∼ p(α) ∝ α−3 on [1 , 4] .

C. Competing Methods 

Baselines included modality-specific data-driven methods (UNet [40] for MRI; FBPConvNet [41] for CT), physics-driven methods (MoDL [7], MambaRoll [42], RAM [39]), few-step generative methods (SSDiffRecon [23] and FlowDPS [43], each with five sampling steps), and one-step drifting methods (RDDM [27], SA-RDM [29]). All methods operated on individual cross-sections, with MRI represented using two channels for the real and imaginary components and CT using a single channel. All models were trained from random ini-tialization using the same training data and imaging operators. Competing methods followed their published architectures and objectives, with key hyperparameters selected on the validation sets. Physics-driven unrolled methods used K = 3 cascades for CT and K = 5 for MRI. Models were trained with Adam for 80k iterations for CT and 20k iterations for MRI, with the learning rates selected from 10 −6–10 −3 and halved every 10k iterations. Performance was assessed using PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FID against reference CT or MR images from fully-sampled acquisitions. Performance differences were assessed using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. C. Effect of Guidance-Coupled Temperature Scheduling 

Increasing the guidance strength α places greater empha-sis on image characteristics learned by the prior, including high-spatial-frequency detail that is weakly constrained by undersampled measurements. This shifts the reconstruction from subject-specific anatomy supported by the acquired data toward characteristics represented more strongly by the learned prior. Under conventional guidance with a fixed similarity-kernel temperature (CFG), stronger guidance can therefore in-troduce plausible but subject-inaccurate detail, alter local con-trast, or amplify noise and artifacts. These changes can reduce agreement with the reference images, degrading subject-level PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS as well as distribution-level FID. The proposed guidance-coupled temperature scheduling alleviates these losses by progressively localizing the drift interactions strengthened at higher α, limiting guidance-induced deviations while retaining prior-supported image detail. For a controlled comparison, CFG was implemented within the same DriftRecon formulation by setting β = 0 ,which keeps the similarity-kernel temperature fixed, whereas guidance-coupled scheduling used β = 0 .25 . Table III com-pares the two formulations over α = 1 –4 and reports the metric changes between α = 1 and α = 4. Compared with CFG, DriftRecon improves the ∆1→4 changes by 1.4dB PSNR, 0.3% SSIM, 0.2 LPIPS, and 1.9 FID in CT. The corre-sponding improvements in MRI are 1.2dB PSNR, 1.6% SSIM, 0.7 LPIPS, and 4.6 FID. Thus, coupling kernel temperature to guidance strength consistently limits the degradation in reference-based quality metrics as guidance is increased. Fig. 6 provides a representative visual comparison of this behavior. Under CFG, increasing guidance produces progres-sively larger changes in tissue structure and local contrast, whereas DriftRecon better preserves subject-specific image content while still allowing sharper local tissue depiction. The enlarged region further shows that the proposed schedule re-tains finer structural detail with substantially smaller deviations from the α = 1 reconstruction.

VI. D ISCUSSION 

DriftRecon combines one-step generative reconstruction with explicit constraints from the imaging operator. Operator-nullspace drifting confines the learned prior to image com-ponents unresolved by the acquired measurements, while measurement conditioning incorporates the forward model throughout the generator. Guidance-coupled temperature scheduling provides continuous control over the fidelity– realism trade-off by progressively localizing drift interactions as guidance increases. Across accelerated MRI and sparse-view CT, DriftRecon outperformed established physics-driven and generative reconstruction methods as well as recent drift-ing approaches while requiring a single generator evalua-tion. Ablation studies supported the contributions of operator-nullspace drifting, measurement conditioning, and auxiliary supervision, while the guidance analysis showed that the proposed temperature schedule substantially limits fidelity loss at increased guidance strengths. The present formulation transfers progressive distribution refinement from inference to training and reconstructs each image in a single generator evaluation. Additional inference computation could nevertheless be useful when further refine-ment is warranted. Drift Flow Matching recently combined direct drifting with optional flow-based refinement, enabling test-time scaling between one-step generation and iterative transport [44]. Diffusion-bridge formulations have also been investigated for medical image synthesis [45]. A related ex-tension of DriftRecon could retain one-step reconstruction as the default while allowing a small number of nullspace-restricted refinement steps for challenging acquisitions or when additional computation is available, without returning to the long inference of conventional diffusion models. A second direction concerns inference-time adaptation of the fidelity–realism trade-off. DriftRecon currently uses a global guidance strength together with the correspond-ing guidance-dependent temperature. Acquisition-adaptive reconstruction has previously been considered in model-based networks [46], and related adaptation may be useful when acquisition characteristics vary across sites. Fed-erated reconstruction provides another relevant setting, particu-larly when institutions differ in sampling protocols, data distri-butions, or reconstruction models [47]. Extending DriftRecon in this direction could permit acquisition- or site-specific guidance while retaining a common generative formulation. The operator-nullspace formulation is independent of the particular reconstruction module used within the genera-tor. State-space models have recently been investigated for physics-guided MRI reconstruction [48], while MambaRoll has demonstrated multiscale state-space modeling for accel-erated MRI and sparse-view CT reconstruction [42]. Such modules could be incorporated into DriftRecon while retaining the same restriction of the generated component to the operator nullspace. State-space modeling has also been extended to multimodal medical image synthesis [49], providing a pos-sible direction for extending drifting beyond reconstruction to conditional image synthesis. The accuracy of the nullspace decomposition depends on the forward model used during reconstruction. In MRI, errors in coil-sensitivity estimation or trajectory specification alter the encoding operator, while CT reconstruction can be affected by inaccuracies in acquisition geometry or system calibration. Future work can examine robustness to such model mismatch and, where needed, jointly refine the relevant acquisition parameters during reconstruction. Extension to non-Cartesian MRI and additional CT acquisition geometries would likewise require appropriate realization of the corresponding imaging operator and nullspace. These factors determine how accu-rately measurement-supported and unresolved image compo-nents can be separated in practice. 

VII. C ONCLUSION 

We introduced DriftRecon, a one-step medical image recon-struction method that models distributional drift directly in the nullspace of the imaging operator. DriftRecon combines nullspace-restricted perturbations and generated components with guidance-coupled temperature scheduling to preserve measurement-supported image content while controlling the fidelity–realism trade-off. Across accelerated MRI and sparse-view CT, DriftRecon outperformed established generative re-10 

construction methods and recent drifting approaches while requiring a single generator evaluation. These results support operator-nullspace drifting as an effective formulation for efficient generative medical image reconstruction.

ABSTRACT 

Fluctuation-based super-resolution techniques are an appeal-ing alternative to single-molecule and structured-illumination microscopy, as they adapt to standard acquisition settings and exploit the independent stochastic fluctuations of fluo-rophores across a sequence of frames. However, most existing approaches lack an explicit model of the acquisition process and provide only relative intensity estimates, limiting quan-titative analyses. In this work, we combine model-based and generative approaches to exploit the full stochastic informa-tion in the sequence, proposing a reconstruction framework built on flow matching and a double-Poisson forward model accounting for blinking, blur, under-sampling and noise. The method alternates two steps: a flow matching model first learns the distribution of the acquired data; then, the sample and the background are jointly estimated by minimising a Kullback–Leibler divergence between observed and simu-lated distributions via regularised proximal gradient steps. On simulated and real Argolight data, the method resolves lines separated by 120 nm, outperforming SACD and eSRRF. 

Index Terms — Fluctuation-based fluorescence microscopy, super resolution, distribution matching 

1. INTRODUCTION 

The spatial resolution of light microscopy is fundamentally limited by the diffraction limit, stemming directly from the physics of the optical system used for the acquisitions. Single-molecule localisation microscopy (SMLM) [1, 2] overcomes this limit by isolating and localising individ-ual emitters, but requires photo-switchable fluorophores, high illumination intensities and thousands of sparse frames, resulting in long acquisitions, costly hardware and poten-tial phototoxicity which limits its use in live-cell imaging. Fluctuation-based methods offer a cheaper alternative: they exploit the independent stochastic intensity fluctuations of fluorophores that naturally occur in standard widefield ac-quisitions, requiring minimal changes to the optical settings, being compatible with many fluorescent labels and relying on short captures. Starting from SOFI [3], which reduces the effective PSF size through temporal correlations of pixel intensities, several methods have been proposed: SRRF [4] and its improved version eSRRF [5] apply correlation anal-ysis to radiality maps, SACD [6] adds deconvolution steps to reduce out-of-focus contributions, while MUSICAL [7] and MSSR [8] follow alternative statistical strategies. Acomplementary line of work relies on sparse optimisation relying either on ℓ1 or ℓ0 relaxations, see SPARCOM [9] and COL0RME [10], respectively. Nonetheless, most of these methods exploit only selected statistics of the fluctuations (typically second-order) and provide relative intensities only, which limits quantitative downstream analyses. In this work, we propose a physics-aware learning ap-proach for image reconstruction which incorporates an ex-plicit double-Poisson forward model of the fluorophore blink-ing, blur, under-sampling and noise. Rather than relying on selected statistics, in analogy with FluoGAN [11], the proba-bility distribution of the observation sequence is here learned by means of a generative model, and then the forward pa-rameters (i.e., high-resolution signal and out-of-focus back-ground) are estimated with a tailored optimisation scheme so as to match the distribution of the observations. For this, we leverage recent advances in flow matching (FM) [12] and dif-fusion models [13] to learn the data distribution and adapt to our task the losses described in [14, 15]. The result is a gen-eral framework for model-based inverse problems from i.i.d. noisy observations, which we apply to fluctuation microscopy with promising results on simulated and real data.

NUMERICAL RESULTS 

We evaluate the capability of the proposed approach to super-resolve widefield fluorescence microscopy images, achieving resolutions smaller than the diffraction limit evaluated thanks to Argolight SIM slide. Our first results are on data simulated by means of the double-Poisson forward model (1). Next, we show the robustness of the proposed approach on real images of an ARGO-SIM pattern [5]. 

Simulated Argolight data. We simulate T = 500 frames of an ARGO-SIM calibration pattern consisting of 8 groups of 4 parallel lines. The centre-to-centre separation distance d

between the two inner lines increases progressively in steps of 

30 nm starting from 120 nm . Fluorophore emission along the lines is modelled pixel-wise using a Poisson distribution with 

β = 1 and corrupted according to (1) with an under sampling factor r = 6 , Gaussian blur with FWHM = 311 nm , and Poisson-Gaussian noise with α = 12 , σ = 5 . The corrupted frames have a final pixel size of 100 nm .To quantitatively assess line separation, we computed the 1D intensity profiles of the reconstructions by summing pixel in-tensities along the direction of the lines and normalising them in the range [0 , 1] . We evaluated our method on the simu-lated data and found optimal performance with regularisation weights λ1 = 0 .05 and λ2 = 40 in terms of the peak-to-valley ratio of the profiles. We compare our results with those obtained with SACD [6] (using 20 initial and 100 final LR it-erations and with the exact PSF) and eSRRF [5] (with radius 

R = 1 and sensitivity S = 1 ). As illustrated in Fig. 1, our method successfully resolves the two inner lines down to the finest tested separation of 120 nm , as also confirmed by the intensity profiles plot. In contrast, under the tested configu-rations, eSRRF and SACD are only capable of resolving line pairs separated by at least 300 nm and 240 nm , respectively. Fig. 2 shows the outputs at the different steps of our method, comparing observations from Yobs with those generated at Step 1, at the start of Step 2 and at convergence of Step 2. The strong visual alignment between Yobs and the samples drawn from the distribution p ¯Y learned at Step 1 confirms that FM accurately models the degradation process. Furthermore, the similarity between Yobs and the simulated degradation Ysim 

at the end of Step 2 validates that the variational distance min-imization yields physically consistent results.

4. CONCLUSIONS 

In this paper, we presented a physics-informed generative approach for fluctuation-based fluorescence microscopy super-resolution, combining flow matching techniques with model-based regularisation taking advantage of a double-Poisson problem describing the physical behaviour of the fluorophores and the whole image formation process.

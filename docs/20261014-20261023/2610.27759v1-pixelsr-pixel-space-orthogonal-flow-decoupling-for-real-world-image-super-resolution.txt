ABSTRACT
Latent diffusion models have recently achieved remarkable progress in Real-world
Image Super-Resolution (Real-SR). However, existing methods consistently ex-
hibit a persistent seesaw effect: enhancing perceptual realism compromises struc-
tural fidelity. This effect, we argue, is not intrinsic to diffusion models, but largely
stems from the architectural constraints of prevailing two-stage frameworks. To
address this challenge, we propose PixelSR, a novel single-stage Pixel Diffusion
Transformer for Real-SR that operates directly in pixel space. Specifically, Pix-
elSR consists of two core designs: Pixel-space Conditional Diffusion and Orthog-
onal Flow Decoupling. The former establishes an effective pixel-space restoration
framework by integrating spatially aligned LR conditioning, visual semantic guid-
ance, and lightweight texture refinement, thereby bypassing VAE compression
and enabling end-to-end optimization directly in RGB space. Complementarily,
the latter decomposes the asymmetric flow target into a low-rank semantic compo-
nent and a noise-free orthogonal texture component. By routing these components
to specialized branches, it achieves decoupled optimization with reduced mutual
interference. Benefiting from these designs, our PixelSR restores perceptually re-
alistic details while faithfully preserving structural content. Extensive experiments
across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that PixelSR not only achieves competi-
tive performance, but also effectively mitigates the fidelity–realism seesaw effect.

INTRODUCTION
Real-world Image Super-Resolution (Real-SR) aims to recover High-Resolution (HR) images from
Low-Resolution (LR) observations undergoing complex and unknown degradations (Fang et al.,
2025; Wu et al., 2026b). Recently, Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) have dominated this task by
performing denoising in the compressed latent space of a pre-trained VAE, substantially reducing
computational and memory costs (Rombach et al., 2022; Sheng et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024b).
Despite this efficiency, prior studies have highlighted fidelity limitations of latent representations,
with text and high-frequency details susceptible to degradation even during VAE reconstruction
alone (Yao et al., 2025; Medi1 & Rampini, 2026; Esser et al., 2024; Gupta et al., 2024). These
limitations are particularly relevant to Real-SR, where LDMs typically exhibit a seesaw effect: im-
proving perceptual realism often comes at the cost of fidelity to the LR, as illustrated in Figure 1.
Qualitatively, this effect manifests as structural distortions, severe text degradation, and content
hallucinations. Quantitatively, improvements in no-reference perceptual quality, as measured by
MANIQA and MUSIQ, are consistently accompanied by lower PSNR and SSIM, indicating de-
graded reference-based fidelity.
We argue that this seesaw effect is not an insurmountable limit of diffusion models, but rather stems
from two structural bottlenecks of the LDM paradigm: (1) pre-trained VAEs inevitably perform
lossy reconstruction, discarding high-frequency details and fine-grained structures, which inherently
limits the upper bound of structural fidelity; (2) the disjointed two-stage pipeline prevents end-to-end
optimization, causing reconstruction and approximation errors to cascade and accumulate between
latent generation and pixel decoding. These architectural limitations motivate a fundamental ques-
tion: Can we bypass the latent bottleneck and perform Real-SR directly in the pixel space?
Emerging advancements in pixel-space diffusion have demonstrated the feasibility of generative
modeling directly on raw pixels without relying on a pre-trained VAE. However, bypassing the VAE
bottleneck alone does not automatically resolve the persistent fidelity–realism trade-off. In conven-
tional flow matching, predicting the standard velocity target v := ϵ −x0 couples full-dimensional
noise with clean-image reconstruction, requiring a single network to simultaneously perform high-
level semantic generation and low-level texture recovery (Chen et al., 2026a). This task entangle-
ment is particularly problematic for Real-SR: the former role relies on generative priors to synthesize
realistic content, whereas the latter demands strict pixel-level alignment with the LR observation. To
solve this challenge, several methods attempt to ease this optimization burden through architectural
bypasses or direct clean-image prediction (Hoogeboom et al., 2024; Li & He, 2026). Nevertheless,
they either complicate the transformer recipe, suffer from numerical ill-conditioning, or fail to ex-
plicitly partition these conflicting roles within the denoising flow. Therefore, the key challenge of
pixel-space Real-SR lies in how to orthogonally decouple these two roles within a unified, condi-
tional framework, thereby reconciling the tension between perceptual realism and structural fidelity.
Based on the above analysis, we propose PixelSR, a novel single-stage pixel-space diffusion frame-
work for Real-SR that aims to reconcile structural fidelity and perceptual realism, as displayed in
Figure 2. Specifically, our PixelSR is built upon Pixel-space Conditional Diffusion (PCD), which
integrates spatially aligned LR conditioning, visual semantic guidance, and lightweight texture re-
finement to perform end-to-end restoration directly in RGB space, thereby eliminating the need for
lossy VAE encoding and decoding. To alleviate task entanglement in conventional flow matching,
we further develop Orthogonal Flow Decoupling (OFD) by decomposing the asymmetric velocity
uA := P ϵ −x0 into a low-rank semantic component P uA and an orthogonal texture component
(I −P )uA, enabling specialized optimization with reduced interference.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We present PixelSR, a single-stage end-to-end Pixel Diffusion Transformer for Real-SR.
Its PCD framework adapts the original diffusion backbone to operate directly in pixel space,
bypassing the lossy VAE bottleneck.
• We propose OFD to mitigate task entanglement in pixel-space flow matching. By decom-
posing asymmetric velocity target into a low-rank semantic and an orthogonal texture com-
ponent, OFD assigns them to specialized modules to minimize optimization interference.

• Extensive experiments across multiple Real-SR benchmarks demonstrate that PixelSR de-
livers consistently strong performance across both fidelity- and perception-oriented metrics,
effectively mitigating the fidelity–realism seesaw effect.

RELATED WORK
2.1
REAL-WORLD IMAGE SUPER-RESOLUTION
The methodology of Real-SR has evolved from fidelity-oriented reconstruction to perceptual detail
generation. Early deep-learning approaches, including convolutional neural networks and vision
Transformers (Dong et al., 2015; Liang et al., 2021), primarily optimize pixel-wise reconstruction
objectives (e.g., L1 or L2 losses). Although these methods preserve structural integrity and achieve
favorable distortion metrics, they inherently suffer from over-smoothing due to the regression-to-
the-mean problem. To improve perceptual quality, generative adversarial networks (Wang et al.,
2021; Zhang et al., 2021) are introduced by utilizing adversarial and perceptual losses to recover
high-frequency textures under complex degradations. However, despite producing sharper details,
they frequently produce unnatural visual artifacts.
More recently, diffusion models have emerged as a powerful alternative for realistic detail genera-
tion. While early approaches train conditional diffusion models specifically for SR (Saharia et al.,
2023; Yue et al., 2023), recent methods increasingly adapt large-scale pre-trained text-to-image pri-
ors (Wang et al., 2024a; Duan et al., 2025). Despite their impressive perceptual quality, most of
the latter operate in a VAE-compressed latent space, potentially losing fine-grained structures and
separating latent prediction from final RGB reconstruction. To improve deployment efficiency, re-
cent studies attempt to bypass or streamline the VAE stage via distillation (Wang et al., 2026; Chen
et al., 2025). However, as post-hoc compression, they still fail to achieve genuine, pixel-space joint
optimization. In contrast, our PixelSR establishes pixel-space flow modeling, seamlessly integrating
restoration and reconstruction via explicit semantic–texture decoupling.
2.2
PIXEL-SPACE DIFFUSION MODELS
Early diffusion models operate directly in pixel space, typically using U-Net architectures with
multi-scale features and skip connections to propagate high-dimensional signals (Ho et al., 2020).
Subsequently, LDMs shift the diffusion process to VAE-compressed latent representations, substan-
tially reducing computational and memory costs and thereby enabling more scalable training and
generation (Rombach et al., 2022; Lipman et al., 2023).
Lately, however, the paradigm is shifting back toward pixel-space modeling to construct self-
contained, tokenizer-free generative frameworks. Specifically, these studies mainly address the
high-dimensional prediction burden through architectural design or prediction-objective reformu-
lation. For example, PixelDiT (Yu et al., 2026) and DiP (Chen et al., 2026c) augment Transformer
backbones with pixel-level or U-Net-style decoders to facilitate fine-grained reconstruction. Alterna-
tively, JiT (Li & He, 2026) adopts clean-image prediction (x0-prediction) under the low-dimensional
manifold assumption, while AsymFlow (Chen et al., 2026a) restricts noise modeling to a low-rank
subspace via an asymmetric velocity target uA. Nevertheless, the monolithic targets of these meth-
ods still couple semantic generation with texture reconstruction in the same prediction space. Unlike
these methods, our OFD explicitly factorizes prediction objectives into a low-rank semantic compo-
nent and a noise-free orthogonal texture component for specialized optimization.

3.2
PIXEL-SPACE CONDITIONAL DIFFUSION
As the restoration backbone of PixelSR, PCD establishes a single-stage Pixel Diffusion Transformer
for Real-SR. It adopts an augmented DiT backbone (Yao et al., 2025) and directly instantiates the
conditional flow through x0-prediction in RGB space. To adapt the original generative backbone
to Real-SR, we incorporate three complementary designs: spatially aligned LR conditioning, visual
semantic guidance, and lightweight pixel-level texture refinement, as shown in Figure 2.
Spatially Aligned LR Conditioning.
Given an LR observation y, we first resize it to the target
resolution determined by the upscaling factor, obtaining ey ∈RB×C×H×W . Then, we construct the
noisy state xt ∈RB×C×H×W on the same spatial grid and concatenate them channel-wise:
ht = Concat (xt, ey) ∈RB×2C×H×W .
(8)
This pixel-aligned conditioning provides each noisy location with its corresponding LR evidence,
imposing dense structural constraints throughout restoration. Subsequently, the resulting input is
divided into non-overlapping p × p patches and embedded into DiT tokens:
f0 = PatchEmbedp(ht) ∈RB×L×DDiT,
(9)
where DDiT denotes the hidden dimension of the DiT backbone and L = (H/p)(W/p) is the
number of patch tokens.
Visual Semantic Guidance.
Spatially aligned LR pixels may provide insufficient semantic evi-
dence under severe degradation. We therefore use a frozen pre-trained vision encoder to extract
high-level representations:
z = Πvis (Evis(y)) ,
(10)
where Πvis projects the visual features into the DiT conditioning space. We further inject z into
each DiT block through cross-attention:
fn+1 = DiTn (fn, t, z) ,
n = 0, . . . , N −1.
(11)
Accordingly, the concatenated LR input anchors spatial structures, while the visual embeddings
provide complementary semantic guidance for severely degraded content.
Lightweight Texture Refinement.
To complement patch-level semantics with fine-grained tex-
tures, we introduce a lightweight Pixel Decoder composed of PiT blocks (Yu et al., 2026). It oper-
ates on pixel-level tokens under the global guidance of the DiT backbone. Specifically, each p × p
region of ht is represented as p2 pixel tokens:
q0 = PixelEmbedp(ht) ∈R(BL)×p2×DPiT,
(12)
where DPiT denotes the hidden dimension of the lightweight Pixel Decoder. After that, the final
DiT features fN are expanded into patch-wise conditions cpat and injected into the corresponding
PiT blocks through AdaLN:
qm+1 = PiTm (qm, cpat) ,
m = 0, . . . , M −1.
(13)
This design further refines local textures under the global structural and semantic context provided
by the DiT backbone.
Overall, the basic PCD predicts the complete clean image as bx0 = PixelUnpatchify(Φpix(qM))
and optimizes it via Eqs. 3 and 5, establishing an effective framework for direct pixel-space restora-
tion. However, its monolithic x0 target still couples semantic generation with texture reconstruction
in the same prediction space. To this end, we next introduce OFD to explicitly decouple these
heterogeneous objectives.
3.3
ORTHOGONAL FLOW DECOUPLING
As illustrated on the right of Figure 2, our OFD builds upon the asymmetric velocity target but
goes beyond its monolithic prediction by explicitly factorizing and assigning its complementary
components to task-specialized branches.

Orthogonal Target Factorization.
Unlike AsymFlow, which predicts uA as a unified target, OFD
explicitly exposes its complementary projections. Using the projector properties in Eq. 6, we obtain:
P uA = P v,
(I −P )uA = −(I −P )x0.

whereas its orthogonal complement (I −P )uA is completely noise-free, reducing the objective in
this subspace to a deterministic reconstruction of the clean-image residual. Moreover, these two

Task-Aligned Branch Assignment.
Motivated by this physical correspondence, OFD assigns
each decoupled objective to the network branch best suited to its characteristics. Specifically, the
global-focused DiT backbone is tasked with semantic prediction busem, while the local-focused Pixel
Decoder targets texture prediction butex, yielding:
bu∥= P busem,
bu⊥= (I −P )butex.
(15)
Accordingly, this task-aligned separation successfully isolates their prediction spaces, mitigating
optimization interference while allowing the Pixel Decoder to leverage noise-free supervision for
high-fidelity texture reconstruction.

branch predictions are converted into full-rank velocity. From xt = x0 + tv and Eq. 14, we derive:

Implementation Details.
PixelSR is optimized end-to-end in pixel space. The patch-level back-
bone consists of N = 28 DiT blocks with hidden dimension DDiT = 1024, while the pixel-level
decoder comprises M = 4 PiT blocks with hidden dimension DPiT = 32. We use a frozen pre-
trained DINOv2-B/14 (Oquab et al., 2023) as the visual semantic encoder Evis. Following the rank
analysis in AsymFlow (Chen et al., 2026a), we fix r = 16 across all experiments. This choice also
matches the 2 × 2 × 4 = 16 latent coefficients corresponding to each 16 × 16 RGB patch under
an f8c4 VAE, facilitating dimensionally aligned Procrustes fitting. Please see Appendix A.1 for
further implementation details.
4.2
COMPARISON WITH STATE-OF-THE-ART
Compared Methods.
We compare PixelSR with recent diffusion-based Real-SR methods, in-
cluding DiffBIR (Lin et al., 2024), StableSR (Wang et al., 2024a), OSEDiff (Wu et al., 2024a),
SinSR (Wang et al., 2024b), DiT4SR (Duan et al., 2025), DiT-SR (Cheng et al., 2025),
CODSR (Chen et al., 2026b), and VOSR (Wu et al., 2026a). All competing methods are evaluated
using their officially released checkpoints and default inference settings. Notably, these methods
operate in compressed latent spaces, whereas PixelSR performs restoration directly in RGB space.
Quantitative Comparisons.
Table 1 reports the quantitative results on four benchmarks. Existing
methods exhibit different preferences along the fidelity–realism spectrum. For example, SinSR
and DiT-SR achieve competitive PSNR and SSIM on several benchmarks but are less favorable on
perceptual-quality metrics. Conversely, DiffBIR, CODSR, and VOSR attain strong no-reference
scores in certain cases, often accompanied by degraded reference-based fidelity. PixelSR achieves
the best overall rank across the four benchmarks while maintaining competitive performance on both
metric groups. In particular, it obtains substantial fidelity improvements on the challenging UGCSR
benchmark without sacrificing perceptual quality. Together with the trade-off analysis in Figure 1,
these results demonstrate that our PixelSR effectively alleviates the fidelity–realism seesaw effect.
Qualitative Comparisons.
Figure 4 presents qualitative comparisons on challenging real-world
cases. In the first example, competing methods distort character strokes or hallucinate incorrect
layouts, whereas PixelSR preserves text structures consistent with the HR reference. In the second
example, competing methods tend to produce over-smoothed or irregular feather patterns. PixelSR
reconstructs finer and more natural textures without altering the bird’s appearance. These visual
results further corroborate the quantitative evidence that our single-stage, end-to-end PixelSR better
reconciles structural fidelity and perceptual realism.
Efficiency Comparisons.
We evaluate all methods on a single NVIDIA H800 GPU using their
official sampling settings. As reported in Table 1, PixelSR requires approximately 4.5 seconds per
image, with runtime comparable to several multi-step latent diffusion baselines. This work focuses
on restoration quality and the fidelity–perception trade-off, while integrating one-step distillation for
further acceleration is left for future exploration.

CONCLUSION
In this paper, we rethink the fidelity–realism trade-off in Real-SR by challenging the prevailing latent
diffusion paradigm. To bypass lossy VAE compression and cascading errors, we present PixelSR,
a single-stage framework operating directly in RGB space. Crucially, to resolve task entanglement
in pixel-space flow matching, we introduce Orthogonal Flow Decoupling, which mathematically
decomposes asymmetric velocity prediction into semantic generation and deterministic texture re-
covery assigned to specialized sub-networks. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our PixelSR
substantially alleviates the fidelity–realism seesaw effect and establishes a more favorable Pareto
frontier. Beyond super-resolution, our work validates the viability of pixel diffusion without VAE
encoding and decoding, providing a principled baseline for harmonizing generative priors with strict
structural fidelity.

LOW-RANK PROJECTOR CONSTRUCTION
OFD relies on a rank-r orthogonal projector P ∈RD×D to partition each D-dimensional pixel patch
into two complementary subspaces. We parameterize P using an orthonormal basis A ∈RD×r
satisfying:
ATA = Ir.
(19)
The corresponding projector is defined as:
P := AAT.
(20)
It satisfies P T = P , P 2 = P , and rank(P ) = r. Moreover, the orthonormality constraint preserves
the Gaussian distribution of projected pixel-space noise. Specifically, if ϵ ∼N(0, ID), then:
ATϵ ∼N(0, Ir).
(21)
We investigate three strategies for constructing the projector.
Learnable Projector P .
The first strategy constructs P from a learnable orthonormal basis A,
which is jointly optimized with PixelSR under the following constraint:
ATA = Ir.
(22)
The resulting projector is:
PLearn := AAT.
(23)
This formulation allows the projected subspace to adapt directly to the restoration objective. How-
ever, the projector continuously changes during training and does not explicitly incorporate prior
knowledge about the representation structure of natural images.

The second strategy constructs a data-dependent projector from the
principal directions of pixel patches. Specifically, we collect K normalized and vectorized RGB
patches and stack them as:
X = [x1, . . . , xK]T ∈RK×D.
(24)
For a patch size of p, the pixel dimension is:
D = 3p2.
(25)

Ablation on Projection Strategies.
Since the functional roles of the induced subspaces depend
on the projection basis, we compare three rank-matched constructions of P under the same PCD
architecture and training settings: a learnable orthonormal basis optimized with the restoration ob-
jective, an SVD basis derived from dominant pixel-patch variations, and a Procrustes basis aligned
with corresponding pre-trained VAE tokens. As shown in Table 5, the Procrustes projector achieves
the best results across all metrics, suggesting that the compact organization of VAE representations
provides a useful prior for defining the low-rank subspace.
Furthermore, to examine whether restoration performance depends on the source VAE’s reconstruc-
tion quality, we compare alignment using SD2.1, SD3, and FLUX.2 VAEs, adapting the projection-
layer input dimensions to their respective latent representations. As shown in Table 5, the three
variants achieve broadly comparable SR performance, with no source VAE consistently outperform-
ing the others across all metrics. These results suggest that the benefit of Procrustes alignment is not
specific to a particular VAE. This is consistent with the VAE’s limited role as an offline reference
for constructing P : PixelSR models both P and I −P components directly in RGB space, rather
than passing images through a VAE encoding–decoding bottleneck during restoration training or
inference.

## ABSTRACT 

One-step generative models enable fast text-guided image editing, yet they often introduce unintended changes to regions that should remain unchanged. Temporal aggregation stabilizes editing fields across noise levels, but leaves their spatial allocation insensitive to local variations in reliability. Consequently, temporal stability alone does not ensure spatially consistent editing. To address this lim-itation, we propose ER-Chord, a training-free and inversion-free framework for reliability-guided spatial redistribution. Our key insight is that agreement between editing fields at neighboring noise levels provides a local reliability signal for allocating editing strength. We formulate this allocation as an equal-energy op-timization that redistributes control energy according to local reliability while preserving the original editing directions and total control energy. This formu-lation admits a unique global solution that can be efficiently computed using existing field evaluations, requiring no additional denoiser calls. Experiments on two benchmarks, PIE-Bench and TEdBench, demonstrate improvements over state-of-the-art one-step editing baselines, achieving superior source preservation alongside consistent gains across multiple generative backbones.The project page is at https://github.com/Peizeli1/ER-Chord .

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Distilled text-to-image models, such as SD-Turbo, InstaFlow, and SwiftBrush-v2 (Sauer et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024; Dao et al., 2024), compress diffusion sampling into a single neural function evaluation. Although this acceleration enables real-time image synthesis, editing real images remains difficult because it requires introducing requested semantic changes while preserving the identity, structure, and unedited regions of the source image. Existing accelerated editors address this trade-off through one-step inversion (Nguyen et al., 2025), few-step reconstruction (Deutch et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2025), or inversion-free transport (Kulikov et al., 2025; Lu et al., 2026). Among these approaches, training-free and inversion-free transport is especially attractive because it eliminates both auxiliary model training and iterative latent inversion. However, executing an edit through a single transport update imposes strict requirements on the spatial organization of the resulting control field. ChordEdit (Lu et al., 2026) stabilizes one-step editing by aggregating source-to-target proxy fields from nearby noise levels. This formulation primarily addresses temporal variations in the estimated transport field. Because this aggregation rule is applied uniformly across the latent grid, the magnitude at each spatial location is merely a passive result of combining the endpoint fields. However, the two endpoint estimates do not agree equally across all locations. Some regions exhibit consistent local control vectors across noise levels, whereas others show substantial disagreement. The framework requires no auxiliary guidance, training, inversion, additional denoiser evaluations, or backpropagation. aggregation ignores this spatial disparity and distributes editing strength without considering local reliability. Consequently, it often introduces large updates into unedited backgrounds, causing visual artifacts. Therefore, temporal stability does not ensure spatial consistency, leaving spatial magnitude allocation as a crucial yet unaddressed degree of freedom. This observation motivates a distinction between where the estimated control is reliable and where a semantic edit should occur . Existing localized editing methods typically use cross-attention maps or internal feature representations to identify edit regions (Couairon et al., 2023; Simsar et al., 2025; Soni et al., 2025; Nguyen et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2026). In contrast to these localization approaches, we retain the temporally aggregated editing directions and use endpoint agreement solely to determine their relative spatial strengths. Thus, endpoint consistency serves as an intrinsic reliability signal for the control field rather than a spatial localization map. Based on this principle, we propose ER-Chord, a training-free and inversion-free framework for reliability-guided spatial redistribution. ER-Chord preserves the local directions of the Chord field and maintains the total control energy, modifying only how this fixed budget is distributed across spatial locations. We measure local reliability through the agreement between the two Chord endpoint fields and formulate the reallocation as a reliability-weighted quadratic optimization problem subject to an exact equal-energy constraint. This constraint prevents trivial source preservation caused by simply reducing the global editing strength. Although this formulation couples all spatial locations through a shared energy budget, it yields a unique global solution that rescales each local Chord vector by a positive scalar. The entire solution depends on a single scalar variable that can be found efficiently through one-dimensional bisection. ER-Chord therefore requires no additional denoiser evaluations, auxiliary training, or backpropagation. The equal-energy formulation also provides a direct interpretation of the resulting redistribution. Locations with higher endpoint consistency receive larger scaling factors relative to their original Chord vectors, while a shared scalar coordinates these adjustments to preserve the total control energy exactly. Hence, ER-Chord modifies only the relative spatial allocation of energy without altering local control directions or the overall update scale (Figure 1). As shown in Figures 2 and 3, under identical total control energy, ChordEdit disperses editing strength into irrelevant backgrounds, causing color bleeding and artifacts. In contrast, ER-Chord concentrates energy into semantically consistent regions, producing clean target edits while faithfully preserving source structures. Empirically, ER-Chord consistently outperforms one-step editing baselines across diverse bench-marks using the same denoiser budget. Without tuning hyperparameters for specific datasets or backbones, our configuration substantially improves background preservation on PIE-Bench, general-izes effectively to other benchmarks such as TEdBench in perceptual quality and source fidelity, and transfers across multiple generative backbones (InstaFlow, SwiftBrush-v2, and SD-Turbo). ## 2 RELATED WORK 

Real-image editing and inversion. Text-guided editing must introduce the requested semantics without losing the identity and layout of the source image. SDEdit starts reverse diffusion from a partially noised source, while DiffEdit estimates an edit mask from differences between conditional 3predictions (Meng et al., 2022; Couairon et al., 2023). Prompt-to-Prompt controls cross-attention, and MasaCtrl and PnP reuse self-attention or intermediate features from a source branch to preserve struc-ture (Hertz et al., 2023; Cao et al., 2023; Tumanyan et al., 2023). Inversion-based methods instead seek a latent trajectory that accurately reconstructs the source. Null-text Inversion optimizes unconditional text embeddings, EDICT introduces coupled transformations for exact inversion, and subsequent methods reduce the mismatch accumulated along inversion and denoising trajectories (Mokady et al., 2023; Wallace et al., 2023; Ju et al., 2024; Huberman-Spiegelglas et al., 2024; Yang & Ye, 2026). A related line of work separates inversion and invariance within flow transformers to enable versatile editing (Xu et al., 2025). These approaches improve fidelity by modifying the inversion path, internal features, or spatial masks. ER-Chord instead operates after a one-step source-to-target control field has been formed and changes only its local magnitudes, without reconstructing an inversion trajectory or modifying network attention and features. 

Few-step and one-step editing. Consistency-based and flow-based models have made high-quality synthesis possible with one or a few neural function evaluations (Song & Dhariwal, 2024; Luo et al., 2023; Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023). This development has motivated editors designed specifically for low-step samplers. TurboEdit and InstantEdit pair specialized inversion with few-step reconstruction, whereas SwiftEdit learns a one-step inversion model and uses mask-guided atten-tion rescaling for localized editing (Deutch et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2025; Nguyen et al., 2025). BiFM jointly learns forward and inverse flows, FlowEdit constructs an inversion-free transport from pretrained flow models, and EEdit accelerates inversion-based editors by exploiting redundant computation across space and time (Dai et al., 2026; Kulikov et al., 2025; Yan et al., 2025). Re-lated acceleration efforts span frequency-energy constrained routing for diffusion adaptation (Yin et al., 2025) and future-guided frame selection for long-horizon video generation (Yin et al., 2026). ChordEdit is the closest starting point for our work. It is training-free and inversion-free, and stabi-lizes a one-step update by aggregating source-to-target fields across nearby noise levels (Lu et al., 2026). A subsequent analysis interprets its chord window largely as an effective timestep shift and separates its procedure into coarse transport and fine alignment (Li et al., 2026). ER-Chord leaves this temporal estimator and cleanup unchanged, introduces no additional model evaluations, and only redistributes the resulting field magnitude over space. forest city

Spatial localization and magnitude alloca-tion. Spati-ally precise editors generally iden-tify where an edit belongs and then restrict generation in that region. DiffEdit derives a mask from conditional prediction differences, while Learnable Regions and ZONE obtain regions through learned boxes or segmenta-tion (Couairon et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024). FoI, LIME, and LOCATE-dit extract or refine attention-based regions and use them to constrain the denoising pro-cess (Guo & Lin, 2024; Simsar et al., 2025; Soni et al., 2025). ALE-Edit further limits at-tribute leakage through object-restricted text embeddings, region-guided attention masking, and background blending (Mun et al., 2025). Localization has also been incorporated into one-step editing. SwiftEdit uses mask-guided attention rescaling, while WhereEdit discov-ers editable regions from internal features and applies local modulation (Nguyen et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2026). These methods use semantic or structural evidence to decide where a modification should occur. ER-Chord neither predicts an editable region nor changes the semantic direction of the edit. It uses endpoint consistency only to rescale the magnitudes of fixed local Chord directions under an unchanged per-image energy budget. Localization methods determine where an edit should act, whereas ER-Chord adjusts the strength of the existing Chord field across locations. 43 METHOD 

We develop ER-Chord in three steps. We first revisit the temporally aggregated Chord field and identify spatial magnitude allocation as a degree of freedom that is not explicitly controlled by the temporal estimator. We then use local endpoint consistency as a reliability signal and formulate spatial reweighting as an equal-energy redistribution problem, which preserves both the original control directions and the total control strength. Finally, we show that this seemingly coupled optimization admits a simple direction-preserving solution determined by a single shared scalar, enabling an efficient implementation with negligible additional computation. 3.1 FROM TEMPORAL AGGREGATION TO SPATIAL ALLOCATION 

Let Isrc denote the source image, and let csrc and ctar denote the source and target conditions, respectively. Given a latent state xτ , we follow ChordEdit Lu et al. (2026) and denote by R(xτ , t )

the source-to-target proxy field constructed at noise time t. In particular, for the two neighboring endpoints t − δ and t, we write Rt−δ := R(xτ , t − δ) and Rt := R(xτ , t ).ChordEdit temporally aggregates these two endpoint fields to obtain a stabilized control field. At each spatial location p ∈ Ω, its control vector can be written as 

uC(p) = tt + δ Rt−δ (p) + δt + δ Rt(p). (1) The coefficients in (1) are global temporal scalars and do not depend on the spatial location p. The corresponding derivation from the Chord surrogate is provided in Appendix A.1. Equation (1) provides a temporally stabilized editing vector at every spatial location. However, the resulting spatial magnitude ∥uC(p)∥2 is only an implicit consequence of the temporal aggregation. In particular, the aggregation rule does not explicitly compare the magnitude assigned to location p with the agreement between Rt−δ (p) and Rt(p). Therefore, two locations with substantially different endpoint consistency are still governed by the same temporal mixing rule. This observation suggests separating temporal stabilization from spatial magnitude allocation. We retain the local directions supplied by uC and treat their spatial magnitudes as the remaining degree of freedom. Our goal is to redistribute these magnitudes according to local endpoint consistency while preserving the total control strength of the original Chord field. Section 3.2 develops this reliability-guided equal-energy redistribution. 3.2 RELIABILITY -G UIDED EQUAL -E NERGY REDISTRIBUTION

As shown in Table 1, the primary benefit of ER-Chord lies in substantial gains in source content preservation. Specifically, ER-Chord improves unedited-region PSNR from 22 .20 to 22 .77 dB ( +0 .57 dB), lowers MSE from 6.84 to 6.35 , and reduces perceptual distortion LPIPS from 128 .25 to 120 .23 (10 3× scaling). Detailed structural preservation metrics (e.g., SSIM and Structure Distance) exhibit concordant improvements in the appendix (Table 6). Crucially, these preservation gains are accompanied by improved target semantic alignment: CLIP-Whole rises from 25 .58 to 25 .74 , and CLIP-Edited from 22 .96 to 23 .16 . The two methods have the same total pre-cleanup control energy by construction. Thus, the gains cannot be attributed to uniformly reducing the control field; they are consistent with a more effective spatial allocation.

## 5 CONCLUSION 

This paper investigates spatial magnitude allocation in training-free, one-step image editing. Existing transport methods stabilize updates across noise levels using temporal weights shared across spatial 9locations, leaving local reliability unaccounted for in the allocation of editing strength. We propose ER-Chord, which uses endpoint agreement to redistribute control energy under an exact pre-cleanup energy constraint. The resulting field preserves the original local directions and has a unique global solution determined by a scalar root, with no additional denoiser evaluations relative to ChordEdit. Experiments on PIE-Bench, TEdBench, and three generative backbones (SD-Turbo, SwiftBrush-v2, and InstaFlow) show improved source preservation alongside target alignment in the tested settings. These results suggest that spatial allocation is a useful complement to temporal stabilization. The equal-energy constraint prevents a trivial improvement from globally weakening the edit: energy reduced in one location must be reallocated to another. The endpoint-consistency signal determines that reallocation, while a shared scalar root keeps the computation small. Because the method rescales the Chord field without changing its local directions, it cannot correct an erroneous direction supplied by the underlying field. Testing how this dependence changes across additional editing tasks and backbones is a useful direction for future work. AI U SE STATEMENT

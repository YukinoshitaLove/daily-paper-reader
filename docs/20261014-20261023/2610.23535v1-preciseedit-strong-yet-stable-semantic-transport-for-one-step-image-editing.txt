Abstract
Recent one-step text-to-image (T2I) models enable efficient image
synthesis, opening up new possibilities for interactive Web-based
image editing. However, existing one-step editing methods often
construct a global transport field through simple vector operations
between conditional predictions. While such strong transport can
effectively drive the image toward the target semantics, it may be
released along unreliable directions or over non-edited regions,
leading to structural distortions within the editing region and back-
ground drift elsewhere. More importantly, stabilizing the editing
process through global smoothing or uniformly weakening the
transport field often makes large semantic changes overly conser-
vative. In this work, we revisit one-step image editing from the
perspective of the tension between semantic strength and transport
stability, and identify a key insight: strong semantic transport is
not inherently unstable. What matters is whether it is applied along
reliable directions and at reliable spatial locations. Motivated by this
insight, we introduce PreciseEdit, a model-agnostic, training-free
and inversion-free one-step editing framework that strengthens
target-semantic alignment while preserving background fidelity
and structural consistency. We further propose a dual-noise-scale
role decomposition strategy: a higher-noise scale produces the
strong target-directed transport used for actual editing, whereas
a lower-noise scale jointly serves as a probe for directional agree-
ment and spatial structure. Extensive experiments on PIE-Bench
demonstrate that PreciseEdit achieves state-of-the-art performance
and effectiveness on various editing tasks.

However, efficient one-step editing that enables strong seman-
tic transformations while preserving high fidelity remains chal-
lenging. Some fast editors rely on trained inversion networks or
explicit inversion [9, 23], while training-free and inversion-free
approaches construct editing directions directly from conditional
predictions [16, 36]. Yet, compressing semantic transformation into
a single update creates a pronounced strength–stability dilemma.
ChordEdit [18] stabilizes editing through temporal smoothing and
a low-energy field but can under-realize large semantic changes,
whereas the concurrent work WhereEdit [13] enables stronger
spatially constrained editing but still relies on relatively coarse
localization and an additional U-Net evaluation.
Despite their different formulations, these approaches expose
two prominent drawbacks. First, globally weakening transport im-
proves preservation but limits semantic transformations. Second,
stronger transport improves target-semantic alignment but can
cause background drift or structural distortion when applied un-
reliably. These issues naturally lead us to reconsider the current

paradigm and pose a critical question: Can strong semantic trans-
port remain stable in one-step image editing?
To address this question, we begin by analyzing different seman-
tic transport regimes and the factors governing their stability. Fig. 1
reveals that, with the official GT masks from PIE-Bench [14], both
ChordEdit and WhereEdit substantially improve non-edited-region

Meanwhile, ChordEdit remains conservative even with accurate
spatial guidance, whereas the stronger transport of WhereEdit
achieves larger semantic changes but can still affect non-target
regions.
These observations suggest that strong semantic transport is
not inherently unstable; reliable spatial localization is critical to
its stability. Moreover, because a one-step update offers no later
opportunity to correct misdirected transport, directional reliability
is equally important.
This insight naturally motivates a selective-release editing strat-
egy: construct a strong transport field directed toward the
target semantics, but release it only at reliable spatial loca-
tions and along reliable directions. Guided by this principle, we
propose PreciseEdit, a training-free and inversion-free framework
that explicitly regulates strong semantic transport through spatial
and directional reliability.
Implementing this idea, however, raises two critical challenges:
(1) How to precisely and efficiently identify where to edit?
and (2) How to keep strong semantic transport stable?
For Challenge 1, we propose PreciseMask, a structure-aware
spatial gating mechanism that combines lower-noise cross-attention
semantics with self-attention structural affinity to identify reliable
edit regions. PreciseMask reuses attention from the main U-Net
evaluation, requiring neither external segmentation nor additional
U-Net evaluations.
For Challenge 2, we introduce Strong Conditional Transport
(SCT), which augments source-to-target conditional contrast with
anchor-relative target attraction to provide sufficient semantic mod-
ification. Cross-scale directional agreement suppresses transport
directions inconsistent with a lower-noise reference, while Precise-
Mask provides complementary spatial regulation. Together, they
enable strong transport to remain stable within a single-step update.
To effectively integrate these two modules, we propose a dual-
noise-scale role decomposition strategy.
The higher-noise scale provides strong semantic transport, while
the lower-noise scale supplies spatial evidence and directional ref-
erences. This complementary strategy couples semantic strength
with transport stability within a unified one-step framework.
The main contributions can be summarized as follows:
(1) We propose PreciseMask, fusing lower-noise cross- and
self-attention for precise, structure-aware edit localization
without an external segmenter or extra U-Net evaluations.
(2) We introduce Strong Conditional Transport (SCT) to
strengthen target-directed transport, with cross-scale di-
rectional agreement suppressing unreliable directions.
(3) We propose a dual-noise-scale strategy that leverages the
complementary properties of the two noise scales to decou-
ple transport-field construction from spatial localization,
yielding leading PIE-Bench results across backbones.

Related Work
2.1
Text-Guided Editing with Diffusion/Flow
Models
Diffusion and latent-diffusion models established a strong founda-
tion for text-guided visual generation and editing [11, 12, 20, 26].
Early real-image editors commonly rely on inversion, attention con-
trol, guidance, or latent optimization [6, 10, 15, 21, 34, 35]. Methods
such as PnP [34] and ProxEdit [10] achieve high-quality editing but
require iterative inversion and multi-step sampling. Recent distilled
and consistency-based generators [8, 22, 24, 30, 37, 38], including
SD-Turbo [27], InstaFlow [17], and SwiftBrush-v2 [4], enable one-
or few-step generation, but their accelerated dynamics make precise
editing more challenging.
2.2
Training-Free and Inversion-Free Editing
To improve efficiency, recent studies avoid per-image inversion and
additional model training [16, 18, 36]. FlowEdit [16] and InfEdit [36]
provide inversion-free editing but still rely on iterative or few-step
updates. Fast editors such as TurboEdit [5], invertible consistency
distillation [32], and SwiftEdit [23] reduce the sampling cost, yet
compressing semantic transformation into very few updates makes
fidelity and edit strength harder to balance. ChordEdit [18] stabi-
lizes one-step transport through a low-energy control field, but the
resulting conservative transport can limit large semantic changes.
2.3
Mask-Guided Controlled Image Editing
Spatial controllability is central to precise editing. Prior work lo-
calizes modifications with explicit masks [1–3] or internal atten-
tion/features [11, 19, 31, 33]. LIME [28] combines intermediate
2


features and cross-attention, LayerEdit [7] decomposes objects, and
WhereEdit [13] aggregates cross-attention to estimate editable re-
gions. These approaches may require inversion, iterative sampling,
external segmentation, or extra localization/optimization, limiting
one-step efficiency. PreciseEdit instead reuses lower-noise cross-
and self-attention with graph refinement, requiring no external
segmenter, learnable parameters, or additional U-Net evaluation
for mask generation.
3
Method
We propose PreciseEdit, a training-free and inversion-free frame-
work for one-step image editing. PreciseEdit separates semantic
strength from stability: the higher-noise scale provides the pri-
mary SCT, while the lower-noise scale supplies both a directional
reference and attention-derived spatial support. The final update
combines the primary SCT, cross-scale directional modulation, and
continuous PreciseMask gating in latent space.

3.2.1
Anchor-Relative Target Attraction. Multi-step editing can cor-
rect early errors through iterative feedback, whereas one-step edit-
ing must realize sufficiently large semantic changes in a single up-
date. We therefore seek a transport that is explicitly target-directed

the target-conditioned prediction.

3.3
PreciseMask
Directional agreement determines whether the primary SCT is
trustworthy, but not where it should act. We therefore use edit sup-
port to quantify spatial evidence for applying the requested latent
update. PreciseMask is constructed entirely at the lower-noise scale
𝑡𝑙by reusing cross- and self-attention from the source- and target-
conditioned branches. As illustrated in Fig. 4, branch-specific cross-
attention produces semantic-support seeds, confidence-weighted
self-attention graph-Laplacian refinement propagates them along
coherent visual structures, and adaptive soft gating converts the
refined support into PreciseMask.

timestep 𝑡𝑟, yielding 𝑥𝑝𝑟𝑜𝑥. The final edited latent is composited as:
𝑥𝑜𝑢𝑡= (1 −𝑀) ⊙𝑥+ 𝑀⊙𝑥𝑝𝑟𝑜𝑥.
(23)
Define the target-conditioned refinement residual as𝑟𝑝𝑟𝑜𝑥= 𝑥𝑝𝑟𝑜𝑥−
e𝑥. Expanding Eq. (23) gives:
𝑥𝑜𝑢𝑡= 𝑥+ 𝜂𝛾𝑀2 ⊙𝑢𝑆𝐶𝑇
ℎ
+ 𝑀⊙𝑟𝑝𝑟𝑜𝑥.
(24)
Here 𝑀2 = 𝑀⊙𝑀denotes element-wise squaring. The primary
SCT is therefore attenuated quadratically by the soft gate, whereas
the refinement residual is weighted linearly by 𝑀. Strong transport
is preserved where 𝑀≈1 and decays faster near uncertain bound-
aries, realizing selective release according to directional and spatial
reliability. The final image is decoded by the frozen VAE decoder
as 𝐼𝑜𝑢𝑡= D(𝑥𝑜𝑢𝑡).

4.2
Comparison with Prior Methods
Quantitative Results. Table 1 compares preservation, semantic
alignment, and efficiency under the unified PIE-Bench protocol.
PreciseEdit reaches 24.37 dB PSNR-Unedit and 23.36 CLIP-Edited,
improving over ChordEdit [18] by 1.73 dB and 1.20, respectively,
and over WhereEdit [13] by 0.41 dB and 0.70.
PreciseMask recovers 9.81 dB PSNR-Unedit while retaining 23.36
CLIP-Edited, directly supporting selective release.
The GT-guided variant further reaches 27.34 PSNR-Unedit and
23.90 CLIP-Edited, indicating that more accurate spatial support
remains meaningful headroom.
Qualitative Results. Figure 5 shows the same trend across
diverse edit types. Existing methods often either under-realize
the target concept or alter unrelated appearance and geometry.
This behavior appears in both global edits, where background
changes can leak into foreground structure, and localized object
or attribute edits, where excessive transport can disturb shape
or identity. PreciseEdit more consistently changes the requested
concept while retaining source composition, object structure, and
non-edited appearance.
Taken together, these results suggest that the key advantage
is not maximizing preservation or edit strength in isolation, but
retaining strong target-directed transport while controlling its di-
rectional and spatial reliability. We next examine these factors
separately.

Conclusion
In this work, we revisited one-step image editing through the ten-
sion between semantic strength and transport reliability. Instead
of globally suppressing strong transport, we proposed PreciseEdit,
a training- and inversion-free framework that selectively trusts it
according to directional and spatial reliability. Our dual-noise-scale
design uses a higher-noise scale to construct Strong Conditional
Transport, while a lower-noise scale provides directional calibration
and structured spatial support through PreciseMask. By combin-
ing cross-attention semantic seeds, self-attention graph-Laplacian
refinement, and adaptive soft gating, PreciseEdit localizes strong
semantic changes while preserving irrelevant regions. Extensive ex-
periments on PIE-Bench [14] demonstrate that PreciseEdit achieves
a favorable fidelity–editability Pareto balance while retaining low
latency and memory usage. Its consistent gains across multiple
one-step backbones further support the generality of the proposed
formulation. We hope that the perspective of separating seman-
tic strength from reliability will provide a useful foundation for
efficient, precise, and controllable image editing.

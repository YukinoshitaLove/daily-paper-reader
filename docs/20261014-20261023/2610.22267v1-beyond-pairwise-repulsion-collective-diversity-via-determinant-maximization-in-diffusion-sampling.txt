Abstract. Strong guidance in text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models con-
centrates sampling toward high-density modes of the conditional distri-
bution, collapsing independently sampled noise vectors onto nearly iden-
tical visual prototypes. This limits utility for tasks that demand distri-
butional breadth, most critically synthetic data augmentation. Diversity,
however, is a collective property of the whole batch: a set can preserve ev-
ery pairwise distance yet still collapse onto a low-dimensional subspace.
Existing sum-of-pairwise noise-optimization and repulsion objectives re-
spond only weakly to this higher-order failure. We propose DetNO
(Determinant Noise Optimization), which formulates diverse generation

This set-level functional equals the squared batch volume; its repulsion is

Introduction
Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) [12] has become the standard mechanism for
steering text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models [11, 24, 27] toward high-fidelity,
prompt-aligned images. Strong CFG, however, concentrates sampling toward
the high-density peak of the conditional distribution, collapsing its effective sup-
port onto a narrow region of the image manifold (Fig. 1). Under deterministic
samplers such as DDIM [26], the initial noise fully determines the final image, yet
independent noise draws collapse onto the same narrow visual mode. Multiple
images generated from the same prompt converge to nearly identical visual pro-
totypes. The collapse limits utility in applications that depend on distributional
breadth, most critically synthetic data augmentation, where near-duplicate out-
puts provide diminishing returns for downstream model training [9].
Recovering this diversity requires treating a batch as a whole, since diversity
is a collective property of the set rather than a sum over its pairs. A batch can
preserve every pairwise distance yet still collapse onto a low-dimensional sub-
space, a higher-order failure mode to which independent pair contributions re-
spond only weakly. Existing approaches meet this criterion only partially. Meth-
ods that intervene during denoising repel intermediate latents [3, 14] or manip-
ulate text embeddings [31], paying a per-step cost that scales with trajectory
length. Contrastive Noise Optimization (CNO) [13] instead optimizes the initial
noise with an InfoNCE loss in the Tweedie prediction space, intervening before
denoising begins, where the noise largely determines semantic structure [2, 8].

This motivates a single scalar measuring the collective volume a batch occu-
pies, penalizing redundancy at every order up to B simultaneously. We realize
this scalar as the log-determinant of an RBF Gram matrix over one-step Tweedie
predictions, making all-order diversity differentiable. We further show that this
objective is self-regulating: repulsion amplifies when the batch is most redundant
and relaxes as diversity is achieved, an adaptation that an unweighted sum-of-
pairwise objective lacks in the same analysis.
We propose DetNO (Determinant Noise Optimization), illustrated in Fig. 2,
which pairs this log-determinant diversity term with an anchor-based fidelity
term via Lagrangian relaxation. A single hyperparameter λ controls the diversity-
quality trade-off, with no training or sampler modification. Empirically, the col-
lective objective delivers on both diversity and downstream utility. Across three
backbones (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3), DetNO achieves leading intra-batch diversity
in VendiScore and Mean Self-Similarity, with the advantage over CNO widen-
ing as the batch size grows. A controlled toy experiment shows faithful recovery
of all modes where pairwise methods distort mode structure, and downstream
few-shot classification produces accuracy gains over both standard sampling and
CNO, reaching statistical significance in the low-shot regime (p<0.01).
Our main contributions are as follows:
– We recast diverse T2I generation as a set-level rather than pairwise prob-
lem: constrained RKHS volume maximization on initial noise. The objective
pairs a log-determinant diversity term with an anchor-based fidelity term,
controlled by a single hyperparameter λ, with no sampler modification.
– We show that the log-determinant amplifies repulsion when the batch is
most redundant and relaxes as diversity is achieved, a self-regulating prop-

Related Work
2.1
Recovering Mode Diversity in Diffusion Models.
Prior methods recover the limited diversity of conditional diffusion models through
repulsion, differing mainly in where they apply it but sharing a diversity signal
that decomposes into a sum of pairwise interactions. One line intervenes during
denoising: SPELL [14] adds sparse repellency terms to the reverse SDE, Particle
Guidance (PG) [3] repels intermediate latents via a joint-particle potential, and
DiversityPrompt [31] optimizes a token embedding online to steer generation
toward low-density regions. All incur per-step overhead that scales with trajec-
tory length. Contrastive Noise Optimization (CNO) [13] instead shifts repulsion
to the initial noise, optimizing latents via InfoNCE in the Tweedie prediction
space before sampling begins. Both, however, quantify diversity as a sum over
sample pairs, so their repulsion never leaves the span of pairwise directions and
responds only weakly to higher-order redundancy that collapses the batch onto
a low-dimensional subspace. A separate line targets rare modes of the uncondi-
tional or class-conditional distribution via classifier guidance [25, 29], self-guided
sampling [30], or modified noise schedules [28]. Our goal instead is to recover sup-
pressed modes of a text-conditional distribution under strong guidance for a fixed
prompt.
2.2
Initial Noise Optimization in Diffusion Models.
InitNO [8] steers noise toward valid regions via attention-based scores for bet-
ter text adherence, while [2] learn a mapping to optimal noise configurations
to characterize CFG behavior. Neither addresses diversity. CNO [13] establishes
that optimizing the initial noise alone can recover suppressed modes, using a
pairwise contrastive objective. We inherit this noise-space pipeline from CNO;
what changes is the structure of the diversity signal, from a sum over pairs to
log-determinant maximization, a set-level functional equal to the squared RKHS
volume that penalizes redundancy at every order. This objective is jointly opti-
mized with an anchor-based fidelity term under a single hyperparameter λ. The
log-determinant as a diversity criterion originates in GDPP [4], which regularizes
a GAN’s generator with a DPP kernel. We instead apply it as an inference-time
objective on the Tweedie predictions of a frozen diffusion model, requiring no
training and no DPP sampling.

Classifier-Free Guidance
Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) [12] enhances text-image alignment by interpo-

5
Experiments
Implementation Details. We evaluate on three pre-trained T2I backbones: SD1.5 [24],
SDXL [22], and SD3 [5]. SD1.5 and SDXL use DDIM [26] with 50 steps, while
SD3 uses the Euler sampler with 28 steps following its default configuration. We
compare against PG [3], SPELL [14], DiversityPrompt [31], and CNO [13]. We
evaluate all methods under identical conditions (prompt set, random seeds, CFG
scale, and hardware) to ensure fair relative comparison; absolute values may dif-
fer from original papers accordingly. We use the official implementations of all
baselines, including CNO, with the hyperparameters reported in their original
papers. Following [13, 31], we sample 2,000 prompts from the MS-COCO [18]
validation set and generate 5 images per prompt for SD1.5 and SD3, and 3
images per prompt for SDXL.
Evaluation Metrics. For image quality, we report Precision [17], Density [19],
CLIPScore [10], PickScore [15], and ImageReward [33]. For diversity, we report
Recall [17], Coverage [19], Vendi Score [6], and Mean Self-Similarity (MSS) [13].
Among these, VendiScore and MSS serve as our primary diversity metrics, as
they measure intra-batch coverage directly without reference to an external dis-
tribution. They are therefore better suited to evaluating diversity-enhancing
methods than reference-dependent metrics such as Recall and Coverage [20].

6
Conclusion
We presented DetNO, an initial-noise optimization framework that maximizes
the RKHS volume among Tweedie predictions via a log-determinant diversity
term and an anchor-based fidelity term. Experiments on SD1.5, SDXL, and SD3
confirm consistent diversity gains across backbones and samplers, with Pareto
analysis showing that λ enables smooth interpolation between the two objectives.
A toy experiment and a spectral analysis (Proposition 1) trace this advantage
to its mechanism: repulsion that intensifies where the batch is most collapsed
and relaxes once diversity is restored. In downstream few-shot classification, the
broader mode coverage translates into statistically significant accuracy gains in
the low-data regime. Treating diversity as a property of the whole batch, rather
than of its pairs, thus produces image sets that are not only more varied but
measurably more useful.

A.6
Effect of Kernel Bandwidth σ
The RBF kernel bandwidth σ controls the sensitivity of the Gram matrix to
inter-sample distances in the pooled Tweedie prediction space. We compare five
configurations: two fixed small values (σ ∈{0.01, 0.5}), a fixed moderate value
(σ = 1.0), a dynamic median updated at every optimization step, and our default
choice of a fixed median heuristic computed once from the reference predictions
(Eq. 5).
As shown in Tab. S4, small fixed values (σ = 0.01, 0.5) yield near-uniform
Gram matrices where all off-diagonal entries approach 1, providing weak diver-
sity signal and resulting in the lowest VendiScore and the highest MSS. σ = 1.0
and the dynamic median improve diversity metrics but at a moderate quality

A.7
Computational Overhead
Tab. S5 compares the computational cost across methods. Following CNO, we
apply a stop-gradient operator on the UNet output when computing Tweedie
predictions, which eliminates the need to retain UNet activations during back-
propagation. As a result, DetNO achieves the same GPU memory footprint as
CNO (28.3 GB) and requires the same number of extra forward passes (Nopt+1).
The marginal time overhead relative to CNO (17.8 s vs. 17.7 s) stems solely from
the Gram matrix computation and its matrix inversion, both of which are neg-
ligible compared to the UNet forward passes.
A.8
Effect of Gradient Computation Strategy.
As shown in Table S6, stop-gradient already yields clear diversity gains over the
baseline (MSS, VendiScore) at a marginal quality cost. Full gradient achieves
moderately stronger gains (particularly in Recall and VendiScore) by propagat-
ing richer second-order information through the denoising network, but at the
cost of storing all intermediate UNet activations during backpropagation. Stop-
gradient closes most of this gap while reducing peak memory to the level of
standard inference (Tab. S5), making it the more practical default; we adopt it
in Algorithm 1 and note that full gradient can be used when memory budget
permits.
